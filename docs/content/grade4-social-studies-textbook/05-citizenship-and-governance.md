# Chapter 5 — Citizenship and governance in Kenya

## 5.1 Being a good citizen at school

One morning, Mike finds a pencil case beside the classroom door. Nobody sees him pick it up. He takes it to his teacher so that its owner can collect it. Mike has made an honest choice.

### Qualities of a good citizen

Citizenship involves belonging to a community and taking part responsibly in its life. Good citizenship at school can be seen through actions: telling the truth, respecting others, sharing fairly, following reasonable safety rules, caring for property and helping people who need assistance.

A learner who finds a lost item returns it. A learner who borrows a book returns it in good condition. A learner who disagrees with a classmate listens before responding. These actions show honesty, responsibility and respect.

Good citizens do not have to agree with everyone. They can express different opinions politely and report unsafe or unfair treatment to a trusted adult. They include learners who have different abilities, backgrounds or beliefs.

Citizenship also matters when using digital devices. A responsible digital citizen does not share someone's picture or private information without permission, does not spread unkind messages and asks for help when something online feels unsafe.

### Put citizenship into practice

Choose one duty you can carry out safely for a week, such as organising shared books, including others in games or using materials carefully. Record how the action helped the community. At the end of the week, reflect on what you could improve.

### Check your understanding

1. Name four qualities of a good citizen at school.
2. Why is returning a lost pencil case an example of honesty?
3. Describe a responsible way to disagree with a classmate.
4. A group refuses to include a learner because of a disability. Suggest a fair action.
5. Give one example of good digital citizenship.

## 5.2 Living peacefully with others

Two learners want to use the same ball during break. They begin arguing about who should play first. A third learner suggests that each group take turns for an agreed period. The learners decide on a fair arrangement and continue playing.

### What promotes peace?

Peace is more than the absence of fighting. People live peacefully when they feel safe, respect one another and resolve disagreements without harming others. Fair rules, honest communication, patience, cooperation and respect for differences can help promote peace.

Conflict can arise when people disagree, feel left out or misunderstand each other. Feeling angry is not wrong, but hurting another person is not an acceptable response. A learner can move away from an argument, calm down, explain the problem respectfully and seek help from a trusted adult.

For an ordinary disagreement between learners who feel safe, both may listen to each other, identify the problem, suggest fair solutions and agree on a way forward. Nobody should be pressured to negotiate with someone who is threatening or repeatedly bullying them. Safety comes first.

### Building peace at school

A peace corner can provide a quiet supervised place for reflection. A peace poster can remind learners to include others and seek help. A school may organise cooperative games, discussion circles or an approved nature trail where learners practise sharing and working together.

A peacebuilding activity should have a clear purpose. For example, a class could agree to reduce name-calling by practising respectful words, reporting bullying and checking at the end of each week whether everyone feels included.

### Plan a peace activity

With your teacher, identify a common, non-private classroom problem. Write two peaceful ways to respond. Design a poster or organise a cooperative activity. Do not use real classmates' names in a poster about disagreements. If you are working alone, write a dialogue showing a peaceful solution.

### Check your understanding

1. Name three factors that promote peace at school.
2. How can a learner respond when feeling angry during a disagreement?
3. Explain why taking turns can help resolve a conflict over a shared item.
4. What should you do if another learner threatens your safety?
5. Describe a peacebuilding activity and how you would know whether it helped.

## 5.3 Every child has rights

Children need care, protection and opportunities to grow and learn. Every child deserves respect and safety, regardless of their family, gender, disability or background.

### Rights and responsibilities

A right is something a person is entitled to. Children have rights to education, protection from harm, care, dignity and other basic needs. Responsibilities are actions we take to care for ourselves and respect others, such as keeping learning materials safe and treating people kindly. A child's rights do not disappear because the child makes a mistake or fails to carry out a responsibility.

Child abuse means harming a child or failing to protect a child from serious harm. It includes physical, emotional or sexual abuse, exploitation and neglect. Sexual abuse includes inappropriate sexual touching or conduct involving a child. A child is never responsible for being abused.

Some harmful practices include early or forced marriage, female genital mutilation (FGM), slavery, child trafficking and forced labour. FGM involves harming a girl's genital organs for non-medical reasons. These practices violate children's safety and dignity. Children with disabilities may also face mistreatment or exclusion. Everyone has an equal right to protection.

Unsafe or unauthorised school transport can put children at risk. Use transport arranged and approved by responsible adults, wear a seat belt where provided and tell a trusted adult if transport feels unsafe.

### Why abuse happens and how it affects children

Abuse can occur when people misuse power, ignore children's rights, exploit children or continue harmful practices. Poverty and lack of knowledge may increase risks, but they never excuse harming a child.

Abuse can cause injury, fear, sadness, interrupted schooling and difficulty trusting others. Different children respond in different ways. Support and protection from caring adults and trained professionals can help.

### Seeking protection and promoting rights

If someone hurts you, makes you feel unsafe or asks you to keep an unsafe secret, tell a trusted adult, such as a teacher, caregiver or another safe adult. If the first adult does not help, tell another. You do not have to face the situation alone or investigate it yourself. If you are in immediate danger, move to a safer place if you can and seek adult or emergency assistance.

A class can promote children's rights through a poster about kindness, inclusion, safe transport and seeking help. No learner should be asked to tell a personal story of abuse in front of the class. Adults are responsible for protection and for responding safely to a disclosure.

### Check your understanding

2. Explain why a child's rights do not depend on whether they finish their duties.
3. Name three forms of child abuse or exploitation and explain why they are harmful.
4. Explain one possible effect of child abuse on a learner's education.
5. What could a child do if a trusted adult does not respond to a safety concern?

## 5.4 Making decisions together

A class wants to choose a game for a special activity. Learners suggest several suitable games. They discuss the choices, listen to different opinions and vote. The teacher checks that every proposed game is safe and accessible. The class accepts the agreed decision while respecting those who preferred a different game.

### Democracy at school

Democracy is a way of making decisions in which people have opportunities to participate and express their views. Democratic practices include fair elections, listening to different opinions, consultation and shared decision-making.

A democratic process should have clear and fair rules. Candidates should have an equal chance to explain their ideas. Eligible voters should be able to choose freely without threats or unfair pressure. Votes should be counted honestly.

For example, a class electing a representative can agree on the role, invite willing candidates, hear their ideas, conduct a fair vote, count votes and announce the result. A secret ballot can help learners make a private choice. The winner should serve the whole class, including learners who voted for someone else.

Democracy helps people participate in decisions, practise responsibility and choose leaders fairly. A majority decision does not permit people to mistreat a minority or ignore safety and rights.

### Hold a mock election

With your teacher, choose a fictional class role or harmless classroom decision. Establish fair rules, invite volunteers to explain their ideas, vote and count the results. Discuss how the process could include everyone. If learning independently, design a sample ballot and describe how the votes would be counted.

### Check your understanding

1. What does democracy mean?
2. Name three democratic practices at school.
3. Why might a secret ballot be useful?
4. Explain why the elected representative should listen to people who voted for another candidate.
5. Write four steps for conducting a fair class election.

## 5.5 Children's government in school

A learner has an idea for improving the school library. She shares it with her class representative, who presents the idea during a meeting of learner representatives. The teacher helps the representatives discuss it and agree on a proposal to the school administration.

### The composition of children's government

A children's government is a school arrangement through which learners participate in leadership and communicate their views. Schools may organise it differently. A children's government may include a chairperson or president, a deputy, a secretary, class representatives and representatives responsible for specific areas such as learning, environment or welfare.

These are examples of roles, not a compulsory structure for every school. Ask your school about its actual arrangement.

```text
          Learner representatives
                    |
     Chairperson / meeting coordinator
         /            |             \
 Class representatives |       Welfare representatives
                  Secretary
```

The diagram shows possible roles in a learner council. Adult school leaders remain responsible for school safety, administration and decisions requiring adult authority.

### What children's government does

Learner representatives may collect suggestions, present concerns, help organise approved activities and encourage learners to care for shared resources. They should listen to all learners, communicate accurately and treat people fairly. Representatives do not have permission to punish, threaten or investigate other children.

A secretary can write a record of agreed decisions. A chairperson can invite different representatives to speak. Class representatives can report the discussion back to their classmates. Learners who hold no leadership position can still participate by sharing ideas and taking part in approved activities.

### Respectful participation

Respect children's government by listening to its representatives, sharing constructive suggestions and carrying out agreed safe activities. Leaders also owe respect to those they represent. If a leader behaves unfairly, report the concern through the school's trusted adult channels.

### Simulate a meeting

In a supervised group, choose an imaginary issue, such as improving a reading corner. Assign roles, hear suggestions, agree on one action and record the decision. At home, write a short example of meeting minutes.

2. Explain two functions of learner representatives.
3. What is the work of a meeting secretary?
4. Give one way a learner without an elected role can participate.
5. Why should a learner representative not threaten or punish classmates?

## 5.6 Leaders in our community

A village plans a community clean-up. Different people contribute ideas. A community leader helps organise the discussion, while trained workers and responsible adults arrange safe waste collection. Good leadership helps people work towards a common purpose.

### Different community leaders

Communities have many kinds of leaders. Religious leaders may guide people in matters of faith, encourage support for others and help bring people together. Clan leaders may help members of their communities preserve traditions and discuss matters affecting families. Village leaders may help communicate community concerns, coordinate local activities and connect residents with relevant services.

Roles differ between communities and institutions. Some leaders hold formal government positions; others are recognised through community or religious arrangements. A title alone does not make someone a good leader.

### What good leaders do

Good leaders listen to others, explain decisions, act honestly, encourage cooperation and take responsibility for their actions. They consider different people's needs and do not favour someone unfairly. They seek advice from people with relevant knowledge rather than pretending to know everything.

Imagine a community choosing where to hold a meeting. A good leader asks whether the location is safe, whether people with disabilities can reach it and whether the time is suitable for participants. The leader explains the decision and invites feedback.

Leadership is not limited to adults with titles. A learner can practise good leadership by organising a group task fairly, listening to a quiet group member and taking responsibility for an agreed duty.

### Interview or role-play

Ask a willing community leader, through a trusted adult, about one of their duties and one quality that helps them perform it. Alternatively, use a fictional role-play in which a group must plan a safe community activity. Show how the leader listens, includes others and explains a decision.

### Check your understanding

1. Identify three kinds of community leaders.
2. Explain a duty that a village leader may perform.
3. Name four qualities of a good leader.
4. How can a group leader include a learner who rarely speaks?
5. Give an example of a decision that shows honesty and fairness.

## 5.7 How county governments work

Kenya has a national government and 47 county governments. County governments bring certain public services and decisions closer to the people in different parts of the country.

### The structure of county government

A county government has a county executive and a county assembly. The county executive carries out county functions and implements county laws and plans. The county governor heads the county executive, assisted by a deputy governor and other members of the county executive committee. County officers help deliver services.

The county assembly makes laws for the county, approves relevant county plans and budgets and oversees the work of the county executive. It includes elected ward representatives, called Members of County Assembly (MCAs), and other members provided for by law.

```text
                 County government
                 /               \
       County executive        County assembly
       Governor and deputy     MCAs and other members
       Executive committee     County laws and oversight
       County administration
```

The two parts have different duties and work within Kenya's Constitution and laws.

### Duties of the governor

The governor provides leadership to the county executive, helps guide county development plans, oversees implementation of county policies and services and is accountable for the county executive's work. The deputy governor assists the governor. County departments and staff carry out many daily tasks.

Examples of county functions include county health services, local markets, refuse removal and certain county roads. Not every public service is the responsibility of the county government. National and county functions are defined by law.

### Roles of an MCA

An elected MCA represents people in a ward in the county assembly. MCAs take part in making county laws, considering county plans and budgets and overseeing the county executive. They can raise issues affecting residents in assembly discussions. An MCA is not the same as a governor and does not personally carry out all county services.

Ward and village administrators help coordinate county administration and community participation in their areas, in accordance with their legal duties. Residents can learn about public participation opportunities and present concerns through appropriate county channels.

Discuss how residents can give feedback about that service. Do not assume a named official still holds office without checking a current official source.
