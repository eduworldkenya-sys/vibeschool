# Chapter 3 — Social organisations

## 3.1 The culture of our county

At a cultural day in school, Luca watches a group perform a traditional dance. Other learners display baskets, clothing, musical instruments and drawings of houses. Each group explains something about the people who developed or use those items. No single display tells the whole story of a community.

### Ways people express culture

Culture includes the ways people live, the knowledge they share, their beliefs, arts and customs. Traditional culture includes practices and knowledge passed from older generations to younger ones. Culture can also change over time.

People express culture in many ways. Traditional clothing may be worn for ceremonies or everyday activities. Food can reflect crops, animals, cooking methods and local knowledge. Housing may use materials available in an area. A basket, pot, stool or tool made by people is an artefact. Traditional sports and games allow people to enjoy themselves and learn skills. Festivals and ceremonies may mark important occasions or bring communities together.

For example, a woven basket can show knowledge of suitable fibres, patterns and weaving techniques. Different communities may make different kinds of baskets for carrying, storage or decoration. We can appreciate the skill without assuming that everyone in a community uses the same design.

### Why culture is important

Culture helps people share knowledge, express their identity and learn about their history. Traditional games, songs and stories can bring generations together. Crafts can support livelihoods. Cultural exchanges allow people to learn from one another.

Kenya has many communities and many cultural traditions. Families may practise a mixture of traditions, adopt new ones or choose not to practise particular customs. Treat every learner's background with respect. A cultural practice is not automatically acceptable just because it is old. Practices that harm people or violate their rights should not be continued.

### Our culture gallery

Choose one of these six categories: dress, food, housing, artefacts, sports and games, or festivals and ceremonies. Make a drawing or model, or interview a willing adult about an example. Label your display and explain what it is used for. Respect family privacy and ask permission before recording or photographing anyone.

### Check your understanding

1. Name six ways people express traditional culture.
2. What is an artefact? Give an example.
3. Explain two reasons traditional culture is important.
4. Draw one example of traditional housing or clothing and label two features.
5. Explain how you would respond respectfully if a classmate practises a different tradition.

## 3.2 Our school

Joy notices an old photograph in the school office. The classrooms in the photograph look different from those she sees today. Her teacher explains that the school has changed over the years. Like communities, schools have histories, values and rules.

### The history of a school

The history of a school is the story of how it began and changed. It may include when the school opened, its first classrooms, important people, changes in enrolment or achievements. To tell a school's history accurately, collect information from reliable sources such as school records, old photographs or a conversation with a member of the school administration.

A timeline places events in the order in which they happened. Here is an imaginary example, not the history of a real school.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 2010 | School opened with two classrooms |
| 2015 | A library was built |
| 2022 | A new classroom block opened |

If two sources disagree about a date, ask for clarification instead of selecting a date at random.

### School values, motto and rules

Values are principles that guide the way people behave. A school may value honesty, responsibility, respect and hard work. Learners demonstrate honesty when they return something they find. They show responsibility by caring for shared materials and completing agreed duties.

A motto is a short statement that expresses an aim or guiding idea. An imaginary school's motto might be “Learn, Serve, Grow.” This is only an example; use the actual wording of your school's motto.

Rules explain what learners and other members of the school community are expected to do. Rules help maintain safety, fairness, order and a good learning environment. Being disciplined means making responsible choices, even when nobody is watching. A learner should seek help from a trusted adult when a rule seems unsafe or unfair.

### Make a school story poster

Collect three confirmed events from your school's history. Arrange them in a timeline. Write two values and the school's actual motto. Add one example of how to follow a school rule. If learning independently, use a school you previously attended or a fictional example clearly labelled as such.

### Check your understanding

1. Name two sources you could use to learn your school's history.
2. Arrange these imaginary events in time order: new library in 2020; school opened in 2005; new classrooms in 2012.
3. Explain the difference between a school motto and a school rule.
4. Recite your school's motto and explain its meaning, or explain the meaning of the example motto above.
5. Give one way you can demonstrate responsibility at school.
