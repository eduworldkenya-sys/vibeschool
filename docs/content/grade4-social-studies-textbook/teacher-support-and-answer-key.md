# Grade 4 Social Studies — Teacher support and answer key

**Internal teacher editorial draft.** Keep this material out of the learner textbook's public chapter bodies. The questions below correspond to the five numbered checks in each section. Accept other accurate, appropriate examples and oral responses. Do not award marks for disclosing private experiences. Verify local facts with a current map, school record or authorised local source before teaching. No student account or real person's information is required.

## 1.1 Finding our way
**Preparation and teaching:** Draw the school cross-map on the board. Ask pupils to point in opposite directions, then label N, E, S and W. Turn a paper map and use its north arrow to find the same direction. Model gate → classroom → library, then ask pairs to exchange starting points. Support: provide a tactile or high-contrast compass rose and spoken descriptions. Extend: give a two-leg route with a different starting point. Check that a learner names the direction relative to the correct starting place, not the direction they personally face.
**Answers:** 1. North N, east E, south S, west W. 2. North. 3. West. 4. North to the classroom, then west to the library. 5. E.g. clear directions help a visitor reach a destination.
**Revision:** From the garden to the water tank, give two legs. Answer: south to the classroom, then east.

## 1.2 Knowing our county
**Preparation and teaching:** Obtain a current same-scale county map and verify actual sub-county names. Trace shared boundaries; model comparing outlines with equal grid squares. Support: trace one boundary using a raised line and compare two simple shapes. Extend: explain why a map of a larger scale can make a smaller county look large. Avoid assuming every nearby county is a neighbour.
**Answers:** 1. A line separating the county from another area. 2. Any two of Kiambu, Kajiado and Machakos. 3. Area is the surface occupied; population is the number of people living there. 4. Same scale makes the areas comparable. 5. Check actual county, two sub-counties and a neighbouring county against the map.
**Revision:** Two equally scaled outlines cover 12 and 20 equal squares. Which has greater area? Answer: the outline covering 20 squares.

## 1.3 Land and water features
**Preparation and teaching:** Show actual locally appropriate photographs or a simple clay model. Ask learners to distinguish natural features from bridges and buildings. Model land profiles, then connect features to community uses and conservation. Support: use tactile models and labels; extend: discuss why a flood-prone valley may require precautions. Do not require outdoor trips or dangerous climbing.
**Answers:** 1. Valley. 2. A plateau is raised relatively flat land; a plain is broadly fairly flat land, not necessarily elevated. 3. E.g. water for farming and habitat, or properly managed water supply. 4. Check the raised hill, low valley and river channel are recognisable and correctly labelled. 5. E.g. avoid littering or protect vegetation.
**Revision:** Is a dam wall a natural physical feature? Answer: no, it is human-made.

## 1.4 Weather and seasons
**Preparation and teaching:** Display four seasonal-condition cards and model safe daily observations. Discuss overlap between heat, dryness and rain. Demonstrate a weather chart; ask learners to interpret a pattern rather than memorise fixed months. Support: use icons with text; extend: compare observations across several weeks. Reinforce that learners must not enter floodwater.
**Answers:** 1. Hot, cold, dry and rainy. 2. E.g. water becomes scarce, so farmers may conserve water or adjust planting. 3. Slippery roads, reduced visibility or flooding. 4. Avoid the flooded route, seek a safe alternative with a trusted adult. 5. Check three days, coherent observations and a reasonable pattern.
**Revision:** A sunny day follows two rainy days. Does that prove the rainy season has ended? No.

## 1.5 Keeping our history alive
**Preparation and teaching:** Use authorised photographs or a supervised interview. Model distinguishing museum, monument and cultural centre. Ask why preservation matters, then plan an age-appropriate conservation activity. Support: provide a captioned example; extend: compare two local heritage sites and their purposes. Never allow unsupervised visits, collecting artefacts, or handling hazardous litter.
**Answers:** 1. To collect, care for and display historically, culturally or scientifically important objects. 2. A monument commemorates; a cultural centre shares and preserves culture. 3. Any two: learning history, protecting evidence, cultural identity, tourism/work. 4. Writing can damage historic material and spoil evidence. 5. E.g. supervised safe litter collection or an awareness poster.
**Revision:** A visitor sees a loose object at a historic site. What should they do? Leave it and tell the responsible adult or site guide.

## 2.1 We need one another
**Preparation and teaching:** Use a familiar local product and map producer, transport, seller and consumer. Ask what would happen if one link stopped working. Include unpaid care and support, not just financial transactions. Support: provide role cards; extend: draw two-way arrows showing mutual benefit. Avoid requiring children to perform dangerous or commercial work.
**Answers:** 1. Relying on one another to meet needs. 2. Farmer provides produce; trader sells it to customers and buys it or helps it reach them. 3. Any two: nurse, doctor, cleaner, pharmacist, receptionist or other relevant worker. 4. Accept a logical sequence with three roles. 5. E.g. offer to fetch a trusted adult or help with a light item; support makes tasks easier and strengthens cooperation.
**Revision:** How might a transport worker depend on farmers? Farmers provide goods to transport and help create demand for transport services.

## 2.2 Where people live
**Preparation and teaching:** Use equal-area dot diagrams and a clear legend. Have learners compare density without assuming dots represent exact house locations. Support: place counters on large cards; extend: discuss different service needs in a clustered and dispersed settlement. Use no identifiable home locations.
**Answers:** 1. Population is the number of people; distribution is how they are spread. 2. Many people living in a relatively small area. 3. Any two: jobs, transport, water, schools, health services. 4. Check dots are spread apart. 5. Distribution helps plan a location accessible to people needing the service.
**Revision:** Two equal areas contain the same number of dots, but one has a cluster. Do their totals differ? No; distribution differs.

## 3.1 The culture of our county
**Preparation and teaching:** Invite voluntary, respectful examples from learners; use objects or images with consent. Compare six categories, and distinguish culture from stereotypes. Support: match pictures to categories; extend: explain how a tradition can change over time. Do not ask learners to represent a whole ethnic or religious group, or celebrate harmful practices.
**Answers:** 1. Dressing, food, housing, artefacts, sports/games, festivals/ceremonies. 2. A human-made object, e.g. a woven basket. 3. Any two: identity, knowledge, history, social connection, livelihoods. 4. Accept a recognisable example with accurate labels. 5. Listen respectfully, avoid ridicule, ask appropriate questions if invited.
**Revision:** Can two families from the same community practise different traditions? Yes; people and cultures are diverse.

## 3.2 Our school
**Preparation and teaching:** Verify the actual school history, values, motto and rules with school administration. Model the imaginary timeline before learners create a real one. Support: order three picture cards; extend: distinguish a verified fact from an unconfirmed memory. Learners without a current school may use a clearly fictional timeline.
**Answers:** 1. Any two: official records, old photographs, administration, reliable interviews. 2. 2005, 2012, 2020. 3. A motto expresses an aim; a rule states expected behaviour. 4. Check actual motto/meaning or reasonable explanation of the example. 5. E.g. care for books, complete a duty, return borrowed items.
**Revision:** Why should a learner verify a date before adding it to a timeline? To avoid recording inaccurate history.

## 4.1 How people earn a living
**Preparation and teaching:** Select locally relevant resources and activities; model resource → activity → benefit → conservation. Support: use picture matching; extend: compare benefits and environmental costs. Children participate through safe learning simulations or supervised age-appropriate tasks, not hazardous labour.
**Answers:** 1. Something useful for meeting a need; e.g. land and water. 2. Any three accurate activity/product pairs: farming/beans, fishing/fish, transport/moving goods. 3. Traders help farmers reach buyers and families obtain goods. 4. To preserve safe water, habitats and future use. 5. E.g. supervised school gardening or market role-play.
**Revision:** Name one way a workshop can conserve resources. Reuse safe offcuts or reduce waste.

## 4.2 Industries in our county
**Preparation and teaching:** Use clean samples or images of raw and finished materials. Demonstrate a flow chart; discuss industry location with a verified local map. Support: sequence three cards; extend: consider pollution prevention and transport. No unsupervised factory visits, machinery or chemicals.
**Answers:** 1. Material used as an input to make a product. 2. Dairy processing; e.g. yoghurt. 3. Roads allow materials to arrive and products/workers to reach destinations. 4. Jobs, goods, income or adding value. 5. Grain → mill → flour → transport/shop → customer.
**Revision:** Why might a dairy processor be near dairy farms? Milk can be collected and transported efficiently; reliable transport and appropriate storage are important.

## 4.3 Starting an enterprise project at school
**Preparation and teaching:** Choose a supervised low-cost project, or use paper simulation. Model costs and money received. Explain roles, safety and honest records. Support: provide a pictorial project checklist; extend: compare two budgets and ways to reduce waste. No compulsory contributions, unsupervised sales, sharp tools or hazardous road demonstrations.
**Answers:** 1. Any two safe supervised projects, e.g. bookmarks and seedlings. 2. To estimate costs, resources, roles and avoid waste. 3. KSh 30 (120 − 90). 4. Accurate records, fair sharing, honest sales or reporting mistakes. 5. Check purpose, resources, roles and fair participation with adult supervision.
**Revision:** A project receives KSh 70 and costs KSh 90. What is the result? A loss of KSh 20.

## 5.1 Being a good citizen at school
**Preparation and teaching:** Model an ordinary fictional decision (returning property, inclusion or listening). Have learners describe observable actions, not label classmates as good or bad. Support: choose between illustrated scenarios; extend: explain citizenship online. Assess behaviour through a short reflective journal or teacher observation.
**Answers:** 1. Any four: honesty, responsibility, respect, fairness, kindness, cooperation. 2. The learner returns an item that is not theirs rather than keeping it. 3. Listen and express a view politely or seek help. 4. Include the learner and adapt the task fairly if needed. 5. E.g. avoid sharing private pictures without consent.
**Revision:** A friend asks you to copy someone else's work. What is a responsible response? Decline and offer to help them learn honestly.

## 5.2 Living peacefully with others
**Preparation and teaching:** Use a fictional disagreement, model turn-taking and calm communication, then plan a cooperative activity. Support: offer sentence starters; extend: suggest a simple measure of improvement. Distinguish ordinary conflict from abuse or bullying; never require mediation with an unsafe person.
**Answers:** 1. Any three: fair rules, respect, patience, communication, cooperation. 2. Move away if necessary, calm down, speak respectfully or seek help. 3. It distributes use fairly and reduces disagreement. 4. Move to safety if possible and tell a trusted adult. 5. Check a safe activity and observable measure, e.g. fewer reported exclusion incidents or more equitable participation.
**Revision:** Must a learner negotiate with someone who threatens them? No. Safety and adult support come first.

## 5.3 Every child has rights
**Preparation and teaching:** Use non-graphic, fictional situations and age-appropriate definitions. Explain rights, responsibilities, unsafe practices, protection and reporting without asking for personal disclosures. Establish a safe private reporting pathway before the lesson. Use inclusive examples, do not blame children, and avoid asking them to investigate harm. If a child discloses abuse, listen calmly, do not promise secrecy, follow applicable school safeguarding and child protection procedures promptly. Support: provide simple safety phrases and trusted-adult options; extend: design a rights-awareness poster without private details.
**Answers:** 1. Any three: education, care, dignity, protection, basic needs. 2. Rights belong to every child and are not conditional on completing duties. 3. Any three accurate examples, e.g. forced marriage, forced labour, neglect; they harm safety, dignity, education or health. 4. E.g. interrupted schooling, fear or difficulty concentrating. 5. Tell another trusted adult; seek safety and appropriate help. Never grade a personal disclosure.
**Revision:** Is abuse ever the child's fault? No.

## 5.4 Making decisions together
**Preparation and teaching:** Run a mock election with fictional roles, willing participants and fair rules. Demonstrate a private ballot and honest counting. Support: use symbol ballots or a spoken accessible alternative; extend: compare voting and discussion. Protect minority rights; never turn participation into a popularity ranking.
**Answers:** 1. A way of making decisions in which people can participate and express views. 2. Fair elections, consultation, shared decisions or listening to different views. 3. It protects the privacy of a choice and reduces pressure. 4. The representative serves everyone. 5. Any logical four steps: agree rules, invite candidates, hear ideas, vote, count honestly, announce.
**Revision:** Is it fair to change the rules after votes are cast? No.

## 5.5 Children's government in school
**Preparation and teaching:** Verify the school's actual learner council structure; use example roles only where appropriate. Simulate a meeting and record one decision. Support: give role cards; extend: write minutes and a report to classmates. Learner leaders have no independent disciplinary or investigative powers.
**Answers:** 1. Any four possible roles: chairperson/president, deputy, secretary, class or welfare representative. 2. Collect suggestions, communicate concerns, organise approved activities, report decisions. 3. Record agreed decisions accurately. 4. Share a suggestion or join an approved activity. 5. Adult leaders are responsible for safety and discipline; learner representatives must treat peers respectfully.
**Revision:** What should a representative do when classmates disagree? Listen, record views fairly and seek appropriate guidance.

## 5.6 Leaders in our community
**Preparation and teaching:** Discuss actual types of leaders without assuming all communities have identical structures. Use a fictional meeting or a supervised interview. Support: match leaders and possible roles; extend: compare two decisions using fairness and accessibility criteria. Do not ask children to contact unknown adults independently.
**Answers:** 1. Religious, clan and village leaders. 2. E.g. communicate residents' concerns or coordinate local activities. 3. Any four: honesty, listening, fairness, accountability, responsibility, cooperation. 4. Invite their opinion without pressure and provide another way to contribute. 5. E.g. share roles using fair criteria and explain decisions openly.
**Revision:** Does a title alone prove a person is a good leader? No; actions and qualities matter.

## 5.7 How county governments work
**Preparation and teaching:** Use a two-branch county government chart; distinguish representation, oversight and executive delivery. Model a waste collection example. Support: sort role cards into executive and assembly; extend: explain how feedback or public participation may help. Check current office holders and specific local arrangements against an official source. Avoid partisan examples and promises that an MCA personally delivers every service.
**Answers:** 1. 47. 2. County executive and county assembly. 3. Any two: leads county executive, guides development planning, oversees implementation, accounts for executive work. 4. Represents ward residents, takes part in county lawmaking, budget/plan consideration and executive oversight. 5. County services can arrange lawful waste management; residents can raise issues through county participation/feedback channels and representatives.
**Revision:** Which part makes county laws and oversees the executive? County assembly.

## Chapter reviews — assessment criteria

For each chapter project, check four aspects: accuracy of the relevant concepts; application in a local or fictional example; clarity of the learner's diagram, explanation or performance; and responsible, safe participation. Use oral, written, tactile or practical evidence where appropriate. The learner can exceed expectations by giving accurate reasons and transferring the idea to a fresh situation. A learner meeting expectations demonstrates the target accurately with limited support. A learner approaching expectations demonstrates part of it and needs focused reteaching; a learner below expectations needs a supported model and a fresh, simpler check.

For the integrated annual community service learning activity, agree with the school and other learning areas on a safe local issue, learning targets and roles. Obtain adult oversight, assess the process and final product, and hold a reflection on what worked, what did not, improvements and learning. A paper plan or classroom simulation is suitable when safe community participation is unavailable; do not claim it is evidence of completed community service.
