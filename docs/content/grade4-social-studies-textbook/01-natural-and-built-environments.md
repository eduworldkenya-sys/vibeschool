# Chapter 1 — Our natural and built environments

## 1.1 Finding our way

At the school gate, Travis tells a new learner, “The library is that way.” He points towards a building. The learner walks in the wrong direction. Travis realises that pointing is not always enough. A map and clear directions can help people find their way.

### The four main directions

A compass helps us find directions. Its four main directions are called cardinal points: north (N), east (E), south (S) and west (W). North and south are opposite each other. East and west are also opposite each other. A compass needle settles approximately along the north–south direction. Its marked end helps us identify north.

On many maps, north is at the top. However, a map may be turned in a different direction. Always look at its north arrow before deciding which way to go.

Look at this simple school map. Read it with north at the top.

```text
                     N
                     ↑
               School garden
                     |
   Library ----- Classroom ----- Water tank
                     |
                  Main gate
                     S
```

The library is west of the classroom. The water tank is east of the classroom. If David stands at the gate and walks towards the classroom, he moves north. To return to the gate he moves south. A direction describes the position of one place in relation to another place.

### Giving clear directions

Suppose a visitor is standing at the library and wants to go to the water tank. First, the visitor moves east to the classroom. The visitor then continues east to the water tank. If the visitor is at the garden and wants to reach the gate, the visitor moves south past the classroom.

When we give directions, we name the starting place, the direction and the destination. We can also mention a landmark along the way. A landmark is a feature that helps people recognise a place.

Directions are useful to people delivering goods, visitors, farmers moving around their land and travellers reading maps. At unfamiliar places, follow safe paths and ask a trusted adult for help. A compass direction does not tell us whether a path is safe.

### Try it

Draw a four-point compass rose in your exercise book. Label the directions. Draw a simple plan of your room or school compound with a north arrow. Ask a partner to give directions between two features. If learning at home, write directions between two objects in your drawing.

### Check your understanding

1. Name the four cardinal points and their abbreviations.
2. In the school map, which direction is the garden from the classroom?
3. Which direction is the classroom from the water tank?
4. Give directions from the gate to the library.
5. Explain one way directions help people in everyday life.

## 1.2 Knowing our county

Kenya is divided into 47 counties. A county is an area with its own county government. Counties contain smaller administrative areas called sub-counties. Learning the names of places in our county helps us describe where we live and understand how people obtain services.

### Where is our county?

A county map shows its boundaries and important places. A boundary is a line separating one area from another. Two counties are neighbours when they share a boundary. A map key explains the symbols used on a map.

For example, Nairobi County shares boundaries with Kiambu, Kajiado and Machakos counties. Find Nairobi on a county map of Kenya. Then trace its boundary and identify the counties touching it. A county that is close but does not share the boundary is not a neighbouring county in this sense.

A good map of your own county can help you identify sub-counties. Use a current map from a reliable source and ask your teacher or guardian to help you check unfamiliar names. Do not guess a sub-county simply because you have heard the name of a town.

### Comparing county sizes

Some counties cover a larger area of land than others. Area tells us how much surface a place occupies. A county with a large area may have fewer residents than a smaller county. Area and population are different measurements.

Compare county outlines drawn at the same scale. Imagine placing equal-sized squares over each outline. An outline that covers more squares has a greater area. Count full squares and estimate the partly covered squares. This gives an approximate comparison, not the exact area.

A large drawing of a small county does not make the real county larger. Check that maps use the same scale before comparing them.

Knowing the location and size of counties helps people plan journeys, locate services and understand their surroundings.

### Investigate

With a teacher or trusted adult, use a current county map. Write your county's name, list its sub-counties, and identify its neighbours. Draw an outline, add a north arrow and label neighbouring counties. Compare it with one neighbouring county using the same map scale.

### Check your understanding

1. What is a county boundary?
2. Name two counties that share a boundary with Nairobi.
3. What is the difference between a county's area and its population?
4. Why must two maps have the same scale before you compare the sizes of counties?
5. Make a small county fact card with its name, two sub-counties and one neighbour. Check your information against a reliable map.

## 1.3 Land and water features

When Venessa travels from her home to visit relatives, she notices flat land, raised land and a river running through a valley. These are physical features: natural shapes or features of the Earth's surface. A road, bridge or house is made by people and is not a natural physical feature.

### Recognising physical features

A hill is raised land, usually lower than a mountain. A mountain rises high above the surrounding land. A valley is a low area between hills or other raised land. A plain is a broad area of fairly flat land. A plateau is an area of high land with a relatively flat top.

A river is flowing water in a natural channel. A lake is a body of water surrounded by land. Physical features vary from county to county; not every county has a lake, a mountain or a large river.

```text
      Mountain                Plateau
          /\                  ______
         /  \        Hill    /      \
 _______/    \______/\_____/        \____ Plain
                   ↓
                 Valley
```

A sketch can show the general shape of a feature. It does not have to be a perfect picture of the real landscape. The low area between raised features is a valley.

### Why these features matter

Rivers may provide water for farming and other needs when the water is managed safely. Lakes can support fishing, tourism and habitats for living things. Fertile plains and some valleys support farming and settlement. Hills and mountains may attract visitors and contain forests that protect water catchment areas.

Physical features also influence where roads and buildings can be constructed. Building on very steep slopes can be difficult. Settling in areas that often flood can be dangerous.

We care for our physical environment by avoiding littering, protecting vegetation and keeping water sources clean. Do not enter rivers, climb steep slopes or visit unfamiliar places without appropriate adult supervision.

### Make a landscape model

Use paper, clay or clean recycled materials to represent a plain, hill, valley and river. Label each feature. Explain how the river may be useful and how people can care for it. Alternatively, draw and label a landscape.

### Check your understanding

1. Which feature is a low area between raised land?
2. How is a plateau different from a plain?
3. Give two ways a river can help a community.
4. Draw and label a hill, a valley and a river.
5. Describe one action you can take to protect a physical feature.

## 1.4 Weather and seasons

On a rainy morning, Laban wears a raincoat and leaves home with his guardian earlier than usual. The road is muddy and vehicles move more slowly. On a hot afternoon, he carries drinking water and takes breaks in the shade. Weather affects what people do.

### Four common seasonal conditions

In Kenya, we commonly describe periods of weather as hot, cold, dry or rainy. Their timing and duration differ between counties. Hot weather can occur during a dry period, and a rainy period can also be cool. These descriptions are not four fixed, identical seasons across the country.

During hot periods, people may wear light clothing, seek shade and drink safe water regularly. In cold periods, people wear warmer clothes and may change their outdoor activities. During dry periods, water may become scarce, so people conserve water and farmers may adjust their work. Rainy periods can support planting and growth of crops but can also cause mud, floods and delays.

### Staying safe on rainy roads

Rain can make road surfaces slippery and reduce drivers' ability to see clearly. Floodwater may conceal holes or strong currents. Pedestrians should use safe crossing places, avoid walking through floodwater and follow a trusted adult's guidance. Never attempt to cross a flooded road, even if another person has crossed it.

### Keeping a weather chart

A weather chart records observations in an organised way. Here is an example for five days. These observations are imaginary.

| Day | Weather observed | Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Sunny and hot | Rested in shade |
| Tuesday | Cloudy and cool | Wore a sweater |
| Wednesday | Rainy | Used a raincoat |
| Thursday | Rainy | Observed muddy paths safely |
| Friday | Dry and sunny | Watered plants carefully |

One week of observations tells us about that week's weather. It does not prove the beginning or end of a whole season. Keep records over longer periods to notice patterns.

### Observe and record

Make a weather chart for seven days. Observe from a safe place at roughly the same time each day. Record what you see, and write how the weather influenced one activity. Compare your results with a friend's.

### Check your understanding

1. Name four seasonal conditions people experience in Kenya.
2. Explain one way a dry period affects farming.
3. Why can rainy weather make travel more dangerous?
4. What should a learner do when a usual walking route is flooded?
5. Make a three-day weather chart and explain one pattern you observe.

## 1.5 Keeping our history alive

Rita visits a cultural centre with her family. She sees old tools, musical instruments and photographs. A guide explains how people used them. Rita learns that history is not only something written in a book; people can learn from places, objects and stories.

### Historic places and buildings

A museum collects, cares for and displays objects of cultural, historical or scientific importance. A monument is built or preserved to remember a person, event or important part of history. A cultural centre helps communities preserve and share cultural practices, art and knowledge.

Fort Jesus in Mombasa is an example of a historic building. The Nairobi National Museum is an example of a museum. Your county may have historic buildings, monuments, museums or cultural centres of its own. Some places are important to a community even when they are not famous throughout Kenya.

### Why should we preserve them?

Historic places help us learn about people's lives in the past. They protect evidence such as objects, photographs, buildings and written records. They can support tourism and provide work for guides and other community members. They may also give people a sense of belonging and respect for different histories.

Old objects and buildings can be damaged by water, careless handling, fire or graffiti. Visitors should obey rules, avoid touching fragile objects without permission and leave displays where they belong. Never collect artefacts from a historic site or remove part of a building as a souvenir.

### Our history project

Ask a trusted adult about a historic place in your county. Make a picture booklet with its name, location, what makes it important and one way to protect it. Draw the place if you cannot visit it. If a supervised class visit is possible, take photographs only where permission is given. A supervised litter-collection activity in a safe approved area can help care for a site; do not touch sharp or unknown objects.

### Check your understanding

1. What is the purpose of a museum?
2. How is a monument different from a cultural centre?
3. Give two reasons historic places are important.
4. Explain why visitors should not write on an old building.
5. Suggest one safe activity that could help conserve a historic place.
