# Chapter 2 — People and population

## 2.1 We need one another

It is early morning at a market. David's family brings vegetables from their farm. A shopkeeper sells bread, and a transport worker brings sacks of maize to the market. Later, a nurse attends to a patient at the health centre. Each person provides something that other people need. None of them can provide everything alone.

### How people depend on one another

Interdependence means that people rely on one another to meet their needs. A farmer grows food, but may need tools, seeds, transport and customers. The customers need the farmer's food. A shopkeeper needs suppliers and buyers. In a health centre, nurses, doctors, cleaners and other workers perform different duties so that patients can receive care.

People also depend on one another in places of worship and in their neighbourhoods. They may encourage one another, share information, help someone who is ill or work together on a community activity. People can cooperate even when they have different beliefs, occupations or backgrounds.

Consider this chain:

```text
 Farmer grows beans
         ↓
 Transport worker carries beans
         ↓
 Trader makes beans available
         ↓
 Family buys and cooks beans
```

The arrows show how a product reaches a family. Money paid for the beans helps support the people whose work made the beans available. Other relationships, such as helping a neighbour, may not involve money at all.

### Why interdependence matters

When people work together, they can accomplish tasks that would be difficult to do alone. They can exchange skills, obtain goods and services, solve problems and support people who need assistance. Cooperation can create friendships and make a community stronger.

For interdependence to benefit everyone, people should treat one another fairly. They should keep promises, respect people's work and avoid taking advantage of others. A person who cannot contribute in the same way as others still deserves respect.

Imagine a water pipe serving several homes is broken. Some neighbours can report the problem, others can help organise a safe temporary supply, and trained workers can repair the pipe. Children should not try to repair the pipe or enter a dangerous work area.

### Draw the connections

Choose a product used at home, such as a loaf of bread, a school exercise book or a shirt. Draw a chart showing three or more people involved in making it available. Add a note explaining what each person contributes. With a group, act out the roles; if learning alone, write a short dialogue instead.

### Check your understanding

1. What does interdependence mean?
2. Explain how a farmer and a trader depend on each other.
3. Name two people whose work helps a health centre serve patients.
4. Draw a chart showing how a product moves from a producer to a customer.
5. A neighbour is unable to carry a heavy bag. Explain a safe way you could help and why it matters.

## 2.2 Where people live

Look out of a vehicle window on a journey from a town to a farming area. You may see many houses close together near a market and fewer houses across a large stretch of farmland. People are not spread evenly across every part of a county.

### Patterns of population distribution

Population is the number of people living in a given place. Population distribution describes how those people are spread across that place. When many people live close together, the area is densely populated. When relatively few people live across a large area, it is sparsely populated.

People may live in clusters, with homes grouped together, or along a route such as a road. In some places homes are scattered far apart. A map can show these patterns using dots. In the imaginary maps below, each dot stands for the same number of people.

```text
 Close together    Along a route     Scattered
 • • •             •                 •        •
 • • •             •                    •
 • • •             •                 •        •
```

A dot map does not usually identify a person's actual house. Its key tells us what each dot represents. A map with more dots in one area shows a greater concentration of the represented population there.

### Why distribution varies

People often choose to live near jobs, water, schools, health services and transport routes. Land suitable for farming can attract settlement. Steep slopes, very dry conditions and areas at risk of flooding may make settlement difficult. These are possible influences, not rules that explain every place.

A sparsely populated area may still contain important communities, farms and wildlife habitats. A densely populated area is not automatically a better place to live. Both types of area need appropriate services.

Population maps help people plan schools, roads, health facilities and other services. A county may need different solutions for a village where homes are far apart and a busy urban neighbourhood.

### Investigate a pattern

Draw two same-sized rectangles. Put twelve dots close together in one rectangle. Spread twelve dots evenly across the second rectangle. Both rectangles have the same number of dots, but the dots are distributed differently. Explain the difference. With your teacher, compare the exercise with a reliable map of population distribution in your county. Do not mark the location of private homes or identify individual residents.

### Check your understanding

1. What is the difference between population and population distribution?
2. What does densely populated mean?
3. Give two possible reasons people may settle near a town.
4. Draw a dot pattern showing homes scattered over a wide area.
5. Why might a county need information about where people live before choosing a location for a new health centre?
