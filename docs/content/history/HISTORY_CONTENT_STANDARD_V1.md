# VibeSchool History Content Standard v1

Status: candidate standard for Form 4 History & Government manual production.

## Mission
Produce curriculum-faithful, historically accurate, teachable, memorable and KCSE-useful learning artifacts. A learner who relies on a certified unit should be able to understand the syllabus objective, explain it in their own words, connect it to chronology and causation, and answer aligned examination questions.

## Non-negotiable principles
1. Curriculum authority and teaching content are separate layers.
2. Never silently expand, narrow or rewrite the curriculum objective.
3. Facts, dates, names, treaties, places and quotations must be traceable to reliable sources.
4. Distinguish long-term causes, contributing conditions, immediate triggers, course, consequences and significance.
5. Explain before compressing into revision notes.
6. Questions must test what was taught and match their command word.
7. The author cannot certify its own work.
8. No fabricated quotations, anecdotes, statistics, sources or examiner claims.
9. Kenyan learners are the primary audience; global history must still be situated accurately and clearly.
10. One canonical knowledge artifact should support Learn, Revise and Exam experiences.

## Production personas / skills
### Curriculum Guardian
Locks syllabus code, objective, content boundary, source/version and traceability. Fails syllabus drift.

### Historical Researcher
Builds a claim/evidence ledger. Separately verifies chronology, names, geography, treaties, statistics and disputed interpretations.

### History Author / Teacher
Writes as an excellent Kenyan secondary-school History teacher: clear, rigorous, human, explanatory and age-appropriate. Teaches relationships, not lists.

### Historical Reasoning Specialist
Checks chronology; cause/consequence; continuity/change; similarity/difference; significance; evidence; perspective; argument construction.

### Explainer
For each important idea answers: What is it? What happened? Why? How? Example/evidence? Why does it matter? How does it connect?

### Story Editor
Uses people, decisions, tension and chronology to sustain attention without inventing historical fiction.

### Misconception Detector
Identifies plausible learner errors and provides concise corrections with reasoning.

### KCSE Examiner
Checks command words, mark demand, scope, answer depth and examination usefulness. Does not claim a question is a past-paper item unless verified.

### Question Author
Creates recall, understanding, application/connection, historical reasoning and examination-practice questions without repetitive paraphrases.

### Marking-Scheme Author
Provides acceptable points, required explanation depth, mark allocation and model-answer logic. Avoids making one exact wording the only acceptable response where alternatives are historically valid.

### Fact Checker
Independently verifies factual claims and flags uncertain/disputed claims for qualification or removal.

### Learner Editor
Checks readability, mobile scanability, transitions, vocabulary load, repetition and whether explanations work for a typical Form 4 learner.

### Independent Critic
Asks: if a learner studied only this artifact, could they understand the objective and answer aligned examination questions accurately? Searches for omissions and shallow reasoning.

## Canonical unit architecture
Every full unit must support these fields/sections where applicable:
1. Curriculum identity: subject, form, syllabus code, topic/subtopic, objective(s), source locator.
2. Prerequisite knowledge.
3. Opening hook/problem/question.
4. Learner-facing outcomes.
5. Historical context: time, place and actors.
6. Key vocabulary with plain-language definitions.
7. Main teaching sections. Each major concept includes explanation, evidence/example, reasoning, connection and a retrieval/check question.
8. Chronology/timeline.
9. Key people, places, organisations and documents where relevant.
10. Maps/visuals only when they serve a learning purpose; every visual requires a caption and learning purpose.
11. Source/evidence analysis where appropriate.
12. Explicit connections between events and concepts.
13. Common misconceptions and corrections.
14. Think Like a Historian task.
15. Formative knowledge checks distributed through the lesson.
16. Topic synthesis/summary.
17. Revision mode: compressed facts, chronology, connections and memory prompts.
18. KCSE-aligned examination practice.
19. Marking scheme.
20. Model answers and at least one weak-vs-strong comparison for an explanation/essay-style item.
21. Mastery assessment.
22. Glossary.
23. Sources and claim/evidence ledger.
24. QA metadata: author version, reviewers, findings, repairs and certification status.

## Explanation standard
A bullet point is not an explanation. For an explanatory point, the learner should be able to see a causal or logical chain. Preferred pattern:
- Claim/idea.
- Context or mechanism.
- Concrete historical example/evidence.
- Consequence/significance.
- Link back to the question/objective.

## Question ladder
- Retrieval: name/state/identify/when/where.
- Understanding: describe/outline in context.
- Explanation: explain why/how using developed points.
- Connection: connect two or more causes/events/results.
- Evidence/source reasoning: infer, compare, contextualise or evaluate when suitable.
- KCSE practice: syllabus-aligned examination form and mark demand.
- Mastery: mixed, cumulative and transfer questions.

## Marking standard
Every assessed item records: command word, marks, expected depth, acceptable points, explanation requirements, common errors and a model response where useful. Mark schemes reward historically valid alternatives rather than memorised wording.

## Readability / editorial standard
- Define technical terms on first meaningful use.
- Prefer short paragraphs and meaningful headings for mobile reading.
- Avoid note-dump prose and unexplained lists.
- Do not infantilise the learner.
- Use transitions to show why the next section follows.
- Repeat strategically for retrieval, not accidentally.
- Separate fact from interpretation where interpretation is contested.

## Quality gates
A candidate cannot be CERTIFIED unless all mandatory gates pass:
- curriculum_alignment: exact objective and boundary represented;
- historical_accuracy: no known material factual error;
- evidence_traceability: material claims have adequate sources;
- completeness: objective can be learned from the artifact;
- explanation_depth: key points explain why/how, not only what;
- chronology_integrity: sequence is accurate and intelligible;
- causal_clarity: cause/trigger/consequence distinctions are sound;
- learner_readability: suitable for Form 4 and mobile reading;
- assessment_alignment: questions test taught material and objectives;
- marking_quality: marks and expected depth are defensible;
- exam_usefulness: command-word practice and developed answers are present;
- misconception_coverage: major predictable misconceptions addressed;
- source_integrity: no fabricated citation/quotation/source;
- internal_consistency: Learn, Revise, Exam and marking content do not contradict each other.

## Workflow
CURRICULUM_LOCKED -> RESEARCHED -> AUTHORED -> FACT_CHECKED -> CURRICULUM_CHECKED -> EXAM_REVIEWED -> EDITED -> CRITIQUED -> REPAIRED (if required) -> CERTIFIED -> PUBLICATION_ELIGIBLE.

CERTIFIED is not the same as published. Publication remains a separate governed action.

## Gold-standard specimen
The first certification specimen is Form 4 History & Government, 24.2.1 The First World War (1914-1918), Causes. Once it passes this standard, its architecture becomes the reusable History production template.
