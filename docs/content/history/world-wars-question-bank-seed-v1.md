# World Wars Assessment Bank v1

Status: QA_REPAIRED_CANDIDATE / ORIGINAL VIBESCHOOL PRACTICE / NOT KNEC PAST-PAPER CONTENT

Purpose: provide enough objective-bound assessment material for classroom checks, homework, topic tests, revision and exam practice without forcing teachers to invent questions outside the certified content.

## Assessment rules
Every item must retain: curriculum leaf, objective code, command word, marks, expected depth, acceptable answer areas, misconception/diagnostic value, difficulty, provenance and question-bank version.

For explanation items, do not mark by keyword alone. A developed response should normally contain `valid factor + accurate historical detail + causal/significance link`.

For descriptive course questions, reward chronological coherence and accurate sequencing; do not reward causes/results that do not answer the command.

For discussion/evaluation, reward developed consequences/evidence and reasoned judgement where required.

## H4-WW-01 First World War — Causes · objective 24.1.0(a)

### Retrieval
1. Define militarism. (1)
2. Name the two major pre-war alliance groupings. (2)
3. Identify the immediate event at Sarajevo in June 1914. (1)
4. Name the state that received the Austro-Hungarian ultimatum of July 1914. (1)

### Understanding
5. Explain two ways nationalism increased tension before 1914. (4)
6. Explain how the naval arms race worsened Anglo-German relations. (2)
7. Explain how alliances helped a Balkan crisis become a wider European war. (4)

Indicative developed areas: strategic commitments; expectations of support; Russian support for Serbia; German support for Austria-Hungary; Franco-Russian alignment; British security concerns/Belgian neutrality in the wider escalation. Do not present alliances as automatic legal triggers for every belligerent.

### Historical reasoning
8. Distinguish an underlying cause from an immediate trigger using WWI. (4)
Expected: clear distinction + one accurate example of each + explanation of relationship.

9. `The assassination of Franz Ferdinand was more a trigger than a complete cause of WWI.` Explain. (6)

### KCSE-style VibeSchool practice
10. Explain five causes of the First World War. (10)
Indicative areas: nationalism; imperial rivalry; militarism/arms race; alliances; Balkan instability; Sarajevo assassination; July Crisis/mobilisation. Credit five distinct developed points; avoid double-crediting the same factor under different wording.

### Weak-answer diagnostic
`Countries had weapons, so war started.` Diagnosis: names militarism vaguely but lacks mechanism. Repair: explain arms expansion, mobilisation planning, suspicion and how crisis decisions became militarised.

## H4-WW-02 First World War — Course · objective 24.1.0(b)

### Retrieval
1. State the years of WWI. (2)
2. State the year the United States entered the war. (1)
3. Name the treaty associated with Russia's withdrawal. (1)
4. State the date of the armistice ending fighting on the Western Front. (1)

### Understanding
5. Explain why trench warfare developed on the Western Front. (4)
6. Explain two ways the war became a global conflict. (4)
7. Explain why 1917 was a turning-point year. (4)
Expected areas: US entry; Russian revolutions/withdrawal trajectory; strategic implications.

### Chronology
8. Arrange and annotate: Marne; Somme; US entry; Brest-Litovsk; German Spring Offensive; Armistice. (6)

### KCSE-style VibeSchool practice
9. Describe the course of the First World War from 1914 to 1918. (12 candidate marks)
Marking blueprint: up to 2 marks per accurate developed chronological stage, maximum 12. Suggested stages: 1914 outbreak/Belgium/Marne; trench stalemate; widening theatres/sea war; 1916 attritional battles; 1917 Russia/US; 1918 German offensives; Allied counterattack/Central Powers collapse/armistice. Examiner review may adjust exact convention before publication.

10. Explain three factors that helped the Allies prevail in 1918. (6)
Only use taught evidence: American manpower/material; Allied coordination/adaptation; German exhaustion/blockade/shortages; failure of Spring Offensive; weakening of Central Powers allies.

### Misconception diagnostic
`The Treaty of Versailles ended the fighting on 11 November 1918.` Correction: armistice ended fighting; Versailles was signed in 1919.

## H4-WW-06 League of Nations — Performance · objective 24.1.0(f)

### Retrieval
1. Name one area of League humanitarian/social work. (1)
2. Identify Manchuria and Abyssinia/Ethiopia as major crisis case studies. (2)
3. State one enforcement weakness. (1)
4. State one membership-related weakness. (1)

### Understanding
5. Explain why the Åland Islands case can be considered a League success. (4)
6. Explain why Manchuria damaged League credibility. (4)
7. Explain why the Abyssinian crisis exposed collective-security weakness. (4)

### Analysis
8. Distinguish institutional weaknesses from member-state choices. Give two examples of each. (8)
Institutional examples: unanimity; no standing armed force; dependence on member enforcement. Political examples: national self-interest; incomplete sanctions; reluctance to confront aggressors; inconsistent great-power participation.

### KCSE-style VibeSchool practice
9. Analyse the performance of the League of Nations. (12 candidate marks)
Expected balance: achievements in dispute settlement/humanitarian/technical work + weaknesses/failures in major-power aggression + reasoned judgement.

10. Explain five reasons the League failed to maintain international peace in the 1930s. (10)
Indicative areas: US absence/inconsistent major-power membership; unanimity/slow action; no independent force; dependence on members; weak/incomplete sanctions; national self-interest; Depression/extremism; inability to deter aggressive powers.

### Model judgement
`The League was not a complete failure: it achieved useful technical, humanitarian and some dispute-settlement successes. Its central security weakness was that enforcement depended on sustained cooperation by major states, which broke down when powerful aggressors challenged the system.`

# Cross-topic cumulative World Wars assessment

## Blueprint
Coverage must include all six official objectives and all nine leaves. Suggested 40-mark topic test:
- Section A short answer/retrieval: 10 marks
- Section B structured explanation/description: 20 marks
- Section C synthesis/judgement: 10 marks

Suggested distribution: WWI 12–14; League 12–14; WWII 12–14, while ensuring causes/course/results and formation/organisation/performance all appear.

## Sample 40-mark VibeSchool practice paper
1. State two causes of WWI. (2)
2. State the date of the WWI armistice. (1)
3. Name two organs of the League. (2)
4. Define collective security. (2)
5. State the immediate trigger of WWII in Europe. (1)
6. Name two post-WWII superpowers. (2)
7. Explain three reasons why WWI became prolonged. (6)
8. Describe three features/functions of League organisation. (6)
9. Explain three reasons the League failed to maintain peace in the 1930s. (6)
10. Explain three causes of WWII. (6)
11. Explain three results shared or comparable across the two world wars. (6)

## Marking principles
- `State/name/identify`: concise accurate point.
- `Explain`: valid point + development showing how/why.
- `Describe course/organisation`: accurate sequential/structural description relevant to the command.
- `Discuss/analyse`: developed evidence, balance where appropriate, and judgement when the prompt requests it.
- Accept historically valid alternatives within curriculum scope.
- Do not claim this reproduces KNEC marking schemes.

# Teacher use modes
From this bank VibeSchool may derive: oral questions, exit tickets, class exercises, homework, quizzes, topic tests, revision sets and mock-style practice. A generated paper must record objective coverage and avoid repeating the same item unless intentional retrieval practice.

Feedback should state: what was correct; what was missing; why marks were lost; one improved model sentence/structure; which curriculum objective to revise next.
