# World Wars Assessment Bank Seed v1

Status: PRE-QA / PRACTICE CONTENT / NOT KNEC OFFICIAL MARKING SCHEMES

Purpose: front-load assessment architecture for all nine World Wars units before QA. Questions are original VibeSchool practice items aligned to the syllabus scope; they must be examiner-reviewed before publication.

## H4-WW-01 First World War — Causes
Retrieval: Define militarism. Name the two major pre-war alliance groupings. Identify the immediate event at Sarajevo. State two areas of European rivalry before 1914. Identify the state against which Austria-Hungary issued the July 1914 ultimatum.
Understanding: Explain how nationalism increased tension in Europe. Explain how imperial rivalry contributed to hostility. Describe how the alliance system could turn a regional crisis into a wider war.
Reasoning: Distinguish an underlying cause from an immediate trigger using WWI. Explain why the assassination cannot by itself account for the scale of the war.
KCSE-style practice: Explain five causes of the First World War. (10 marks). Explain how the alliance system contributed to the outbreak of WWI. (developed response; marks set after examiner review).
Mastery: Rank four major causes by importance and defend the ranking with historical evidence.

## H4-WW-02 First World War — Course
Retrieval: State the years of WWI. Name the principal Western and Eastern fronts. Identify the year the United States entered the war. Identify the agreement associated with Russia leaving the war. State the date of the armistice ending fighting on the Western Front.
Understanding: Describe trench warfare on the Western Front. Describe how the conflict expanded beyond Europe. Explain why 1917 was a turning-point year.
Reasoning: Construct a chronological chain from outbreak to armistice. Compare the strategic problems on the Western and Eastern fronts.
KCSE-style practice: Describe the course of the First World War. Explain factors that enabled the Allies to prevail, if supported by taught content.
Mastery: Build a six-event timeline and justify why each event changes the direction or scale of the war.

## H4-WW-03 First World War — Results
Retrieval: Define reparations. Name the major peace treaty with Germany. State two political results. State two economic results. State two social/human results.
Understanding: Explain how WWI changed Europe's political map. Explain how the peace settlement affected Germany. Explain the relationship between WWI's results and the creation of the League.
Reasoning: Separate immediate from longer-term consequences. Connect one result of WWI to later European instability without claiming inevitability.
KCSE-style practice: Discuss five results of the First World War. (mark allocation after examiner review). Explain political and economic consequences of WWI.
Mastery: Produce a consequence map linking human, economic, political and international effects.

## H4-WW-04 League — Formation
Retrieval: State two reasons for forming the League. Identify the city associated with League headquarters. Identify the US president strongly associated with proposals for a post-war international organization. State two aims. Define collective security.
Understanding: Explain why WWI strengthened demand for international cooperation. Explain the relationship between the peace settlement and League formation. Explain collective security in learner language.
Reasoning: Explain why creating an international body did not itself guarantee peace. Distinguish reasons for formation from functions.
KCSE-style practice: Explain five reasons for the formation of the League of Nations.
Mastery: Write an advisory note from 1919 explaining what a new international organization would need to prevent another major war.

## H4-WW-05 League — Organization
Retrieval: Name the Assembly, Council and Secretariat. Identify the judicial body associated with the League. Name one labour/humanitarian institution associated with League-era international cooperation. State one role of the Assembly.
Understanding: Describe the organization of the League. Explain the intended relationship between major organs. Distinguish Assembly and Council responsibilities.
Reasoning: Trace how an international dispute might move through League structures. Identify one organizational feature that could complicate decisive action.
KCSE-style practice: Describe the organization of the League of Nations. State functions of selected organs only where taught.
Mastery: Draw and annotate an organization chart from memory, then explain the purpose of each major organ.

## H4-WW-06 League — Performance
Retrieval: Name one area of League humanitarian/social work. Identify the Manchurian crisis. Identify the Abyssinian crisis. State one enforcement weakness. State one membership-related weakness.
Understanding: Explain one League achievement. Explain why the Manchurian crisis damaged credibility. Explain why the Abyssinian crisis exposed collective-security weaknesses.
Reasoning: Distinguish institutional weaknesses from decisions made by member states. Assess the claim that the League was a complete failure.
KCSE-style practice: Analyse the performance of the League of Nations. Explain factors that limited its effectiveness.
Mastery: Use an achievements/failures evidence matrix to reach a balanced judgement on League performance.

## H4-WW-07 Second World War — Causes
Retrieval: State the year WWII began in Europe. Identify the invasion that triggered British and French declarations of war. Define appeasement. Name the 1939 German-Soviet agreement. State two examples of Axis aggression/expansion taught in the unit.
Understanding: Explain how dissatisfaction with the post-WWI settlement contributed to instability. Explain how failure of collective security encouraged aggression. Explain appeasement and its limitations.
Reasoning: Distinguish long-term causes from the immediate outbreak. Connect League weakness, aggression and appeasement without treating any one factor as sufficient alone.
KCSE-style practice: Explain five causes of the Second World War. Explain how failure of the League contributed to WWII.
Mastery: Create a causal web and identify the three strongest interactions among causes.

## H4-WW-08 Second World War — Course
Retrieval: Identify the invasion that opened war in Europe. Define blitzkrieg. Name Operation Barbarossa. Identify Pearl Harbor. State the year of D-Day.
Understanding: Describe early Axis successes. Explain how the war became global. Describe major Allied advances toward victory.
Reasoning: Identify and defend two major turning points. Construct a Europe/Pacific parallel timeline.
KCSE-style practice: Describe the course of the Second World War. Explain selected turning points only where the command word and taught evidence require explanation.
Mastery: Produce a chronological briefing covering Europe, North Africa/Eastern Front and Pacific without mixing theatres or dates.

## H4-WW-09 Second World War — Results
Retrieval: Name the international organization created after WWII. State two political results. State two economic/social results. Identify the two emerging superpowers. State one consequence for colonial empires/African nationalism.
Understanding: Explain why WWII weakened several European imperial powers. Explain how the war changed the international balance of power. Explain the relationship between WWII and the creation of the UN.
Reasoning: Compare selected results of WWI and WWII. Explain why decolonization should not be reduced to WWII as a single cause.
KCSE-style practice: Discuss five results of the Second World War. Explain political consequences of WWII.
Mastery: Write a supported judgement: Which result of WWII most reshaped international relations after 1945?

## Cross-topic cumulative assessment
1. Compare the causes of WWI and WWII.
2. Explain how the results of WWI influenced the international system between the wars.
3. Assess how League performance connects WWI peace efforts to the outbreak of WWII.
4. Compare the international consequences of the two world wars.
5. Build a 1914–1945 master timeline with at least twelve curriculum-relevant turning points.

## Examiner review checklist
For every item: command word matches expected response; marks match workload; no untaught content; no ambiguous singular answer where multiple interpretations are valid; no false premise; dates/names verified; model answer does not overclaim; alternative valid responses supported; wording suitable for Form 4; original practice item is not represented as an actual KNEC past-paper question.
