# 24.2.1(c) First World War — Results
Status: DEEP_DRAFT — not certified/published.

## Big idea
WWI did not end when soldiers stopped firing. It altered borders, governments, economies, societies and international relations. A good learner should be able to classify results and explain how wartime destruction produced post-war political change.

## Human and social consequences
Millions of military personnel and civilians died, while many more were wounded, disabled, displaced or psychologically affected. Exact totals vary by source and methodology, so final content must use verified ranges rather than unsupported headline numbers. The war also disrupted families and communities and created major refugee, food and public-health problems.

The influenza pandemic of 1918–20 overlapped with the end of the war and caused enormous global mortality. It should not simply be labelled a direct `result of WWI`; wartime troop movement and social disruption are relevant contexts, but the pandemic requires careful causal wording.

## Economic effects
War consumed vast resources. Governments borrowed heavily, raised taxes, expanded currency/credit and redirected industry toward military production. Infrastructure and productive regions were damaged, especially in heavily fought areas. European economies emerged strained while the United States strengthened its financial/economic position relative to pre-war Europe.

## Political collapse of empires
The war contributed to the collapse of the German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian imperial orders. New states and borders emerged, especially in central/eastern Europe. The principle of national self-determination influenced settlement rhetoric, but it was applied unevenly and minorities remained inside many new borders.

## Russian Revolution and communism
Military defeats, shortages and political crisis contributed to revolution in Russia in 1917. The Bolsheviks eventually consolidated a communist state. This had enormous consequences for later international relations and ideological conflict.

## Peace settlement and Versailles
The victorious powers met at the Paris Peace Conference. The Treaty of Versailles dealt with Germany and imposed territorial changes, military restrictions, reparations obligations and the controversial war-responsibility clause. Other treaties dealt with Germany's former allies.

Teacher caution: avoid claiming Versailles alone caused WWII. Explain how grievances surrounding the settlement later became politically important alongside many other developments.

## League of Nations
The peace settlement created the League of Nations as an international organisation intended to promote cooperation and collective security. This result is the bridge to Chapter 2. Learners should see the sequence: devastation of WWI → search for a new peace/security mechanism → League.

## Mandates and colonial consequences
Former German colonies and former Ottoman territories were redistributed under the League mandates system rather than simply becoming independent. The system was presented as international supervision but retained imperial control in new forms. Exact territory-by-territory treatment belongs in source-verified detail.

## Africa and colonial societies
African territories contributed soldiers, carriers, labour and resources during the war and some African regions experienced direct fighting. After the war, expectations and political consciousness changed in parts of Africa, while colonial rule continued and former German colonies were redistributed. WWI should be treated as one influence in the longer development of African nationalism, not as an instant cause of independence.

## Social and gender change
Wartime mobilisation expanded women's participation in sectors of the economy in several countries as men entered military service. Political and social consequences differed between countries; avoid the simplistic claim that WWI universally `gave women equality`. In some states, suffrage reforms occurred around/after the war, but the relationship varied and broader pre-war movements were crucial.

## International power relations
Europe was weakened economically and demographically. The United States became increasingly important economically, although US political retreat from League membership complicated the post-war international order. Japan had also expanded its international position during the conflict. The new balance was unstable rather than a simple transfer of all power to one country.

## Military/technological legacy
The war accelerated development/use of aviation, tanks, submarines, communications, industrial logistics and medical practices. It also demonstrated the destructive potential of industrialised warfare and chemical weapons, influencing later military planning and arms-control debates.

## Psychological/cultural impact
The scale and character of the war affected literature, memorial culture, politics and public attitudes toward future conflict. In later years, memories of WWI strongly influenced policymakers and publics, including the desire to avoid another European war.

## Cause → consequence map
Industrial war → casualties/destruction/debt; defeat/exhaustion → imperial collapse/revolutions; peace conference → new borders/treaties/mandates; fear of another war → League; colonial participation/redistribution → longer-term political effects; traumatic memory → interwar attitudes.

## Misconceptions
- `All empires disappeared.` Several major continental empires collapsed, but imperialism/colonial rule continued.
- `Self-determination was applied everywhere.` It was selective and incomplete.
- `WWI immediately liberated African colonies.` False.
- `Versailles alone caused WWII.` Over-simplified.

## Teacher notes
Teach results in categories: Human/Social, Economic, Political/Territorial, International, Colonial. Require learners to turn each result into an explanation: `what changed + why it followed from the war + significance`.

## Homework
Classify ten results into categories; explain five results of WWI; write a paragraph connecting WWI results to formation of the League; compare one European and one African/colonial consequence.

## KCSE-style VibeSchool practice
`Discuss five results of the First World War.` Strong answers develop consequences rather than name them. Example: `The war weakened and contributed to the collapse of several European empires. Their defeat/revolution led to new states and borders, changing the political map of Europe and creating new minority/border problems.`
