# 24.2.1(b) First World War — Course
Status: DEEP_DRAFT — not certified/published.

## Big idea
The war did not follow the short-war expectations of 1914. A fast German advance in the west was checked; entrenched armies produced a long war of attrition; fighting spread across several theatres and empires; sea power and economic blockade mattered; new weapons increased killing without automatically restoring movement; the entry of the United States and the collapse/exhaustion of the Central Powers helped transform the balance in 1917–18.

## Learner outcomes
Describe the major phases of WWI in chronological order; distinguish Western/Eastern and other theatres; explain why trench warfare developed; identify major turning points; explain how the war ended.

## 1914: from mobilisation to a two-front war
Germany faced France in the west and Russia in the east. German planning sought to defeat France rapidly by advancing through Belgium before concentrating against Russia. The invasion of neutral Belgium also helped bring Britain into the war. German forces advanced into France, but the advance was halted at the First Battle of the Marne in September 1914. Both sides then tried to outflank one another until a continuous defensive front developed from the North Sea toward Switzerland.

**Why this matters:** the failure to achieve a rapid western victory helped create the prolonged two-front struggle Germany had hoped to avoid.

## The Western Front and trench warfare
By late 1914, opposing armies dug extensive trench systems. Machine guns, artillery, barbed wire and defensive depth made frontal attacks extremely costly. Trenches were not simply holes: systems included front, support and reserve lines, communication trenches, dugouts and defensive strongpoints.

Life at the front involved bombardment, mud, disease, rats, exposure, fear and long periods of waiting interrupted by intense violence. Conditions varied by sector and season, so avoid the myth that every soldier spent four uninterrupted years in a front trench.

### Why offensives caused huge losses
Attackers had to cross exposed ground while defenders could use artillery and machine guns from prepared positions. Artillery preparation could destroy defences but could also warn defenders. Commanders tried new tactics and technologies, but adaptation took time.

## Verdun and the Somme, 1916
Verdun became a prolonged German-French battle and symbol of French resistance and attrition. The Somme offensive involved British and French forces against Germany and produced enormous casualties. Tanks were first used by the British during the Somme, though early tanks were limited and unreliable.

**Teaching point:** do not make learners memorise casualty totals without context. The examination value is understanding attrition, industrial warfare and why neither battle produced the decisive breakthrough hoped for.

## Eastern Front
The Eastern Front covered a much larger geographical area and was generally more mobile than the Western Front. Germany defeated Russian forces at Tannenberg in 1914, while Russia fought Germany and Austria-Hungary across the east. Heavy military losses and domestic strain contributed to crisis inside the Russian Empire.

In 1917 revolution transformed Russia. The Bolshevik government sought to leave the war, and the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk in March 1918 ended Russia's participation, allowing Germany to transfer forces westward.

## Other theatres and a world war
Fighting extended beyond France and eastern Europe. The Ottoman Empire entered the war on the Central Powers side. Campaigns occurred in the Middle East, the Dardanelles/Gallipoli, Africa and elsewhere. Colonial empires supplied soldiers, carriers, labour, food and resources. This global participation is one reason the conflict deserves the name world war.

### Gallipoli
The Allied Dardanelles/Gallipoli campaign of 1915 sought strategic access through the straits and pressure on the Ottoman Empire, among other aims. Ottoman resistance defeated the campaign and Allied forces evacuated. Teach Gallipoli as an example of the wider war and strategic failure, not as an isolated anecdote.

### Africa and East Africa
German colonies became wartime theatres. In East Africa, forces under Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck conducted a prolonged campaign against Allied forces. African soldiers and especially large numbers of carriers supported operations under extremely difficult conditions. Detailed Kenya/East Africa participation and human impact must use dedicated authoritative sources during fact certification.

## War at sea
Britain used naval superiority and blockade to restrict supplies reaching the Central Powers. Germany relied increasingly on submarines (U-boats) to attack shipping. The Battle of Jutland in 1916 was the largest major fleet engagement between Britain and Germany; although tactically contested, the British fleet retained strategic control sufficient to continue the blockade.

Germany's unrestricted submarine warfare became especially important because attacks on shipping affected neutral states and contributed to worsening relations with the United States.

## Technology and total war
WWI combined older and newer technologies: heavy artillery, machine guns, aircraft, submarines, poison gas, tanks, railways and modern communications. Technology changed reconnaissance, logistics and killing power. Yet technology did not automatically create quick victory because opponents adapted and defensive systems remained formidable.

Civilian economies were increasingly organised for war. Governments expanded production, taxation/borrowing, rationing and propaganda and drew women into many forms of wartime work. The exact term `total war` should be explained as increasing mobilisation of state, economy and society, not as meaning every civilian performed the same role.

## 1917: a critical year
Two developments changed the strategic picture.

**Russia:** revolution and eventual withdrawal removed a major Allied power from the eastern war.

**United States:** the United States entered the war against Germany in April 1917. Important causes included unrestricted submarine warfare and the wider deterioration of US-German relations. American manpower, finance and material increasingly strengthened the Allies, especially as forces arrived in greater numbers in 1918.

## 1918: German offensives and Allied advance
After Russia's withdrawal, Germany transferred forces west and launched major offensives in spring 1918. They achieved advances but failed to secure decisive victory and exhausted valuable manpower/resources. The Allies then counterattacked during the later months of 1918, increasingly supported by American forces and improved coordination/tactics.

Germany's allies weakened or left the war. Military setbacks combined with blockade, shortages, exhaustion and political unrest. Germany requested an armistice.

## Armistice
The armistice took effect on 11 November 1918, ending fighting on the Western Front. **Armistice is not the same as the final peace treaty.** The Treaty of Versailles with Germany followed in 1919.

## Timeline
1914 outbreak → Marne → trenches; 1915 Gallipoli/gas escalation; 1916 Verdun/Somme/Jutland; 1917 US entry + Russian revolutions; March 1918 Brest-Litovsk; spring 1918 German offensives; Allied counteroffensive; 11 Nov 1918 armistice.

## Misconceptions
- `WWI was fought only in trenches in France.` It was global and multi-theatre.
- `Tanks immediately ended trench warfare.` Early tanks had serious limitations; tactical change was gradual.
- `America entered in 1914.` US entry came in 1917.
- `11 November was the Treaty of Versailles.` It was the armistice ending fighting; peace settlement followed.

## Formative checks
Why did trench warfare develop? Why was 1917 a turning point? How did Russia's withdrawal both help Germany and create a new urgency? Distinguish armistice from peace treaty.

## KCSE-style VibeSchool practice
`Describe the course of the First World War.` A strong response should be chronological and selective: outbreak/1914 plans and Marne → trench/attrition phase and major fronts → global/sea dimensions → 1917 changes → 1918 offensives/collapse → armistice. Do not answer a course question with a list of causes or results.

## Quick revision
1914 rapid-war plans fail → trenches/attrition; east more mobile; war spreads globally; sea blockade/U-boats; 1916 attritional battles; 1917 Russia destabilises/leaves while US enters; 1918 German gamble fails → Allied advance → Central Powers collapse → armistice.
