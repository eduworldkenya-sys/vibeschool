# VibeSchool Human Editorial Standard

## Purpose
VibeSchool does not publish content to fill a page. Every story must help a real learner make a useful next move.

The test is simple: after roughly three minutes, the reader should be able to say **that actually helped me**.

## The reader is the hero
VibeSchool is never the hero of the article. The learner, teacher or parent is. VibeSchool's role is to notice the problem, make it clearer, and help with the next step.

Do not lead with AI, platform capabilities, curriculum architecture, features or company claims. Never explain the tow truck before acknowledging the flat tyre.

## Required story contract
Before drafting, answer these questions in plain language:

1. Who exactly is reading? Never answer only “students”.
2. What happened today that made this matter now?
3. Where are they when they encounter the problem?
4. What are they worried will happen?
5. What are they blaming themselves for?
6. What have they already tried?
7. What would they type into search or ask a friend?
8. What might they admit privately but not tell a teacher or parent?
9. What does this problem look like in an ordinary Kenyan learner's day?
10. What moment will make the reader think “that's me”?
11. What do they think the problem is?
12. What is the deeper problem?
13. What belief may be keeping them stuck?
14. What useful truth changes how they see the situation?
15. What unanswered question pulls the story forward?
16. Where is the tension?
17. Can the problem be shown in a scene instead of explained abstractly?
18. What concrete example makes the idea clear?
19. Which claims need evidence or an authoritative source?
20. What can be deleted because it is generic filler?
21. What can the reader physically do while reading?
22. Can the reader experience a small win before the ending?
23. What should they understand after three minutes that they did not understand before?
24. What should they stop doing?
25. What should they start doing?
26. Which visual explains something faster or better than prose?
27. What should the ending leave them with: clarity, control, direction, curiosity or relief?
28. What is the most natural next action?
29. Why should they remember that VibeSchool helped them?

## Human voice
Write like an observant, intelligent person who respects young people.

- Smart without sounding academic.
- Kenyan through lived context, not keyword stuffing or forced slang.
- Serious without becoming stiff.
- Warm without motivational clichés.
- Specific instead of generic.
- Confident when the evidence supports confidence.
- Never talk down to the learner.
- Never pretend to know how every learner feels.
- Never fabricate testimonials, quotations, candidate experiences or statistics.

Avoid stock AI language such as “in today's fast-paced world”, “delve into”, “academic journey”, “unlock your potential”, “whether you're”, “it is important to note”, “with the right mindset”, “you've got this”, and repetitive “moreover/furthermore” transitions.

Avoid excessive symmetry. A human story does not need every section to have identical length, identical headings or identical three-item frameworks.

## Narrative shape
Do not force a template, but strong flagship stories usually move through:

**recognisable moment → tension → diagnosis → discovery → useful action → small win → next step**

Open inside the reader's life when possible. Do not spend the first paragraphs announcing what the article will cover.

Keep paragraphs comfortable on a phone. Change rhythm naturally. Use questions, examples, short lines and longer explanatory passages where each is useful.

## Usefulness gate
A story must contain at least one action the reader can take immediately without buying anything.

Examples:
- close the notes and recall from memory;
- classify topics as Green / Amber / Red;
- inspect the last test for repeated lost marks;
- attempt five questions before rereading a chapter;
- make one pathway comparison using actual interests and strengths.

The reader should leave with a changed decision or behaviour, not merely more information.

## Visual standard
Visuals are editorial tools, not decoration.

Use a visual only when it does at least one of these:
1. creates emotional recognition;
2. explains an idea faster than words;
3. gives the reader a usable tool;
4. creates a genuinely shareable moment.

Prefer one strong human hero, one useful explanatory visual and, where warranted, one action visual. Avoid generic smiling-student imagery and dashboard-like infographic overload.

## Community and brand
VibeSchool should represent forward movement, not perfection.

Celebrate the learner who improves from 32% to 46% as naturally as the learner chasing an A. The community belongs to people who are trying, fixing, learning and moving.

Create participation where it is natural: a challenge, a question, a self-test, a decision or a small commitment. Do not manufacture engagement bait.

A useful shareable line may emerge from the story, but do not write empty slogans merely to create quote cards.

## CTA rule
Never end a story with a generic platform advertisement when a problem-specific action exists.

Bad: **Join VibeSchool today.**

Better: **Test what you actually remember.**

Better: **Find the topic costing you marks.**

Better: **Explore which Senior School direction fits you.**

The CTA continues the rescue.

## Gold-standard checks
Reject or revise a flagship story when any answer is “no”:

- Can we name the reader more precisely than “student”?
- Is there a recognisable human moment?
- Does the story address a real pain rather than merely a keyword?
- Is there an unanswered question or tension worth following?
- Does the article provide meaningful value within about three minutes?
- Can the reader do something useful before leaving?
- Is the Kenyan context intrinsic rather than pasted on?
- Are consequential factual claims sourced and current?
- Is there at least one sentence, idea or tool worth remembering or sharing?
- Does the CTA solve the next part of the same problem?
- Would a reader plausibly send this to a friend because it helped?
- Does VibeSchool feel like a useful companion rather than the main character?

## Generic-content rejection test
Temporarily remove the words VibeSchool, Kenya, KCSE/KJSEA/KPSEA and the subject name. If the article could be swapped into another country's generic education site with almost no substantive change, it is not ready.

Also ask: could a generic model produce essentially this story from a one-line prompt? If yes, add observation, specificity, evidence, judgment, lived context or a genuinely useful tool before publication.

## Flagship benchmark
The first benchmark story is **KCSE Is Close. Stop Trying to Revise Everything.** Its core transformation is not “learn revision tips”; it is:

**There is too much to revise → I know what deserves my next hour.**

Future stories may use completely different structures, but they must deliver an equally clear reader transformation.
