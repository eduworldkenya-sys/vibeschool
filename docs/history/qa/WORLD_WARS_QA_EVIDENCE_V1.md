## League of Nations — verified anchors
Source: United Nations Office at Geneva, League of Nations history/archives.

- The League's political credibility and collective-security system were seriously damaged by its failure to intervene effectively after Japan occupied Manchuria in 1931 and Italy invaded Abyssinia/Ethiopia in 1935.
- Nazi Germany withdrew from the League in 1933; the failed World Disarmament Conference of 1932–34 further weakened the organisation.
- QA implication: retain the draft's distinction between useful technical/humanitarian cooperation and failure to deter determined great-power aggression. Do not teach `the League did nothing`.
- Manchuria chronology anchor: Japanese occupation followed the 1931 railway incident; the League sent the Lytton Commission; the response was slow, the League refused to recognise Manchukuo, and Japan rejected the decision and moved toward withdrawal.
- Abyssinia anchor: Italy invaded in October 1935. Final lesson must explain why sanctions/collective action failed without pretending that condemnation itself amounted to effective enforcement.

## WWI — East Africa/Kenya verified anchors
Source: National Army Museum, Commonwealth and First World War / East Africa campaign materials.

- African troops played a major role in the East African campaign.
- By November 1918 the British force in East Africa was mainly composed of African soldiers, including the King's African Rifles recruited from Kenya, Uganda and Nyasaland.
- At least 180,000 Africans served in the Carrier Corps in East Africa providing logistics support according to the museum's Commonwealth overview.
- The campaign involved severe human costs for carriers/camp followers and civilians; figures vary by source and must be attributed rather than presented as uncontested totals.
- German forces under Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck operated across the East African theatre and conducted raids into British East Africa/Kenya.
- QA implication: the Kenyan/East African dimension is legitimate core contextual enrichment for VibeSchool, not invented local colour. Teach African soldiers, carriers/logistics, disease/supply burdens and the regional theatre with sourced wording.

## WWII — Kenya/East Africa verified anchors
Source: National Army Museum collection records on the King's African Rifles and East African formations.

- The King's African Rifles expanded rapidly during WWII with recruits from Kenya and Uganda among its manpower.
- Its early role included defence of Kenya against possible Italian attack from Abyssinia/Italian East Africa.
- KAR units fought in the East African/Abyssinian campaign and later served in theatres including Madagascar and Burma.
- A Kenyan-based East African reconnaissance formation patrolled the Northern Frontier District with bases including Wajir and Garissa and participated in the campaign against Italian forces; it was later renamed the Kenya Armoured Car Regiment.
- QA implication: add Kenya-specific material where it explains the global character and colonial mobilisation of WWII, but do not imply that all Kenyan wartime experience was military or that participation automatically caused independence.

## Editorial/fact repairs required
1. Attribute disputed/variable casualty totals; do not present uncertain numbers as exact.
2. Separate `armistice` from final peace treaty in WWI.
3. Separate League condemnation/investigation from successful enforcement.
4. Avoid deterministic claims such as `Versailles caused WWII` or `WWII caused African independence`.
5. Preserve the distinction between immediate triggers, contributing conditions and long-term causes.
6. In WWII Pacific conclusion, teach atomic bombings, Soviet entry and Japanese decision/surrender as a sequence; avoid unsupported single-cause certainty.
7. Do not label generated examination questions as KNEC past questions.
