# Grade 4 Mathematics
## Numbers · Whole Numbers
### Place Value and Total Value

**Our learning goal:** Explain the place value and total value of digits through ten-thousands.

**Before you begin:** Think about the ones, tens and hundreds you already know. If you need help with these smaller places, use the three-column chart first and build up to larger numbers.

### Discover: a number with a story

A community library records **24,907** books. The librarian writes this number in the register.

Look at the digits. There are five of them: 2, 4, 9, 0 and 7. Do they all represent the same amount? No. **Where a digit stands changes what it represents.**

In 24,907, the digit **4** represents **4,000** books, while **9** represents **900** books. The digit **0** shows that there are **no tens**.

**Think and talk:** If the digit 4 moved to the hundreds column, would it still represent 4,000? Explain your answer.

### Build the idea: groups of ten

We count using groups of ten. Ten ones make one ten. Ten tens make one hundred. Ten hundreds make one thousand. Ten thousands make one ten-thousand.

| A group of | Is the same as |
|---|---|
| 10 ones | 1 ten |
| 10 tens | 1 hundred |
| 10 hundreds | 1 thousand |
| 10 thousands | 1 ten-thousand |

Start at the **ones** place on the right and move left. Each new place is worth **ten times** the place before it.

### Read the place-value chart

The chart shows the position of every digit in **24,907**.

| Ten-thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 4 | 9 | 0 | 7 |
| 20,000 | 4,000 | 900 | 0 | 7 |

You can say:

- The **place** of 2 is **ten-thousands**. Its **total value** is **20,000**.
- The **place** of 4 is **thousands**. Its **total value** is **4,000**.
- The **place** of 9 is **hundreds**. Its **total value** is **900**.
- The **place** of 0 is **tens**. Its **total value** is **0**.
- The **place** of 7 is **ones**. Its **total value** is **7**.

**Three ideas to remember:**

1. **Digit:** the written symbol, such as 4.
2. **Place value:** the position, such as thousands.
3. **Total value:** how much the digit represents in that position, such as 4,000.

To find the total value, multiply the digit by the value of its place.

**4 × 1,000 = 4,000.**

The chart also helps us write numbers in **expanded form**:

**24,907 = 20,000 + 4,000 + 900 + 7.**

The zero is not written as a separate addition, but its position remains important in the number.

### Worked example 1: Find the value of a digit

**Find the place value and total value of 6 in 36,482.**

**Step 1.** Start from the right: ones, tens, hundreds, **thousands**, ten-thousands.

**Step 2.** The digit **6** is in the **thousands** position.

**Step 3.** Six thousands are **6 × 1,000 = 6,000**.

**Answer:** Place value: **thousands**. Total value: **6,000**.

**Check:** Is 6,000 a reasonable part of 36,482? Yes: the number contains 36 thousands altogether.

### Worked example 2: A zero holds a place

**Find the total value of every digit in 40,506.**

| Digit | Place | Total value |
|---:|---|---:|
| 4 | Ten-thousands | 40,000 |
| 0 | Thousands | 0 |
| 5 | Hundreds | 500 |
| 0 | Tens | 0 |
| 6 | Ones | 6 |

**40,506 = 40,000 + 500 + 6.**

The zeros show that no thousands or tens are added. They **hold those places** so that the other digits stay in the right positions.

**Important:** Removing the zeros changes the number. **40,506 is not the same as 456.**

### Worked example 3: The same digit, different values

Look at **50,505**.

The digit **5** occurs three times. Each 5 has a different total value.

- First 5: ten-thousands → **50,000**.
- Second 5: hundreds → **500**.
- Third 5: ones → **5**.

**50,505 = 50,000 + 500 + 5.**

**Explain:** Why is the first 5 worth more than the last 5, even though they are the same digit?

### Worked example 4: Changing the position changes the value

Use number cards to make **15,204**. The digit **5** is in the thousands place, so it is worth **5,000**. The digit **2** is in the hundreds place, so it is worth **200**.

Swap only the positions of **5** and **2**.

The new number is **12,504**.

Now 5 is in the hundreds place and is worth **500**. The 2 is in the thousands place and is worth **2,000**.

**What changed?** The digits stayed the same, but their positions—and therefore their total values—changed.

### Explore: build and test your own number

**Using an interactive place-value chart or five labelled columns on paper:**

1. Label five columns: **Ten-thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones**.
2. Put the digits **3, 0, 7, 4, 2** in order. Read the number: **30,742**.
3. Point to **7**. Name its place. Work out its total value.
4. Exchange the positions of **7** and **4**. Read the new number and explain what each digit is now worth.
5. Make **20,305**. Explain why both zero positions are needed.

**Using an abacus or bottle-top counters:** Build the same number using five labelled place columns. Ten counters in one column can be exchanged for one counter in the next column to the left. If counters are not available, draw circles in a place-value table.

**Explain to a partner or aloud:** “The digit ___ is in the ___ place, so its total value is ___.”

### Try it together

**A.** In **32,416**, what is the place of the digit 2? What is its total value?

**B.** In **60,304**, which digit is in the tens place? Why must that digit be shown?

**C.** Complete the sentence: “In **47,829**, the digit 8 is in the ___ place, so it is worth ___.”

**Help when you are stuck:** Begin at the **right-hand side**. Write **O, T, H, Th, TTh** above the digits. Find the digit's column before calculating its total value. Use a three-column chart first if the larger chart is difficult.

### Watch out for these common mistakes

**Mistake 1:** “The place value of 6 in 36,482 is 6,000.”

That statement confuses the **place** with the **total value**.

**Better:** The place is **thousands**. The total value is **6,000**.

**Mistake 2:** “The zeros in 40,506 can be removed.”

No. Each zero holds a position. Without the zeros, the other digits would move into different places.

**Mistake 3:** “Every digit 5 is worth five.”

Five is the **digit**. Its total value depends on where it appears. In 50,505 the three total values are 50,000, 500 and 5.

**Mistake 4:** “Always count place positions from the left.”

Place positions are counted **from the right**: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands.

### Remember

**Place value** tells us **where** a digit stands. **Total value** tells us **how much** the digit represents there.

A digit in the thousands place represents that many **thousands**. A digit in the ten-thousands place represents that many **ten-thousands**.

**Zeros can hold places.** Repeated digits can have **different total values**. To explain a value, name the **digit**, its **place**, and the **amount** it represents.
