# Grade 4 Mathematics
## Changing Hours and Minutes

**Strand:** Measurement  
**Sub-strand:** Time  
**Learning focus:** Converting between hours and minutes, including durations with extra minutes.

# PART A — COMPLETE LEARNER LESSON

## 1. What Will You Learn?

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Explain the relationship between hours and minutes.
2. Change whole hours into minutes.
3. Change hours and extra minutes into minutes.
4. Change minutes into hours and remaining minutes.
5. Represent time using diagrams and groups of sixty.
6. Compare durations expressed in different units.
7. Solve everyday problems involving hours and minutes.
8. Explain your working and check your answers.

### How will you know you understand?

You can explain **why** an answer is correct, not simply remember whether to multiply or divide.

You can also solve a new problem independently after learning from a mistake.

## 3. Understanding Hours and Minutes

### 3.1 What is a minute?

A minute is a unit used to measure time.

We use minutes for activities such as:

- Washing our hands.
- Taking a short break.
- Completing a classroom activity.
- Walking a short distance.

Some activities last only a few minutes. Others take many minutes.

### 3.2 What is an hour?

An hour is a longer unit of time.

**One hour is exactly 60 minutes.**

We can write this as:

**1 hour = 60 minutes**

This relationship does not change.

Two hours contain two groups of sixty minutes.

60 + 60 = 120 minutes

Therefore:

**2 hours = 120 minutes**

Three hours contain three groups of sixty:

60 + 60 + 60 = 180 minutes.

Therefore:

**3 hours = 180 minutes**

### 3.3 Why do we use multiplication?

Imagine that each container holds exactly 60 counters.

If we have four containers, we can find the total by adding:

60 + 60 + 60 + 60 = 240.

A faster method is:

4 × 60 = 240.

Each container represents one hour, while each counter represents one minute.

That is why, when changing whole hours into minutes, we multiply by sixty.

### 3.4 Understanding extra minutes

Not every duration is a whole number of hours.

For example, a journey may last 2 hours 35 minutes.

This means:

- One complete hour contains 60 minutes.
- Another complete hour contains 60 minutes.
- There are 35 extra minutes.

Total:

60 + 60 + 35 = 155 minutes.

**2 hours 35 minutes = 155 minutes**

The extra 35 minutes do not make another complete hour because 35 is less than 60.

### 3.5 Important distinction

The duration *2 hours 35 minutes* is not the same as 235 minutes.

The first is a duration written in two units. The second is a duration written entirely in minutes.

Always pay attention to the units beside a number.

## 4. Rules for Changing Time Units

### Rule 1: Hours to minutes

Multiply the number of complete hours by 60.

**Minutes = hours × 60**

Example:

5 hours = 5 × 60 = **300 minutes**.

### Rule 2: Hours and extra minutes to minutes

First change the complete hours into minutes. Then add the extra minutes.

**Total minutes = (hours × 60) + extra minutes**

Example:

3 hours 25 minutes

= (3 × 60) + 25

= 180 + 25

= **205 minutes**.

### Rule 3: Minutes into hours and minutes

Find how many complete groups of 60 fit into the given number of minutes.

Then find the minutes remaining.

Example:

145 minutes

= 60 + 60 + 25

= **2 hours 25 minutes**.

### Rule 4: Check the remaining minutes

When a duration is written in the usual form of hours and extra minutes, the extra minutes must be fewer than 60.

For example:

- 1 hour 30 minutes — correct.
- 2 hours 45 minutes — correct.
- 1 hour 75 minutes — can be simplified.

Since 75 minutes = 1 hour 15 minutes:

1 hour 75 minutes = **2 hours 15 minutes**.

# PART B — FULLY EXPLAINED WORKED EXAMPLES

## Example 1: Changing Whole Hours into Minutes

**Question:** Change 4 hours into minutes.

**Step 1 — Recall the relationship**

1 hour = 60 minutes.

**Step 2 — Identify the number of hours**

We have four hours, meaning four groups of sixty.

**Step 3 — Multiply**

4 × 60 = 240.

**Step 4 — Write the correct unit**

240 minutes.

**Step 5 — Check**

60 + 60 + 60 + 60 = 240.

**Answer: 4 hours = 240 minutes.**

**What have we learned?** Multiplication helps us count equal groups of sixty quickly.

---

## Example 2: Changing Hours and Extra Minutes

**Question:** Change 2 hours 35 minutes into minutes.

**Step 1 — Separate the complete hours from the extra minutes**

Complete hours: 2

Extra minutes: 35

**Step 2 — Convert the hours**

2 × 60 = 120 minutes.

**Step 3 — Add the remaining minutes**

120 + 35 = 155 minutes.

**Step 4 — Check using a model**

60 + 60 + 35 = 155.

**Answer: 155 minutes.**

**Why did we add 35?** Because those minutes are part of the same duration, but they do not form another complete hour.

---

## Example 3: Changing Minutes into Whole Hours

**Question:** Change 240 minutes into hours.

**Step 1 — Recall the relationship**

Every complete hour contains 60 minutes.

**Step 2 — Group the minutes into sixties**

240 ÷ 60 = 4.

Or count:

60, 120, 180, 240.

We counted four complete groups.

**Step 3 — Check for any extra minutes**

4 × 60 = 240.

There are no extra minutes.

**Answer: 4 hours.**

**What have we learned?** To convert a total number of minutes into complete hours, count the full groups of sixty.

---

## Example 4: Changing Minutes into Hours and Extra Minutes

**Question:** Change 187 minutes into hours and minutes.

**Step 1 — Find complete groups of sixty**

60 + 60 + 60 = 180.

There are three complete hours in 180 minutes.

**Step 2 — Find the remaining minutes**

187 − 180 = 7.

**Step 3 — Combine**

3 hours 7 minutes.

**Step 4 — Check**

3 × 60 + 7 = 187.

**Answer: 3 hours 7 minutes.**

**Important:** A remainder of 7 means seven minutes, not seventy minutes.

---

## Example 5: A Duration with an Incorrect Remainder

**Question:** Write 2 hours 80 minutes in its simplest form.

**Step 1 — Examine the extra minutes**

There are 80 minutes.

But 80 minutes contain a complete hour and 20 more minutes.

80 = 60 + 20.

**Step 2 — Add the new complete hour**

2 hours + 1 hour = 3 hours.

**Step 3 — Keep the extra minutes**

20 minutes remain.

**Answer: 3 hours 20 minutes.**

**Check:**

2 × 60 + 80 = 200 minutes.

3 × 60 + 20 = 200 minutes.

Both expressions describe the same duration, but **3 hours 20 minutes** is the simpler form.

---

## Example 6: Comparing Two Durations

**Question:** Which is longer: 2 hours 20 minutes or 135 minutes?

**Step 1 — Express both durations in the same unit**

2 hours 20 minutes = 2 × 60 + 20.

= 140 minutes.

**Step 2 — Compare**

140 minutes is greater than 135 minutes.

**Step 3 — Find the difference**

140 − 135 = 5 minutes.

**Answer: 2 hours 20 minutes is longer by 5 minutes.**

**What have we learned?** Convert to the same unit before comparing durations.

---

## Example 7: An Everyday Problem

A bus journey takes 1 hour 45 minutes. A delay adds another 35 minutes.

How long is the total journey?

**Step 1 — Convert the original journey**

1 hour 45 minutes = 60 + 45 = 105 minutes.

**Step 2 — Add the delay**

105 + 35 = 140 minutes.

**Step 3 — Convert back**

140 minutes = 120 minutes + 20 minutes.

= 2 hours 20 minutes.

**Answer: 2 hours 20 minutes.**

**Check:** The original journey lasted less than two hours, and the delay made it longer. A total of 2 hours 20 minutes is reasonable.

---

## Example 8: Identifying and Correcting a Mistake

A learner writes:

3 hours 15 minutes = 315 minutes.

**Is the answer correct?**

No.

The learner appears to have joined the numbers 3 and 15 instead of converting the three hours into minutes.

Correct working:

3 × 60 = 180 minutes.

180 + 15 = 195 minutes.

**Correct answer: 195 minutes.**

**Remember:** Never join the hour and minute numbers to obtain total minutes.

# PART C — LEARNING THROUGH ACTIVITIES

## Activity 1: Make an Hour

**You need:** 60 counters, beans, pebbles or small paper squares.

**Instructions:**

1. Make one group of ten objects.
2. Make five more groups of ten.
3. Count the groups.
4. Count all the objects.
5. Explain what each group represents.

**Questions to investigate:**

- How many groups did you make?
- How many objects are there altogether?
- What does each object represent?
- Why do six groups of ten make one hour?

**Expected discovery:** Six groups of ten minutes contain sixty minutes, which make one hour.

**Alternative:** Draw six groups of ten marks on paper.

---

## Activity 2: Build a Time Bar

**You need:** Paper, pencil and ruler if available.

**Instructions:**

1. Draw a bar representing 60 minutes.
2. Draw another bar of the same length.
3. Draw a third bar representing 30 minutes, half as long as the first.
4. Label each bar.
5. Find the total duration.

**Expected answer:**

60 + 60 + 30 = 150 minutes.

= 2 hours 30 minutes.

**Discussion:** Why must the third bar be shorter? It represents fewer minutes than a complete hour.

---

## Activity 3: Become the Teacher

Work with a partner or explain aloud by yourself.

One learner gives a duration in hours and minutes.

The other learner:

1. Converts it into minutes.
2. Explains each step.
3. Checks the answer by converting back.

Try:

- 1 hour 20 minutes.
- 2 hours 45 minutes.
- 3 hours 10 minutes.

**Expected answers:** 80 minutes, 165 minutes and 190 minutes.

Swap roles and try a new duration.

---

## Activity 4: Investigate a Real Journey

Imagine a family journey lasts 155 minutes.

**Investigate:**

1. How many complete hours does it contain?
2. How many minutes remain?
3. How can you represent it using bars?
4. Which is the clearer way to describe the journey: 155 minutes or 2 hours 35 minutes? Explain your choice.

**Expected calculation:** 2 hours 35 minutes.

Different descriptions may be useful in different situations. The mathematical duration remains the same.

# PART E — COMMON MISTAKES AND HOW TO FIX THEM

### Mistake 1: Treating an hour as 100 minutes

Incorrect: 3 hours = 300 minutes.

Correct: 3 × 60 = **180 minutes**.

**How to improve:** Draw three groups of sixty minutes.

### Mistake 2: Joining the hour and minute numbers

Incorrect: 2 hours 25 minutes = 225 minutes.

Correct: 2 × 60 + 25 = **145 minutes**.

**How to improve:** Convert the hours before adding the extra minutes.

### Mistake 3: Leaving too many extra minutes

Incorrect simplest form: 1 hour 90 minutes.

Correct simplest form: **2 hours 30 minutes**.

**How to improve:** Take another complete group of sixty from the ninety minutes.

### Mistake 4: Forgetting the remaining minutes

Incorrect: 187 minutes = 3 hours.

Correct: **3 hours 7 minutes**.

**How to improve:** After counting complete hours, subtract them from the original total.

### Mistake 5: Comparing unlike units

Incorrect: Deciding that 135 minutes is longer than 2 hours 20 minutes simply because 135 is greater than 2.

Correct: Convert 2 hours 20 minutes into 140 minutes first. Then compare 140 and 135.

**How to improve:** Always compare durations expressed in the same unit.

# PART F — REVISION AND RETENTION

## Quick Revision Notes

Remember these facts:

- One hour contains sixty minutes.
- To change complete hours into minutes, multiply by sixty.
- To change hours and extra minutes into minutes, multiply the hours by sixty and add the remaining minutes.
- To change minutes into hours, find complete groups of sixty.
- Any extra minutes in the simplest form must be fewer than sixty.
- Reverse your calculation to check the answer.

## Revision Questions

Complete these without looking at the examples.

1. Change 2 hours 50 minutes into minutes.
2. Convert 184 minutes into hours and minutes.
3. Rewrite 2 hours 70 minutes in simplest form.
4. Explain why 120 minutes equals 2 hours.
5. Is 3 hours 15 minutes equal to 315 minutes? Explain.
6. A journey lasts 215 minutes. Express it in hours and minutes.

**Answers:**

1. 170 minutes.
2. 3 hours 4 minutes.
3. 3 hours 10 minutes.
4. 120 ÷ 60 = 2 complete groups of sixty.
5. No. It equals 3 × 60 + 15 = 195 minutes.
6. 3 hours 35 minutes.

### Review your learning again

Try three new conversion questions after another lesson or on a later day.

If you cannot remember the rule, rebuild it from groups of sixty rather than guessing.

# PART G — COMPLETE TEACHER GUIDE

## Lesson Preparation

**Suggested duration:** 35 minutes. Adapt to the actual school period and learners' needs.

**Teaching and learning resources:**
- Blackboard or writing surface.
- Chalk or marker.
- Paper and pencils.
- Sixty counters, beans or small objects.
- Printed or hand-drawn 60-minute bars.
- Exercise questions and separate answer key.

A printer, digital device or purchased teaching aid is not required.

## Teaching Sequence

| Time | Teaching stage | Teacher action | Learner evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | Prior knowledge | Ask about minutes and hours | Learners recall 1 hour = 60 minutes |
| 4–10 min | Concrete modelling | Demonstrate groups of sixty | Learners count and explain equal groups |
| 10–18 min | Worked examples | Model conversions in both directions | Learners explain the steps |
| 18–25 min | Guided practice | Work through two examples and correct mistakes | Learners show their reasoning |
| 25–30 min | Independent check | Give three short questions | Learners answer without copying |
| 30–33 min | Feedback | Address misconceptions and provide fresh corrective practice | Learners explain corrected methods |
| 33–35 min | Conclusion | Review the main relationship | Learners summarize the key idea |

### Suggested Teacher Explanation

“Suppose we have two complete hours. Each hour contains sixty minutes. Instead of adding sixty twice, we can multiply two by sixty.

“Now suppose we have thirty-five extra minutes. Do we multiply thirty-five by sixty? No. Those are already minutes, so we add them to the minutes from our complete hours.

“Let us work together.”

Write:

2 × 60 = 120 minutes.

120 + 35 = 155 minutes.

“Can someone explain why we added thirty-five?”

**Expected response:** They were the extra minutes after the complete hours.

“Can somebody check our answer another way?”

**Expected response:** Group 155 minutes into 60, 60 and 35.

### Questions for Understanding

**Question:** Why do we multiply hours by sixty?

**Expected response:** Each hour contains sixty minutes.

**Question:** How many complete hours are in 185 minutes?

**Expected response:** Three complete hours.

**Question:** How many minutes remain?

**Expected response:** Five minutes.

**Question:** Why is 3 hours 65 minutes not the simplest form?

**Expected response:** Sixty of the extra minutes can form another hour, so it becomes 4 hours 5 minutes.

### Formative Assessment

Ask learners to complete:

1. 2 hours 5 minutes = ___ minutes.
2. 200 minutes = ___ hours ___ minutes.
3. Correct this: 3 hours 15 minutes = 315 minutes.

**Expected answers:**

1. 125 minutes.
2. 3 hours 20 minutes.
3. 195 minutes.

### How to Interpret Errors

| Learner response | Possible misunderstanding | Teacher response |
|---|---|---|
| 205 for question 1 | One hour treated as 100 minutes | Rebuild groups of sixty |
| 2 hours 80 minutes for question 2 | Extra minutes not regrouped | Take another complete hour |
| 315 for question 3 | Hour and minute numbers joined | Separate conversion from addition |
| Correct answer, no explanation | Understanding not yet established | Ask learner to demonstrate using bars |

Do not assume one error proves a learner lacks the whole concept. Give a short follow-up question to investigate the misunderstanding.

# PART H — DIFFERENTIATED LEARNING

## Learners Who Need More Support

Start by representing one hour with six groups of ten.

Then introduce:

- 1 hour.
- 2 hours.
- 1 hour 10 minutes.
- 2 hours 10 minutes.

Let the learner move or draw groups before attempting calculations mentally.

Use the sequence:

**See it → Count it → Write it → Explain it → Try again.**

Provide oral explanation opportunities for learners who find writing difficult.

## Learners Progressing Normally

Use combinations of full hours and extra minutes.

Move gradually from familiar values to numbers such as:

- 145 minutes.
- 185 minutes.
- 215 minutes.
- 305 minutes.

Encourage learners to check answers using a second method.

## Learners Ready for Greater Challenge

Give unfamiliar problems requiring comparison, correction and explanation.

For example:

“A learner claims 2 hours 95 minutes is longer than 4 hours. Investigate.”

Expected:

2 hours 95 minutes = 3 hours 35 minutes.

Since 3 hours 35 minutes is less than 4 hours, the claim is incorrect.

Ask the learner to justify the conclusion using two methods.

# PART I — HOME LEARNING AND INDEPENDENT STUDY

## For Parents and Guardians

Your child is learning how to change between hours and minutes.

You do not need a mathematics textbook or special teaching equipment.

Try asking:

“How many minutes are in two hours?”

Allow the child to explain using groups of sixty.

If the answer is incorrect, ask:

“How many minutes are in one hour? How many groups do we have?”

Use a familiar activity, such as a journey or a game, to make the learning meaningful.

Avoid giving the answer immediately.

## For Learners Studying Alone

Use this routine:

1. Read the explanation carefully.
2. Study one worked example.
3. Cover the worked answer and try the calculation yourself.
4. Compare your method with the explanation.
5. Attempt the independent exercises.
6. Return to the diagrams when necessary.
7. Try a fresh problem before deciding you understand.

You do not need to finish every question in one sitting.

### Assessment Design Notes

These items include direct conversions, reverse conversions, explanations, diagram interpretation, comparisons, real-life applications and misconception checks.

Before any question enters a formal assessment, verify its curriculum linkage, originality, answer, marking scheme, difficulty, accessibility and non-disclosure. An explanation revealed during practice must not become an unseen examination answer by accident.

### Suggested Marking Criteria

**One-mark calculation:** Award one mark for the correct answer with its unit.

**Two-mark conversion:** Award one mark for a correct method or mathematical representation and one mark for the correct final answer.

**Three-mark reasoning:** Award one mark for the appropriate conversion, one mark for correct reasoning or comparison, and one mark for a justified conclusion.

**Visual representation:** Check that each bar represents the correct duration, the labels are accurate and the total is calculated correctly.

Provide partial credit where the question and marking scheme permit it. A correct final number with the wrong unit should not automatically receive full credit.

# PART K — FINAL LEARNER MASTERY CHECK

Complete these questions independently, without looking at the worked examples.

1. Express 4 hours 25 minutes in minutes.
2. Change 214 minutes into hours and minutes.
3. Explain how you know that 180 minutes is exactly 3 hours.
4. A learner says 2 hours 65 minutes is already in its simplest form. Correct the learner.
5. Which is longer, 3 hours 5 minutes or 190 minutes? By how many minutes?

### Answer Key and Feedback

1. **265 minutes:** 4 × 60 + 25 = 265.
2. **3 hours 34 minutes:** 214 − 180 = 34.
3. **180 ÷ 60 = 3**, leaving no extra minutes.
4. **3 hours 5 minutes:** 65 minutes contains one extra hour.
5. **190 minutes is longer by 5 minutes:** 3 hours 5 minutes = 185 minutes.

- Can I explain why one hour contains sixty minutes?
- Can I convert hours into minutes without guessing?
- Can I change minutes back into hours?
- Can I find and correct an incorrect conversion?

If you answered no to any of these, return to the matching explanation and try a different question. Learning from corrections is part of mathematics.
