## Lesson 1 — Morning, afternoon and a.m./p.m.

**What you will learn:** Tell whether a clock time is before or after midday and say it correctly.

### Understand
A day has 24 hours. We often write the time in two 12-hour groups. a.m. describes the hours from midnight until just before midday. p.m. describes the hours from midday until just before midnight. We say 7:15 a.m. for a morning time and 7:15 p.m. for an evening time. The same number on a clock can mean different times, so include a.m. or p.m. when it matters. Be careful: 12:00 a.m. is midnight (the start of the day); 12:00 p.m. is midday (noon).

A full day has twenty-four hours. The numbers repeat on a twelve-hour clock. a.m. covers midnight until before midday and p.m. covers midday until before the next midnight.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A school assembly begins at 8:00 in the morning. Write the complete time.

1. 8:00 is before midday.
2. Times after midnight and before midday use a.m.
3. Answer: 8:00 a.m.

### A second way to think
Identify midnight and midday using a full-day line; sort personally meaningful events.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper and pencil; optional clock
1. Draw a line labelled midnight, morning, midday, afternoon, evening and midnight.
2. Write a sensible daily event beside each part of the line.
3. Tell a partner or family member which of your events happen a.m. and which happen p.m.
**What your work should show:** A simple day line with events labelled a.m./p.m.

### Practise together
- **Question:** Breakfast at 6:30 in the morning: write the time. **Answer:** 6:30 a.m. **Why:** Morning comes before midday.
- **Question:** Supper at 7:00 in the evening: write the time. **Answer:** 7:00 p.m. **Why:** Evening comes after midday.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. The library closes at 4:30 in the afternoon. Write the time.
2. The radio news begins at 6:00 in the morning. Write the time.
3. Write 12:00 noon with the correct label.

### Check your learning
1. A child arrives home at 5:15 in the afternoon. Is it a.m. or p.m.?
2. Is 12:00 a.m. midday or midnight?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **The library closes at 4:30 in the afternoon. Write the time.** 4:30 p.m.. Afternoon is after noon.
- **The radio news begins at 6:00 in the morning. Write the time.** 6:00 a.m.. Morning is before midday.
- **Write 12:00 noon with the correct label.** 12:00 p.m.. 12 noon is p.m., not a.m.
- **A child arrives home at 5:15 in the afternoon. Is it a.m. or p.m.?** p.m.. Afternoon is after midday.
- **Is 12:00 a.m. midday or midnight?** midnight. 12:00 a.m. starts the new day.

### Need more help?
Sort four event cards into 'before midday' and 'after midday' before writing the letters.
**A mistake to avoid:** Mistaking 12 a.m. for noon.

### Stretch yourself
Explain why 6:00 alone is not enough to tell a person when to meet.

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** Identify midnight and midday using a full-day line; sort personally meaningful events.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 2 — Reading analogue and digital clocks

**What you will learn:** Read an analogue clock and write the same time on a digital clock.

### Understand
An analogue clock has hands. The short hand gives the hour; the long hand gives the minutes. Each number the minute hand passes counts five minutes: 1 means 5, 2 means 10, 3 means 15, 6 means 30, 9 means 45 and 12 means 00. Between hour numbers, the short hand moves towards the next hour. A digital clock usually shows hours:minutes, such as 09:25. The minutes are two digits. You can add a.m. or p.m. when a display uses the 12-hour system. Some electronic clocks use 24-hour time; for example 15:00 is 3:00 p.m. (optional comparison).

The long minute hand counts five minutes for every number; the shorter hand identifies the hour already passed and advances gradually.

### First worked example
**Problem:** The long hand points to 5 and the short hand is just after 8. Write the time.

1. Minute hand at 5 means 5 × 5 = 25 minutes.
2. Short hand just after 8 means the hour is 8.
3. The reading is 8:25. If it is morning, write 8:25 a.m.

### A second way to think
Read 8:25 and 4:45 on a movable clock and match each to its digital reading.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper clock face with movable paper hands, or draw a clock
1. Set the long hand to 3 and short hand just after 9; read 9:15.
2. Set the hands to show 4:30 and write 04:30 on paper.
3. Compare the two representations with a partner; discuss why the hour hand moves gradually.
**What your work should show:** Two matching analogue and digital readings

### Practise together
- **Question:** Long hand at 6, short hand between 2 and 3: what time? **Answer:** 2:30 **Why:** 6 × 5 = 30 minutes; it is still the second hour.
- **Question:** Long hand at 12, short hand at 11: what time? **Answer:** 11:00 **Why:** The minute hand shows zero minutes.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. Minute hand at 9, hour hand almost at 5 but not yet: what time?
2. Write quarter past 7 digitally.
3. Write twenty minutes past 10 digitally.

### Check your learning
1. A digital clock shows 06:05 in the morning. Read it with a.m.
2. On an analogue clock the minute hand is at 4. How many minutes past?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **Minute hand at 9, hour hand almost at 5 but not yet: what time?** 4:45. 45 minutes have passed after 4; the hour hand approaches 5.
- **Write quarter past 7 digitally.** 7:15. Quarter of an hour is 15 minutes.
- **Write twenty minutes past 10 digitally.** 10:20. Hours are before the colon and minutes after.
- **A digital clock shows 06:05 in the morning. Read it with a.m.** 6:05 a.m.. 05 means five minutes past six.
- **On an analogue clock the minute hand is at 4. How many minutes past?** 20 minutes. 4 × 5 = 20.

### Need more help?
Point to 12, 3, 6 and 9 and rehearse 00, 15, 30 and 45 before other numbers.
**A mistake to avoid:** Reading the number 4 on the minute hand as four minutes.

### Stretch yourself
Compare 3:40 p.m. with a 24-hour display of 15:40; explain why they mean the same moment.

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** Read 8:25 and 4:45 on a movable clock and match each to its digital reading.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 3 — Estimating time during the day

**What you will learn:** Choose a sensible time estimate and explain the clue used.

### Understand
To estimate a time is to make a reasonable guess, not give an exact clock reading. Daily routines can help: waking and breakfast often happen in the morning; lunch often happens near midday. Outdoors, the Sun's position changes the length and direction of shadows. At one particular place, shadows are often shorter around midday than earlier or later, but clouds, season and location make shadows unsuitable for reading an exact time. Check your estimate against a clock. Never look straight at the Sun. Estimate using what you can safely observe.

An estimate is a reasonable approximation, not an exact clock reading. Daily routines and safely observed shadows give clues but vary by place, season and weather.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A learner sees that classes are about to stop for lunch near the middle of the school day. Is 1:00 a.m. or 1:00 p.m. a better estimate?

1. Lunch usually takes place during the daytime after morning learning.
2. 1:00 a.m. is at night, long before morning school.
3. 1:00 p.m. is a sensible estimate; the exact time depends on the school.

### A second way to think
Compare a morning and midday observation from the same safe location and check against a clock.

### Explore
**Materials:** A safe outdoor spot, paper, pencil and clock if available
1. Choose a building or tree whose shadow you can see safely without climbing or touching hazards.
2. Record whether its shadow looks long or short during morning and later in the day.
3. Write an estimated time period and check using a clock; discuss why the shadow cannot give an exact hour.
**What your work should show:** Two observations with time-period estimates and a comparison with a clock

### Practise together
- **Question:** A child is getting ready for school at 7:00. Which is likelier, a.m. or p.m.? **Answer:** a.m. **Why:** Getting ready for morning school is commonly before noon.
- **Question:** Would a shadow alone prove that the time is exactly 12:15? **Answer:** no **Why:** Shadows give clues, not precise minutes.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. A family eats supper at about 6:30. Which label is sensible?
2. A clock shows 10:00 a.m. Does this fall before or after midday?
3. Give one reason an estimate using a shadow may be inaccurate.

### Check your learning
1. Which is a better guess for school lunch, 12:30 a.m. or 12:30 p.m.?
2. Is an estimate the same as an exact measurement?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **A family eats supper at about 6:30. Which label is sensible?** p.m.. Supper is an evening event in this example.
- **A clock shows 10:00 a.m. Does this fall before or after midday?** before midday. a.m. means before midday.
- **Give one reason an estimate using a shadow may be inaccurate.** Clouds or different seasons can change the visible shadow.. Many factors besides clock time affect an observed shadow.
- **Which is a better guess for school lunch, 12:30 a.m. or 12:30 p.m.?** 12:30 p.m.. Lunch is a daytime event.
- **Is an estimate the same as an exact measurement?** no. Estimation is an informed approximation.

### Need more help?
Match picture or word cards for waking, lunch and supper to morning, afternoon or evening.
**A mistake to avoid:** Claiming an exact minute from a shadow.

### Stretch yourself
Explain why two schools may have lunch at different times yet both call it lunchtime.

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** Compare a morning and midday observation from the same safe location and check against a clock.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 4 — Changing hours and minutes

**What you will learn:** Convert between hours and minutes, including a whole hour and extra minutes.

### Understand
One hour contains 60 minutes. To change hours to minutes, multiply the number of hours by 60. To change minutes to hours, divide by 60. If the division leaves extra minutes, record them after the complete hours. For example, 90 minutes is one full hour and 30 extra minutes. For very short tasks we may count minutes; for a long lesson or journey we may use hours and minutes together.

Each complete hour contains sixty minutes. Group minutes into sixties and record fewer than sixty extra minutes.

### First worked example
**Problem:** Change 2 hours 25 minutes into minutes.

1. Two hours = 2 × 60 = 120 minutes.
2. Add the extra 25 minutes: 120 + 25 = 145.
3. Answer: 145 minutes.

### A second way to think
Convert 3 h 15 min = 195 min; reverse 185 min = 3 h 5 min. Check both directions.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper, pencil and a 60-space number strip if helpful
1. Draw a strip representing 60 minutes.
2. Make two copies of the strip to show two hours.
3. Add a strip of 15 more minutes; explain why that makes 135 minutes.
**What your work should show:** A labelled model for 2 hours 15 minutes = 135 minutes

### Practise together
- **Question:** How many minutes are in 3 hours? **Answer:** 180 minutes **Why:** 3 × 60 = 180.
- **Question:** How long is 75 minutes in hours and minutes? **Answer:** 1 hour 15 minutes **Why:** 75 − 60 = 15 minutes after one hour.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. Change 4 hours to minutes.
2. Change 120 minutes to hours.
3. Change 185 minutes into hours and minutes.

### Check your learning
1. How many minutes are in 1 hour 40 minutes?
2. Is 90 minutes equal to 1 hour 90 minutes?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **Change 4 hours to minutes.** 240 minutes. 4 × 60 = 240.
- **Change 120 minutes to hours.** 2 hours. 120 ÷ 60 = 2.
- **Change 185 minutes into hours and minutes.** 3 hours 5 minutes. 3 × 60 = 180; 185 − 180 = 5.
- **How many minutes are in 1 hour 40 minutes?** 100 minutes. 60 + 40 = 100.
- **Is 90 minutes equal to 1 hour 90 minutes?** no. After 60 minutes, count one hour; 90 = 1 hour 30 minutes.

### Need more help?
Use 60 counters or six groups of ten to represent each complete hour.
**A mistake to avoid:** Treating an hour as one hundred minutes.

### Stretch yourself
A bus trip lasts 245 minutes. Express this as hours and minutes and explain the remainder (4 hours 5 minutes).

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** Convert 3 h 15 min = 195 min; reverse 185 min = 3 h 5 min. Check both directions.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 5 — Hours, days and weeks

**What you will learn:** Convert between hours and days and between days and weeks.

### Understand
One day contains 24 hours. One week contains 7 days. The rules are different from hours and minutes: multiply by 24 to change days to hours, and by 7 to change weeks to days. Divide by the same number to work backwards. For example, 48 hours is 2 days because 48 ÷ 24 = 2. A school week may have five school days, but a full calendar week still has seven days.

One day contains twenty-four hours and one calendar week contains seven days; a five-day school week is not a full calendar week.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A community project takes 3 weeks and 2 extra days. How many days is that?

1. 3 weeks = 3 × 7 = 21 days.
2. Add 2 days: 21 + 2 = 23.
3. Answer: 23 days.

### A second way to think
Convert 2 days 6 hours to 54 hours and 17 days to two weeks three days.

### Explore
**Materials:** Any calendar or a hand-drawn seven-day strip
1. Label a strip Monday through Sunday.
2. Draw two strips and count all days: 7 + 7 = 14.
3. Mark five school days on one strip and explain why the week is still seven days.
**What your work should show:** Two labelled seven-day strips and an explanation

### Practise together
- **Question:** How many hours are in 3 days? **Answer:** 72 hours **Why:** 3 × 24 = 72.
- **Question:** How many weeks are in 28 days? **Answer:** 4 weeks **Why:** 28 ÷ 7 = 4.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. Change 5 days into hours.
2. Change 96 hours into days.
3. Change 6 weeks into days.

### Check your learning
1. How many days are in 2 weeks and 1 day?
2. Are 24 days the same as 24 hours?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **Change 5 days into hours.** 120 hours. 5 × 24 = 120.
- **Change 96 hours into days.** 4 days. 96 ÷ 24 = 4.
- **Change 6 weeks into days.** 42 days. 6 × 7 = 42.
- **How many days are in 2 weeks and 1 day?** 15 days. 14 + 1 = 15.
- **Are 24 days the same as 24 hours?** no. 24 hours are one day; 24 days are much longer.

### Need more help?
Count 7, 14, 21, 28 using week strips; count days to hours as groups of 24.
**A mistake to avoid:** Mistaking five common school days for a seven-day week.

### Stretch yourself
Express 17 days as weeks and days (2 weeks 3 days).

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** Convert 2 days 6 hours to 54 hours and 17 days to two weeks three days.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 6 — Measuring and recording duration

**What you will learn:** Record the start and finish of an activity and say how long it lasted.

### Understand
Duration tells us how long something lasts. A start time and a finish time are clock readings. Duration is the difference between them. Write start and finish clearly, including a.m. or p.m. where needed. If a reading passes an hour, count to the next full hour first and then count on. For example, from 9:40 to 10:00 is 20 minutes, and from 10:00 to 10:15 is another 15 minutes: the whole activity lasted 35 minutes. A stopwatch can measure short tasks; a clock is useful for longer tasks.

Clock time marks when an event happens, whereas duration measures how long it lasts. Record start and finish separately.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A pupil starts drawing at 2:25 p.m. and finishes at 3:05 p.m. Find the duration.

1. 2:25 p.m. to 3:00 p.m. = 35 minutes.
2. 3:00 p.m. to 3:05 p.m. = 5 minutes.
3. Total = 35 + 5 = 40 minutes.

### A second way to think
From 9:40 to 10:15 count twenty minutes to ten and fifteen more: 35 minutes.

### Explore
**Materials:** A clock or phone clock used with permission, pencil and notebook
1. Agree on a safe two-minute quiet reading activity.
2. Write the start time; carry out the activity; write the finish time.
3. Calculate the duration and compare with a partner's record. If there is no clock, use a teacher-supplied pair of times.
**What your work should show:** Table with activity, start, finish and calculated duration

### Practise together
- **Question:** An activity runs 10:10 to 10:35. How long? **Answer:** 25 minutes **Why:** 35 − 10 = 25.
- **Question:** A class runs 11:45 a.m. to 12:15 p.m. How long? **Answer:** 30 minutes **Why:** 15 minutes to noon and 15 minutes after noon.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. Reading begins 8:50 a.m. and ends 9:20 a.m. Find duration.
2. A game starts 1:05 p.m. and ends 1:50 p.m. Find duration.
3. Why should we record both start and finish?

### Check your learning
1. A lesson starts 10:55 and ends 11:10. How long is it?
2. Does duration require an a.m. or p.m. label?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **Reading begins 8:50 a.m. and ends 9:20 a.m. Find duration.** 30 minutes. 10 minutes to nine plus 20.
- **A game starts 1:05 p.m. and ends 1:50 p.m. Find duration.** 45 minutes. 50 − 5 = 45.
- **Why should we record both start and finish?** To calculate how long an activity lasted.. One clock reading alone does not show duration.
- **A lesson starts 10:55 and ends 11:10. How long is it?** 15 minutes. 5 minutes to eleven and 10 after.
- **Does duration require an a.m. or p.m. label?** no. A duration is a length such as 30 minutes, not a clock time.

### Need more help?
Mark 9:50, 10:00 and 10:10 on a paper time line; count two 10-minute parts.
**A mistake to avoid:** Subtracting minute digits across an hour without a bridge.

### Stretch yourself
Plan a 45-minute activity that starts at 3:35 p.m. and find its end (4:20 p.m.).

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** From 9:40 to 10:15 count twenty minutes to ten and fifteen more: 35 minutes.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 7 — Finding elapsed time across an hour

**What you will learn:** Find elapsed time by counting forward from start to finish.

### Understand
When events cross an hour, breaking the calculation into pieces helps. Find the minutes from the starting time to the next full hour, then the minutes from that hour to the finish. Add the pieces. If an event lasts more than one hour, count full hours too. When a lesson starts at 9:35 and ends at 10:20, the calculation is 25 minutes to 10:00 plus 20 minutes to 10:20, giving 45 minutes. Always ask whether the answer is sensible.

Find elapsed time by drawing labelled jumps to the next full hour, through whole hours, and then to the finish.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A journey starts at 8:45 a.m. and ends at 10:10 a.m. How long is the journey?

1. 8:45 to 9:00 = 15 minutes.
2. 9:00 to 10:00 = 1 hour.
3. 10:00 to 10:10 = 10 minutes.
4. Total = 1 hour and 25 minutes.

### A second way to think
8:45 to 10:10 is 15 + 60 + 10 = 85 minutes, or 1 hour 25 minutes.

### Explore
**Materials:** Ruler, pencil and paper
1. Draw a straight line with labels 1:50, 2:00, 2:20.
2. Write +10 minutes on the first section and +20 on the second.
3. Read the full duration: 30 minutes. Try another line of your own.
**What your work should show:** A timeline with correctly added intervals

### Practise together
- **Question:** 9:35 to 10:20: find duration. **Answer:** 45 minutes **Why:** 25 minutes to ten and 20 after.
- **Question:** 2:50 p.m. to 4:05 p.m.: find duration. **Answer:** 1 hour 15 minutes **Why:** 10 + 60 + 5 = 75 minutes.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. 7:55 a.m. to 8:25 a.m.: how long?
2. 11:40 a.m. to 1:10 p.m.: how long?
3. 3:20 p.m. to 4:00 p.m.: how long?

### Check your learning
1. 9:50 to 10:15: how long?
2. 2:15 to 3:45: how long?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **7:55 a.m. to 8:25 a.m.: how long?** 30 minutes. 5 + 25 = 30.
- **11:40 a.m. to 1:10 p.m.: how long?** 1 hour 30 minutes. 20 + 60 + 10 = 90 minutes.
- **3:20 p.m. to 4:00 p.m.: how long?** 40 minutes. 60 − 20 = 40.
- **9:50 to 10:15: how long?** 25 minutes. 10 + 15 = 25.
- **2:15 to 3:45: how long?** 1 hour 30 minutes. 45 minutes to three plus 45 to 3:45.

### Need more help?
Use clocks set to the next hour and count in 5-minute jumps.
**A mistake to avoid:** Omitting a full hour in the middle.

### Stretch yourself
A sports event lasts 2 hours 20 minutes and ends at 5:05 p.m. Find its start (2:45 p.m.).

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** 8:45 to 10:10 is 15 + 60 + 10 = 85 minutes, or 1 hour 25 minutes.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 8 — Adding hours and minutes

**What you will learn:** Add two durations and exchange 60 minutes for one hour when needed.

### Understand
When we add durations, add the hours to hours and minutes to minutes. If the total minutes reach 60 or more, exchange every 60 minutes for one extra hour. This is like regrouping in addition, but a time hour has 60 minutes, not 100. For example, 1 hour 40 minutes plus 50 minutes is 1 hour 90 minutes before regrouping; 90 minutes is 1 hour 30 minutes, so the correct total is 2 hours 30 minutes. Do not confuse adding a length of time with finding a clock reading.

Add hours to hours, minutes to minutes, then exchange each complete sixty-minute group into an extra hour.

### First worked example
**Problem:** Add 2 hours 35 minutes and 1 hour 50 minutes.

1. Hours: 2 + 1 = 3 hours.
2. Minutes: 35 + 50 = 85 minutes.
3. 85 minutes = 1 hour 25 minutes.
4. Total = 4 hours 25 minutes.

### A second way to think
2 h 35 min + 1 h 50 min gives 3 h 85 min = 4 h 25 min; check by changing to total minutes.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper cards marked 1 hour, 10 minutes and 5 minutes
1. Build 1 hour 45 minutes with cards.
2. Build another 35 minutes.
3. Group 60 minutes into an hour card; record the new total.
**What your work should show:** Card model and the equation 1 h 45 min + 35 min = 2 h 20 min

### Practise together
- **Question:** 1 hour 20 minutes + 35 minutes = ? **Answer:** 1 hour 55 minutes **Why:** 20 + 35 = 55; no regrouping.
- **Question:** 2 hours 50 minutes + 25 minutes = ? **Answer:** 3 hours 15 minutes **Why:** 50 + 25 = 75 = 1 hour 15 minutes.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. 1 hour 45 minutes + 30 minutes.
2. 2 hours 15 minutes + 1 hour 25 minutes.
3. 3 hours 55 minutes + 20 minutes.

### Check your learning
1. 45 minutes + 35 minutes = ?
2. Is 2 hours 80 minutes written in normal hours/minutes form?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **1 hour 45 minutes + 30 minutes.** 2 hours 15 minutes. 75 minutes = 1 hour 15 minutes.
- **2 hours 15 minutes + 1 hour 25 minutes.** 3 hours 40 minutes. 15 + 25 = 40 minutes.
- **3 hours 55 minutes + 20 minutes.** 4 hours 15 minutes. 55 + 20 = 75 minutes.
- **45 minutes + 35 minutes = ?** 1 hour 20 minutes. 80 minutes is 60 + 20.
- **Is 2 hours 80 minutes written in normal hours/minutes form?** no. Exchange 60 minutes: 3 hours 20 minutes.

### Need more help?
Use 6 ten-minute cards as one hour before adding two durations.
**A mistake to avoid:** Leaving eighty or more minutes in a simplified answer.

### Stretch yourself
A journey has three legs of 50 minutes, 1 hour 35 minutes and 45 minutes. Total 3 hours 10 minutes.

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** 2 h 35 min + 1 h 50 min gives 3 h 85 min = 4 h 25 min; check by changing to total minutes.
**Adaptation:** Use an easier prerequisite model when needed and a novel applied question for a learner ready for challenge.

## Lesson 9 — Subtracting time durations

**What you will learn:** Subtract durations, exchanging one hour for 60 minutes when necessary.

### Understand
Subtraction can tell us how much time remains or how much longer one activity takes than another. Write hours under hours and minutes under minutes. If the top number of minutes is smaller, exchange one whole hour for 60 minutes. For example, 3 hours 10 minutes is the same as 2 hours 70 minutes. Then you can subtract 1 hour 45 minutes: 2 hours 70 minutes minus 1 hour 45 minutes is 1 hour 25 minutes. You can check by adding the difference back.

If the minuend has too few minutes, exchange one of its hours for sixty minutes, subtract and verify by addition.

### First worked example
**Problem:** Subtract 1 hour 45 minutes from 4 hours 20 minutes.

1. 20 minutes is less than 45, so exchange 1 hour.
2. 4 hours 20 minutes becomes 3 hours 80 minutes.
3. Subtract minutes: 80 − 45 = 35.
4. Subtract hours: 3 − 1 = 2. Answer: 2 hours 35 minutes.

### A second way to think
4 h 20 min − 1 h 45 min becomes 3 h 80 min − 1 h 45 min = 2 h 35 min.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper cards marked hour and ten-minute parts
1. Show 2 hours 10 minutes with cards.
2. Exchange one hour for six ten-minute cards: this becomes 1 hour 70 minutes.
3. Take away 45 minutes and discuss what is left: 1 hour 25 minutes.
**What your work should show:** A correct regrouping model

### Practise together
- **Question:** 3 hours 50 minutes − 1 hour 20 minutes = ? **Answer:** 2 hours 30 minutes **Why:** 50 − 20 = 30; 3 − 1 = 2.
- **Question:** 2 hours 5 minutes − 45 minutes = ? **Answer:** 1 hour 20 minutes **Why:** Exchange: 2 h 5 min = 1 h 65 min.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. 5 hours 15 minutes − 2 hours 40 minutes.
2. 3 hours 30 minutes − 1 hour 55 minutes.
3. 2 hours − 35 minutes.

### Check your learning
1. 4 hours 10 minutes − 1 hour 30 minutes = ?
2. Check 2 h 35 min + 1 h 45 min = ?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **5 hours 15 minutes − 2 hours 40 minutes.** 2 hours 35 minutes. 4 h 75 min − 2 h 40 min = 2 h 35 min.
- **3 hours 30 minutes − 1 hour 55 minutes.** 1 hour 35 minutes. 2 h 90 min − 1 h 55 min = 1 h 35 min.
- **2 hours − 35 minutes.** 1 hour 25 minutes. 1 h 60 min − 35 min.
- **4 hours 10 minutes − 1 hour 30 minutes = ?** 2 hours 40 minutes. 3 h 70 min − 1 h 30 min.
- **Check 2 h 35 min + 1 h 45 min = ?** 4 hours 20 minutes. 35 + 45 = 80 min, or 1 h 20 min.

### Need more help?
First subtract times with no exchange, then use a drawn 60-minute hour box to exchange.
**A mistake to avoid:** Borrowing ten minutes instead of sixty.

### Stretch yourself
A community hall is booked for 6 h 15 min; cleaning needs 1 h 50 min. Find available activity time (4 h 25 min).

### Teacher notes
Begin with a prerequisite check, give a concrete demonstration, lead guided practice, then assess independent reasoning. Learners may respond orally, in writing or using drawings or counters.
**Demonstration:** 4 h 20 min − 1 h 45 min becomes 3 h 80 min − 1 h 45 min = 2 h 35 min.

## Lesson 10 — Digital schedules, revision and time responsibility

**What you will learn:** Use digital times to plan a day, calculate duration and check sensible answers.

### Understand
Digital clocks help us record daily events clearly. We can use a simple schedule to see which event starts first and how long activities last. Write both the time and whether it is a.m. or p.m. when using the 12-hour system. For a timetable, check the order: the finishing time must usually be later than the start unless the event crosses midnight. Add a duration to a start time to work out the finish. Accurate time records help us keep appointments, reach school or a clinic when expected and give others reliable information.

A schedule separates clock starts, durations and clock finishes. Calculate end times before assessing overlaps. 14:30 means 2:30 p.m.

### First worked example
**Problem:** A reading club begins at 3:35 p.m. and lasts 50 minutes. When does it finish?

1. 3:35 to 4:00 = 25 minutes.
2. 50 − 25 = 25 minutes still to go.
3. Finish at 4:25 p.m.

### A second way to think
Plan three activities with start, duration and finish; verify each event fits.

### Explore
**Materials:** Paper and pencil; optional digital clock
1. Make a three-event afternoon plan: 2:00 p.m. for 30 min, 2:45 p.m. for 40 min, and 4:10 p.m. for 20 min.
2. Calculate the finish times for each event.
3. Explain whether any events overlap; adjust your plan if they do.
**What your work should show:** A correct three-row timetable and explanation

### Practise together
- **Question:** A meeting starts at 10:15 a.m. and lasts 45 minutes. Finish? **Answer:** 11:00 a.m. **Why:** 15 + 45 = 60, so one hour later.
- **Question:** 3 weeks are how many days? **Answer:** 21 days **Why:** 3 × 7 = 21.

### Exercise — try on your own
1. 07:40 a.m. to 08:15 a.m.: duration?
2. Convert 2 hours 30 minutes to minutes.
3. A bus leaves at 1:50 p.m. and travels 1 h 25 min. When does it arrive?
4. Explain one reason a correct time record is useful.

### Check your learning
1. 2 hours 40 minutes + 35 minutes = ?
2. 5 hours 10 minutes − 2 hours 35 minutes = ?
3. Should we rely on a shadow to measure exactly 10:27 a.m.?

### Answers and helpful feedback
- **07:40 a.m. to 08:15 a.m.: duration?** 35 minutes. 20 min to 8:00 plus 15.
- **Convert 2 hours 30 minutes to minutes.** 150 minutes. 2 × 60 + 30.
- **A bus leaves at 1:50 p.m. and travels 1 h 25 min. When does it arrive?** 3:15 p.m.. 10 min to 2:00, then 1 h 15 min further.
- **Explain one reason a correct time record is useful.** It helps others arrive or plan at the right time.. Practical planning needs accurate timekeeping.
- **2 hours 40 minutes + 35 minutes = ?** 3 hours 15 minutes. 40 + 35 = 75 minutes.
- **5 hours 10 minutes − 2 hours 35 minutes = ?** 2 hours 35 minutes. 4 h 70 min − 2 h 35 min.
- **Should we rely on a shadow to measure exactly 10:27 a.m.?** no. A shadow only supports a rough time estimate.

### Need more help?
Retry three cards in order: choose a.m./p.m., convert an hour, find duration across one hour. Correct each with its worked example.
**A mistake to avoid:** Confusing a duration with a finishing time.
