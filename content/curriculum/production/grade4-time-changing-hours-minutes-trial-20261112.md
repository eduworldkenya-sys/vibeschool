## Learner lesson

### Learning goal
Change hours into minutes and minutes into hours and minutes; show your working and explain any extra minutes.

### Recall what you know
One hour contains **60 minutes**. A minute is shorter than an hour. We use minutes for shorter activities and hours and minutes for longer ones.

### A model you can draw
Imagine **two equal long bars** representing 60 minutes each, followed by a **shorter bar** representing 35 minutes. Each long bar is one complete hour. The bars show **60 + 60 + 35 = 155 minutes**. The short bar is drawn proportionally at 35/60 of a long bar.

### Worked example 1 — Change hours and minutes into minutes
**Question:** Change 2 hours 35 minutes into minutes.

1. Each hour contains 60 minutes, so two hours contain **2 × 60 = 120 minutes**.
2. Add the extra 35 minutes: **120 + 35 = 155 minutes**.
3. Check by grouping the answer as **60 + 60 + 35**.

**Answer: 155 minutes.**

### Worked example 2 — Change minutes into hours and minutes
**Question:** Change 158 minutes into hours and minutes.

1. Find complete groups of sixty: **60 + 60 = 120 minutes**. That is **2 hours**.
2. Find the minutes left over: **158 − 120 = 38 minutes**.
3. Check: **2 × 60 + 38 = 158**.

**Answer: 2 hours 38 minutes.** The extra minutes must be fewer than 60.

### Practise together
1. How many minutes are in 3 hours? **180 minutes**, because 3 × 60 = 180.
2. Express 75 minutes in hours and minutes. **1 hour 15 minutes**, because 75 − 60 = 15.

### Try independently
1. Change 2 hours 5 minutes into minutes.
2. Change 200 minutes into hours and minutes.
3. Explain why 1 hour 75 minutes is not written in its simplest form.
4. Explain how to check whether 3 hours 20 minutes is 200 minutes.

### Check and correct
1. **125 minutes** (2 × 60 + 5).
2. **3 hours 20 minutes** (3 × 60 = 180, with 20 minutes remaining).
3. Convert the 75 minutes to 1 hour 15 minutes; total **2 hours 15 minutes**.
4. **3 × 60 + 20 = 200 minutes**, so the statement is correct.

**If it is difficult:** Draw 60-minute bars or use six groups of ten counters for every hour. Start with one hour, then try two hours with extra minutes.

**Challenge:** A journey lasts 415 minutes. Show complete hours and extra minutes. **6 hours 55 minutes**.

## Teacher Lesson Notes

### Goal and evidence of learning
Learners convert hours to minutes and minutes to hours, with a remainder of fewer than 60 minutes. They show a correct model, explain the grouping and check their answers independently.

### Preparation
Paper, pencil, the proportional 60 + 60 + 35 minute bar diagram or locally available counters. No printer or specialist clock is required.

### Opening and prior knowledge — approximately 3 minutes
Ask: “How many minutes make an hour?” Expected: **60**. If uncertain, count six groups of ten. Ask whether 100 minutes is one hour; expected: **No, it is 1 hour 40 minutes**.

### Explain and demonstrate — approximately 10 minutes
Write **1 hour = 60 minutes**. Draw two equal 60-minute bars, then a shorter 35-minute bar. Write **2 × 60 + 35 = 155 minutes** and explain that each full hour is another group of sixty. Reverse the conversion with **158 minutes = 2 × 60 + 38 = 2 hours 38 minutes**. Show the inverse check.

### Questions and expected answers — approximately 4 minutes
- “Why multiply by sixty?” Every hour contains sixty minutes.
- “Can the extra minutes in simplest form be 75?” No; count another complete hour.
- “Is 3 hours 25 minutes equal to 325 minutes?” No. **3 × 60 + 25 = 205 minutes**.

### Guided and individual work — approximately 8 minutes
Do 3 hours to minutes with counters, then ask learners to attempt the four independent questions from the learner lesson. Observe reasoning rather than only final answers. Learners may explain orally or show counters if handwriting is a barrier.

### Formative check — approximately 3 minutes
Change **2 hours 5 minutes** into minutes. Correct answer: **125 minutes**; expected working: **2 × 60 + 5**. An answer of 205 suggests treating an hour as 100 minutes.

### Misconceptions and correction
- **Hour treated as 100 minutes:** show six groups of ten; count to sixty.
- **Remainder of 60 or more:** group another complete hour.
- **Extra minutes forgotten:** label full-hour bars and the remaining bar separately.
- **Answer guessed:** reverse the conversion to check.

### Support, enrichment and follow-up — approximately 2 minutes
Use one-hour examples and visual models where needed; challenge confident learners with 415 minutes, explaining the remainder. For optional home learning, ask the child to explain the total minutes in a familiar activity. Do not require an adult to teach or use a paid device.

2. Which is longer, 3 hours 10 minutes or 180 minutes, and by how much? **3 hours 10 minutes, by 10 minutes**.
