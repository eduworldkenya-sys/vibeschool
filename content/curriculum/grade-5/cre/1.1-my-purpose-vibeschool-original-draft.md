## Lesson 1 — Discovering My Gifts

**We are learning to:** recognise our abilities, consider what gives our lives purpose, and name practical ways to develop our gifts.  
**Outcome focus:** `G5-CRE-1.1-a`, `G5-CRE-1.1-d`.

At break time, Akinyi sees her friends preparing for the school open day. One pupil enjoys singing. Another repairs a torn classroom display. A third helps younger learners practise their reading. Akinyi says, “I am not good at any of those things.” Her teacher asks her who notices when a classmate is upset. The others point to Akinyi. She often listens carefully and helps friends solve small disagreements.

People have different gifts and abilities. A **talent** is a strength that may come naturally and grows through use. An **ability** is something we can learn or do. We may notice an ability in sport, art, music, caring, organising, fixing things or solving problems. Nobody is expected to have every strength, and we should not laugh at another person's efforts.

Christians believe that each person has value and a purpose in God's creation. Read **Jeremiah 29:11** and discuss why hope for the future can encourage us to use our time well. Knowing our gifts is a beginning, not an excuse to boast. We become more useful when we practise and when we learn from other people.

**Think about it:** How can a quiet pupil who likes listening contribute to the class?

**Try this:** Fold a page into three columns: *What I enjoy*, *What I can do now*, and *What I want to practise*. Write or draw two examples in each column. Choose one skill to practise this week. Ask a trusted adult or teacher for appropriate support.

**Check your understanding**
1. Explain the difference between a talent and an ability in your own words.
2. Describe one way a learner might discover a gift without winning a competition.
3. How could you encourage a classmate who is still learning a skill?

## Lesson 2 — The Parable of the Talents

**We are learning to:** tell the Bible story accurately and explain what responsibility teaches us about our gifts.  
**Outcome focus:** `G5-CRE-1.1-b`, `G5-CRE-1.1-c`.

A parable is a story Jesus used to help people understand an important lesson. In **Matthew 25:14–30**, Jesus tells of a man who goes on a journey. Before leaving, he entrusts different amounts of money to three servants, according to their abilities.

The first servant receives five talents of money and makes five more. The second receives two and makes another two. The third receives one but hides it in the ground. When the man returns, the first two show what they have done with what was entrusted to them. Their master praises their faithfulness. The third servant returns only the talent he had buried, and the master challenges his choice.

In this Bible story, a **talent** is a unit of money. Today we also use the word *talent* for a person's special ability. The lesson for our lives is not that every pupil should earn money. It is about using opportunities responsibly instead of wasting them through fear or carelessness. Our abilities can improve when we practise them and use them to do good.

Consider two learners who both enjoy drawing. One practises making posters to help younger pupils follow class rules. The other hides every drawing because it may not be perfect. What practical encouragement might help the second learner try again?

**Retell:** Describe the story in three stages: what was entrusted, what each servant did, and what happened when the master returned.

**Discuss:** Does having fewer abilities make a person less valuable? Give a reason based on the message of responsible use, not comparison with others.

**Check your understanding**
1. Name the three amounts entrusted to the servants.
2. What did the third servant do with the money?
3. State two lessons a Grade 5 learner can apply from the parable.
4. Give one age-appropriate example of using a gift to help someone else.

## Lesson 3 — Putting My Gifts to Work

**We are learning to:** grow a chosen skill and use it in service to others.  
**Outcome focus:** `G5-CRE-1.1-c`, `G5-CRE-1.1-d`.

Imagine a school garden after several dry days. A group of pupils wants to help. Some carry small watering cans. One learner keeps a simple record of the plants. Another reminds the group which seedlings need shade. The project succeeds because they share the work and listen to one another.

The Christian teaching in **1 Peter 4:10** encourages believers to use the gifts they have received to serve other people. **James 1:17** directs attention to God as the giver of good gifts. These readings invite us to be grateful and responsible. A gift grows when it is practised with patience, honesty and care.

Learning takes time. A pupil may make mistakes while practising a song or reading aloud. Mistakes are opportunities to improve, not reasons to give up. Ask for feedback. Set a small goal. Practise safely and regularly. Then use the skill where it is genuinely helpful.

**A weekly plan:** Choose one ability. Write what you will practise, when you will practise it, who can support you, and one way the skill could benefit another person. At the end of the week, reflect on what changed.

**Think and respond**
1. Why should someone continue practising a skill after making a mistake?
2. Give two ways a learner can use a gift responsibly at school or at home.
3. How can gratitude and service change the way we use our abilities?

## Remember

People have different gifts, talents and abilities. Christians believe these are worth developing and using responsibly. The parable of the talents challenges us to be faithful with what we have, while service reminds us to use our gifts for more than personal praise.

## Topic practice — draft item prompts (NOT YET in the scored bank)

1. **Recall:** In Matthew 25:14–30, what did the third servant do with the talent he received?
2. **Understanding:** Explain why the word *talent* is used differently in the parable and when we speak about a learner's abilities.
3. **Application:** A pupil is good at reading stories but is shy. Suggest a safe, realistic way to grow that ability and share it.
4. **Reflection:** Describe one strength you wish to develop and one small action you can take this week.

## Optional teacher preparation — not a publication requirement

- **Before reading:** Invite learners to share strengths without ranking or embarrassing one another. Do not require disclosure of private family information.
- **Lesson 1:** Use learners' ordinary school experiences, not a competition, to recognise different abilities. Refer to Jeremiah 29:11.
- **Lesson 2:** Read Matthew 25:14–30 from an authorised classroom Bible, distinguish the historical monetary meaning of *talent*, and check that the story is retold faithfully.
- **Lesson 3:** Use a simple school-based example of service. Let learners choose a safe, age-appropriate skill to practise.
- **Support:** Offer drawing, oral answers or paired discussion for learners who struggle with longer written responses.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to compare responsibility in the parable with service in 1 Peter 4:10.
- **Evidence:** Look for clear retelling, an explanation of stewardship, and a realistic weekly action rather than a performance ranking.
