**Description for readers**

Taraji is good at making decisions. Living with them is another matter.

She has chosen to pursue her CPA qualification without attending a conventional college. She wants freedom, but freedom comes with bills, difficult questions, distracting friends and nobody to remind her when she's falling behind.

Taraji is outgoing, ambitious and bold enough to try things her own way. She's also wonderfully capable of getting herself into trouble.

Between work, family expectations, unexpected friendships and the realities of independent study, she discovers that choosing your own path doesn't mean walking it alone.

*Your Way Forward* is a story about finding your people, trusting your judgment, facing the consequences of your choices and building a future that belongs to you.

**Description for readers**

Nobody takes attendance when you study on your own. Nobody decides when you begin, when you ask for help or how you arrange the rest of your life. That freedom can be exciting. It can also be difficult.

This short, practical companion helps you think about your reasons for pursuing CPA, what the official qualification requires, how to make learning visible, how to organise your own time and how to find people who understand the journey.

It does not offer a perfect timetable or a shortcut to qualification. It helps you begin with the life you actually have.

## Story canon

- **Taraji** is the protagonist, replacing the superseded working name Jean. She is outgoing, bold, disciplined in intention but often distracted, sometimes confused and willing to change course. She owns the consequences of her choices; she never wins merely because she is unconventional.
- Taraji prepares for CPA independently in Kenya. The plot contrasts learning routes without implying that formal college is bad or that professional examination standards can be ignored.
- **Asha** is her close friend attending physical college; their paths deserve equal respect.
- **Nuru** works at a salon and studies CPA; she brings practical insight and becomes a friend.
- **Biko** works at a pharmacy and studies alongside work; careful, quiet, perceptive.
- **Kelvin** is initially competitive, later revealed to be thoughtful and capable of explanation.
- **Omondi** combines work, fatherhood and CPA study. **Salma** and **Imani** represent newcomers to the group.
- **Grace**, Taraji's mother, runs a tailoring business and offers grounded counsel; **Lenny** is Taraji's younger brother; **Mr Otieno** runs Kavu Print.
- **Tuesday Table** is the group's name. It is fictional, not a claim that a named real-life VibeSchool feature has launched.
- Book One ends with Taraji passing **one** CPA paper, not qualifying as a certified accountant. Her wider journey remains open.
- The books are original fiction and guidance, **not** KASNEB material or a substitute for official registration, examination and qualification requirements.

## Editorial principles

Write scenes before lessons. Prefer believable action, particular detail, differentiated dialogue, humour, awkward silences and earned outcomes over generic exhortation. Give each scene tension or change. Respect the reader's intelligence. Do not imply one study schedule, personality or learning route fits everyone. Never promise guaranteed success or disguise promotional copy as a scene.

The reader is free to learn independently **and** free to use teachers, classes, books and peers. Independence is ownership of choices, not isolation.
