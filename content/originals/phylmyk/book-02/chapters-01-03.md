# Phylmyk and the Dantez Files
## Book Two: The Secret of Kwa Kariz
**VibeSchool Originals · Gema Mysteries · Text-only edition**

*In Gema, everybody knows the garage. Nobody knows what is hidden inside it.*

---

## Chapter One: The Boy Who Knew Too Much

Yobra Mninja had a problem.

A very serious problem.

His little sister had discovered something she was never supposed to find.

And now she was sitting on his bed, staring at him as if he were a criminal.

"Start talking," Phylmyk said.

Yobra continued folding his shirt.

"Talking about what?"

"The road."

"What road?"

"The old road."

"Gema has many roads."

"The one you told me never to visit!"

Yobra folded another shirt.

Phylmyk stood up.

"Yobra!"

"What?"

"If you fold one more shirt, I'm arresting you."

He looked at her.

"With what authority?"

"Brotherly obstruction of justice."

"That's not a crime."

"It will be when I'm president."

Yobra almost smiled.

Almost.

But when Phylmyk held up the photograph of the five black-helmeted motorcycle riders, his expression changed.

He sat down.

"Where did you get that picture?"

"Lucanji found it inside a book at the Reading Festival."

"Who gave him the book?"

"We don't know."

Yobra held out his hand.

"Give it to me."

"No."

"Phylmyk."

"Tell me what you know."

For a long moment, her brother stared at the photograph.

Then he said something that made her stomach tighten.

"Those people weren't always bad."

Phylmyk blinked.

"What do you mean?"

"The Shadow Riderz."

"You know them?"

"I know what they used to be."

"Then who are they?"

Yobra looked toward the window.

Outside, Gema was settling into the evening.

The last matatus were leaving the stage. A man pushed a wheelbarrow full of empty crates past their gate. Somewhere in the distance, a dog barked at something nobody could see.

Yobra lowered his voice.

"Four years ago, there was a terrible storm."

Phylmyk remembered the storm.

She had been eight.

It had rained for three days.

The stream near Tamtam had flooded, the bridge had become impassable, and some families had been cut off from the town.

"Five volunteers from Gema used motorcycles to help carry supplies along the safe roads," Yobra continued. "Books, food, medicine, whatever the community needed."

"The Shadow Riderz?"

He nodded.

"That's what some people called them."

Phylmyk looked at the photograph again.

"But they were helping people?"

"Yes."

"So why is Dantez involved with them?"

"I don't know."

"Why did somebody send me this photograph?"

"I don't know."

"And why does the message say you know the road?"

Yobra rubbed his face.

"Because I helped them."

Phylmyk froze.

"You were a Shadow Rider?"

"No! I was twelve. They were adults. I helped record supplies and carry messages at Kwa Kariz. Once, when the floodwater dropped, I went with one of the volunteers to an old checkpoint."

"On the old road?"

He nodded.

"And what happened there?"

Yobra opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

"Some things went missing," he said.

"What things?"

"Things that belonged to the schools."

"Books?"

"Not only books."

"Then what?"

Yobra stood.

"That's enough for tonight."

Phylmyk couldn't believe it.

"Enough? You've just told me you were involved in a secret motorcycle operation!"

"It wasn't secret."

"Then why didn't you tell me?"

"Because something happened afterward. People were blamed. Friendships ended."

"Whose friendships?"

Yobra looked at the photograph one more time.

"Mine, for one."

He walked toward the door.

"Listen to me carefully, Phylmyk. Whatever happens next, don't go looking for that road."

"Why?"

"Because somebody is trying to make people remember things they'd rather forget."

He switched off the bedroom light.

And left.

Phylmyk sat in the darkness.

Her mind was racing.

The old road.

Five riders.

Missing school supplies.

Dantez.

Yobra.

She reached for her detective notebook.

On a fresh page, she wrote:

**CASE 002: THE SECRET OF KWA KARIZ**

Then she added:

*Suspect Number One: My own brother.*

She paused.

Crossed out *suspect*.

Wrote:

*Person who needs to explain himself.*

"Better," she whispered.

At that exact moment, something struck the window.

*Tak!*

Phylmyk jumped.

A second sound followed.

*Tak! Tak!*

She approached the curtain carefully.

Outside, someone was standing beneath the mango tree.

A small figure.

Wearing a red cap.

"Phylmyk!" the figure whispered.

She opened the window.

"Lucanji?"

"Shhh!"

"What are you doing outside my house?"

Lucanji held up a packet of biscuits.

"Emergency."

"What emergency?"

"Somebody came to Wakibe."

"Who?"

"A man with a silver helmet."

Phylmyk's heart began to race.

"What did he want?"

"He asked Wadani about Kwa Kariz."

"And?"

Lucanji looked behind him.

Then he whispered:

"He said the garage would be finished tomorrow."

Phylmyk frowned.

"Finished how?"

Lucanji pulled a folded paper from his pocket.

"Wadani told me to give this to Yobra."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because you're the detective."

She took the paper.

Written across it in large red letters were six words.

**KWA KARIZ WILL CLOSE AT FOUR.**

Below was the black motorcycle symbol.

Phylmyk stared.

Tomorrow.

Four o'clock.

The garage.

She looked up at Lucanji.

"Did anybody see you come here?"

"No."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

A motorcycle engine sounded at the end of the street.

Lucanji stopped smiling.

The engine idled for a moment.

Then slowly moved away.

Phylmyk shut the window.

For once, she did not feel like making a joke.

Someone knew about her investigation.

Someone knew about Yobra.

And someone wanted Kwa Kariz closed.

## Chapter Two: The Day the Engines Went Silent

The next morning, something was terribly wrong at Kwa Kariz.

There was no hammering.

No shouting.

No engines coughing like sick goats.

No mechanics arguing over spanners.

Not even the radio was playing.

Phylmyk stood outside the garage with Yobra, Mdavido, Lucanji and Vanny Boy.

A large paper notice had been fixed to the gate.

**NOTICE OF INSPECTION**

**STORAGE AREA UNDER INVESTIGATION**

**4:00 PM**

Below it, in thick red handwriting, somebody had added:

**GARAGE CLOSING TODAY!**

Mdavido whistled.

"Even the cars are afraid to talk."

Lucanji pressed his face against the gate.

"Where's Labonator?"

"Probably hugging his blue car goodbye," Mdavido said.

Inside, several mechanics were sitting on empty oil drums.

A man in a grey overall stood beside the office door, holding a ring of keys.

His hands were covered in old grease.

His name was Kariz.

Everybody called the garage **Kwa Kariz** because it was his place.

He had been repairing vehicles in Gema for longer than most people could remember.

"Morning, Mzee Kariz," Yobra called.

Kariz looked up.

"Yobra."

His expression changed when he noticed Phylmyk.

"And you've brought the detective."

Phylmyk straightened her coat.

"Good morning."

Kariz studied her cap.

"Are you investigating my garage or looking for a place to park your hat?"

"Possibly both."

For the first time that morning, he chuckled.

Yobra handed him the paper Lucanji had delivered.

Kariz read it.

His smile vanished.

"Where did this come from?"

"Wakibe."

Kariz cursed under his breath.

Then he pointed at the notice on the gate.

"An officer is coming at four to inspect our storage records. That part is true. But nobody has ordered this garage closed."

"So the red writing is fake?" Phylmyk asked.

"Yes. Someone wants people to believe the worst."

"Why is your storage area being investigated?"

Kariz hesitated.

Then he opened the gate.

"Come inside."

The children followed him into the front office.

On the wall hung photographs of old cars, football teams and mechanics who had worked at Kwa Kariz over the years.

In the corner was an ancient metal filing cabinet.

Kariz pulled out a chair.

"Four years ago, when Tamtam was cut off by the floods, this garage helped organise deliveries."

Yobra looked down.

Phylmyk noticed.

Kariz continued.

"There were five volunteer motorcycle couriers. We had three main routes."

Vanny Boy suddenly raised his head.

"Three routes and five riders?"

"Exactly."

Vanny adjusted his glasses.

"A highly suggestive numerical association."

Mdavido frowned.

"Oh, no. His brain has started."

Kariz opened a drawer and removed a faded photograph.

Five motorcycles stood outside Kwa Kariz.

Behind them was a painted sign.

**3 ROUTES. 5 RIDERS. ONE GEMA.**

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"Thirty-five."

Kariz nodded.

"Some of the volunteers called the project Three-Five."

Phylmyk thought of Dantez's computer system.

*Guardian Grid 35.*

The number on the mysterious stickers.

The receipt showing a time thirty-five minutes ahead.

Maybe Dantez had borrowed more than a symbol from the old riders.

"Why is somebody investigating the garage?" she asked.

Kariz pointed toward a locked storeroom.

"Because a set of donated learning lamps and electrical equipment disappeared after the flood."

"How much equipment?"

"Eighteen boxes."

"Eighteen?"

"Solar reading lamps, charging units, and other materials meant for schools in Tamtam."

"That's a lot of money," Mdavido said.

"It was a lot of hope," Kariz replied.

For a moment, the office became very quiet.

"The children were meant to use the lamps for evening reading," Kariz continued. "But the supplies never reached their final destination."

"What happened to them?"

"That's exactly what nobody could prove."

"Why investigate now?"

"Someone submitted a complaint saying we still have the missing equipment hidden here."

"Do you?"

Kariz looked directly at Phylmyk.

"No."

"Can you prove what happened?"

He tapped the filing cabinet.

"Not without the original delivery ledger."

"Where is it?"

"Missing."

Phylmyk glanced at Yobra.

His face had gone pale.

Kariz noticed too.

"Yobra," he said quietly. "You remember that ledger, don't you?"

Yobra swallowed.

"Yes."

"Then perhaps it's time you told your sister everything."

Before Yobra could answer, a crash sounded from the far end of the garage.

*BANG!*

Everyone jumped.

A mechanic shouted.

"THE BLUE CAR!"

They ran outside.

Labonator's beloved blue car had rolled several metres from where it had been parked.

A mechanic rushed over and secured it.

No one was hurt.

Labonator came running from the back office.

"My car! My beautiful car!"

Mdavido stared.

"Not again."

Phylmyk wasn't looking at the car.

She was looking at the storeroom.

Its door was open.

Someone had taken advantage of the confusion.

And someone had been inside.

## Chapter Three: The Walking Dictionary Strikes Again

"Nobody touch anything!" Phylmyk shouted.

A mechanic stopped halfway through picking up a spanner.

Another froze with a cup of tea in his hand.

Labonator looked horrified.

"Even my car?"

"Your car is fine."

"Fine? It nearly rolled into the road!"

"Then make sure it's secured properly."

Kariz inspected the storeroom doorway.

The padlock was hanging loose.

"Someone opened this," he said.

"Who has keys?" Yobra asked.

"Three people. Me, the senior mechanic and the storekeeper."

"Where are they?"

"All here."

Phylmyk examined the lock without touching it.

There were no obvious signs of damage.

"Maybe somebody left it unlocked," she said.

"Or somebody has another key," Yobra replied.

Vanny Boy crouched nearby.

He studied the dusty floor.

Then the lock.

Then the doorframe.

Finally, he spoke.

"The absence of overt destructive deformation suggests a non-forcible method of ingress, although the evidentiary basis remains insufficient to distinguish authorised access from unauthorised key replication."

Silence.

Mdavido slowly turned toward him.

"Vanny."

"Yes?"

"Did you swallow a dictionary when you were born?"

"That hypothesis is anatomically implausible."

Lucanji giggled.

"He said no."

"Thank you, Lucanji. Very helpful."

Phylmyk bent closer.

Something had caught her attention.

A small blue thread was hanging from a nail near the door.

She pointed it out to Kariz, who placed it carefully into an envelope for the inspector.

"Blue cloth," she murmured.

Labonator looked down at his football jersey.

It was blue.

Mdavido looked at him.

Labonator looked at Mdavido.

"Don't even think about it," Labonator said.

Phylmyk shook her head.

"We're not accusing anyone based on a colour."

Mdavido smiled.

"Look at you. Learning."

Inside the storeroom, nothing appeared obviously missing.

There were shelves of tools, spare parts, tyres and old mechanical equipment.

But Kariz knew something was wrong.

He pointed to a dusty rectangle on one shelf.

"A blue metal case used to stand there."

"What's inside it?" Phylmyk asked.

"An old workshop inventory book. Some photographs. Records from the flood."

Yobra closed his eyes.

"That case."

Kariz turned.

"You remember it?"

"Yes."

Phylmyk folded her arms.

"Why does everyone know about things except me?"

Yobra sighed.

"Because you were eight."

"I'm twelve now."

"That doesn't mean you have to investigate every danger in Gema."

"No. But it means you should stop treating me like I can't understand."

He looked at her.

For a moment, he had no answer.

Then a voice sounded from the garage entrance.

"Ah! The famous detective club."

Dantez.

He stood just outside the gate, wearing a black jacket and carrying a motorcycle helmet.

Labonator hurried toward him.

"Where have you been?"

"Busy."

"Busy doing what?"

"Things."

"Which things?"

"Complicated things."

Mdavido leaned toward Vanny.

"Congratulations. You've found your cousin."

Dantez ignored him.

His eyes moved from the open storeroom to Phylmyk.

Then to Kariz.

"I heard there's trouble."

"Funny," Phylmyk replied. "Trouble often starts shortly before you arrive."

Dantez smiled.

"Careful, Detective. That sounds like an accusation."

"Just an observation."

"Observations can be wrong."

"So can computer geniuses."

Labonator stepped between them.

"Can we please go one day without the two of you fighting?"

Dantez glanced at the storeroom.

"Did they find the case?"

Nobody spoke.

Phylmyk narrowed her eyes.

"What case?"

Dantez hesitated.

Too late.

"The blue one," she said.

He shrugged.

"Everybody knows the old garage has cases."

"No," Kariz said firmly. "Not everybody."

Dantez looked at the garage owner.

Then he smiled.

"Maybe I've been here longer than you think."

He turned toward the street.

Phylmyk called after him.

"Dantez!"

He stopped.

"Who are the Shadow Riderz?"

For a moment, everything seemed to stop.

Even the mechanics looked up.

Dantez turned slowly.

"That's an interesting question."

"Answer it."

He pointed at Yobra.

"Ask your brother."

Then he walked away.

Yobra stared at the ground.

Phylmyk looked at him.

She was beginning to think that the most mysterious person in Gema wasn't Dantez after all.

It was Yobra Mninja.
