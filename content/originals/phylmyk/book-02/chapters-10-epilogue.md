## Chapter Ten: The Man in the Silver Helmet

The safety officer stepped forward.

"Good afternoon. Can I help you?"

The man stood still.

"I'm here for the records."

"What records?"

"The ones you've just taken."

Kariz folded his arms.

"Who are you?"

The man hesitated.

Then removed his helmet.

He was older than Dantez.

Much older.

A narrow scar ran down one cheek.

His eyes moved from Kariz to the notebook.

Then to Yobra.

"You," he said.

Yobra swallowed.

The man smiled.

"You're the little boy from the checkpoint."

"I'm not little anymore."

"No. I suppose not."

The officer asked for the man's identification.

He produced an old company card.

**SILVERLINE LOGISTICS**

**TRANSPORT SUPERVISOR**

**M. JARO**

Kariz stared at him.

"Jaro."

The man nodded.

"Kariz."

"After four years, you come back here?"

"I came for company records."

"Company records? Those supplies belonged to schools!"

Jaro's jaw tightened.

"You're misunderstanding."

The officer intervened.

"Mr Jaro, these documents are relevant to an ongoing complaint. They will need to be examined properly."

Jaro looked at the notebook.

"That boy's notes aren't official records."

"Then why do you want them?" Kariz demanded.

Jaro said nothing.

Yobra felt his fear returning.

The same man.

The same sharp voice.

The same feeling that he was about to be blamed for something.

Then he remembered Phylmyk.

*You should have told someone.*

He took a breath.

"Four years ago, you told me not to mention the delivery."

Jaro turned.

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"You said everybody would blame me."

"You're confused."

Yobra shook his head.

"No."

Kariz watched silently.

Yobra continued.

"I remember your van. I remember the number. I remember you asking for the schedule."

Jaro's face changed.

"I was doing my job."

"Then why did you frighten a twelve-year-old boy?"

The words seemed to hang in the air.

For the first time, Jaro had no immediate answer.

The officer took his details and asked him to remain available for the investigation.

By then, another officer had arrived.

The records were secured.

Jaro was questioned about the missing blue case, the threatening messages and the changes in vehicle identification.

He denied stealing anything.

But he could not explain why he had tried to retrieve the old records without involving the garage owner.

Nor could he explain the messages that had been sent to Wadani.

The officers said the matter required further investigation.

Kariz did not argue.

He was simply relieved that the notebook had been found.

Yobra stood beside him.

"Do you think we'll find the lamps?"

Kariz looked toward the hills.

"I hope so."

The officer closed his evidence bag.

"There's something else you should know."

"What?"

"We checked the original company records before coming here. Silverline Logistics no longer operates under that name."

Kariz frowned.

"What happened to it?"

"It was closed and its remaining assets were taken over by another business."

"Which business?"

The officer hesitated.

"One registered in Sandona."

Yobra looked up.

"Sandona?"

"Yes."

"What's it called?"

The officer checked his notes.

"Tomorrow Holdings."

Kariz stared.

"Tomorrow?"

The officer nodded.

Yobra remembered the strange stickers.

The messages.

The motorcycle symbol.

And the words Dantez had received.

**WE OWN TOMORROW.**

Suddenly, the name sounded much less like a slogan.

And much more like a warning.

## Chapter Eleven: A Very Important Apology

At exactly four o'clock, Kwa Kariz was full of people.

Mechanics.

Customers.

The inspection officer.

Wadani.

Phylmyk.

Yobra.

Mdavido.

Lucanji.

Vanny Boy.

Amani.

And Labonator, who was standing protectively beside his blue car.

The inspector reviewed the garage's documents and the records recovered from Checkpoint Seven.

There were still questions about the old shipment.

But the evidence showed that Kwa Kariz had handed the boxes to the contracted transport service.

The inspector found no basis to close the entire garage that afternoon.

Further inquiries would continue.

Kariz sat down heavily.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he whispered:

"Thank goodness."

Labonator lifted his football into the air.

"KWAAAA KARIZ!"

Mdavido began clapping.

A mechanic sounded the garage bell.

*CLANG!*

The other mechanics cheered.

Kariz jumped up.

"WHO KEEPS RINGING THAT THING?"

Everyone laughed.

Even the inspection officer smiled.

Wadani approached with a tray of tea and mandazi.

"Somebody needs to feed these detectives before they investigate their own stomachs."

Lucanji took two mandazi.

Phylmyk stared at him.

"Lucanji!"

"What?"

"Those are for everybody."

"I know."

"Then why have you taken two?"

"One is for me."

"And the other?"

"My future self."

Mdavido laughed so hard he nearly dropped his tea.

Phylmyk shook her head.

"This town is impossible."

She turned toward Yobra.

"Can we talk?"

He followed her to a quiet corner.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Phylmyk took out her notebook.

She opened the first page of Case 002.

**Person who needs to explain himself: Yobra Mninja.**

She crossed it out.

Yobra smiled.

"What are you doing?"

"Updating my investigation."

She wrote:

**YOBRA MNINJA — IMPORTANT WITNESS. VERY ANNOYING BROTHER.**

He laughed.

"Very annoying?"

"Extremely."

"You're welcome."

She looked up.

"I'm sorry."

He stopped smiling.

"For what?"

"For thinking you might be one of the bad guys."

Yobra leaned against the wall.

"You weren't completely wrong to ask questions."

"But I should have listened."

"And I should have told you the truth."

He held out his fist.

"Partners?"

Phylmyk bumped it.

"Partners."

From the other side of the garage came a shout.

"PHYLMYK!"

Mdavido was waving.

"Come here! We've got something!"

She hurried over.

Vanny Boy had arranged the old photographs on a table.

Beside them lay a copy of the recovered notebook's delivery records.

"The available documentary corpus indicates a significant anomaly within the original route classification system," Vanny announced.

Mdavido looked at Phylmyk.

"He found something."

"What?"

Vanny pointed at the photographs.

"Five riders. Three routes."

"We know," Phylmyk said.

"But the final photographic record contains an additional participant whose identity is not documented in the official project register."

Phylmyk frowned.

"Someone else?"

Vanny nodded.

"The original group consisted of five riders. Yet in this photograph, six helmets are visible."

Everyone leaned closer.

The photograph showed the five volunteers outside Kwa Kariz.

Five motorcycles.

Five riders.

And, half-hidden behind the open door of a white van—

A sixth helmet.

Black.

With a thin silver stripe.

Lucanji pointed.

"That one."

Amani frowned.

"Who is that?"

Kariz examined the photograph.

His face became serious.

"I don't remember a sixth rider."

Yobra leaned forward.

"Neither do I."

Phylmyk looked at the helmet.

Then at the old map.

Then at the names in the notebook.

Something didn't make sense.

"If there were only five riders," she whispered, "who was the sixth?"

Vanny Boy looked directly at her.

For once, he did not use complicated words.

"I don't know."

Mdavido blinked.

"Now I'm worried."

## Chapter Twelve: The Last Message

That evening, Gema was alive again.

Kwa Kariz's doors were open.

Mechanics worked beneath the bright workshop lights.

The radio played.

Customers came and went.

Labonator carefully parked his blue car where it could not roll away.

Wadani's shop was busy.

Children returned books to the reading corner.

At Sandona, Amani was telling her friends about the old volunteer riders.

At Bonde, Mama Sia had placed a new sign outside her bookstall.

**BOOKS FOR EVERY CHILD.**

And at home, Phylmyk sat at her desk.

Her notebook was open.

She had drawn a large circle in the middle of the page.

Inside it were two words.

**SHADOW RIDERZ**

Around the circle she wrote everything she knew.

Dantez.

The black motorcycle symbol.

Guardian Grid 35.

The missing schoolbooks.

The missing lamps.

Silverline Logistics.

Tomorrow Holdings.

The old road.

The sixth helmet.

And Yobra's notebook.

She stared at the page.

They had solved part of the mystery.

Kwa Kariz had been protected from an unjust closure.

The lost delivery records had been recovered.

Yobra had finally told the truth.

But the eighteen boxes of learning equipment were still missing.

And somebody else was using the old riders' symbol.

Someone who seemed to know much more about Gema than Phylmyk did.

She heard a knock.

Yobra entered.

"Still investigating?"

"Yes."

"You should sleep."

"Detectives don't sleep."

"Then explain why you were snoring in Mathematics yesterday."

"I was conducting a controlled experiment on unconscious concentration."

Yobra laughed.

He sat on the bed.

"Listen. I wanted to tell you something."

Phylmyk closed her notebook.

"What?"

"I'm proud of you."

She looked surprised.

"Really?"

"Yes."

"Even though I accused you?"

"Even then."

He smiled.

"You asked questions. You found evidence. And when you were wrong, you admitted it."

Phylmyk thought about Mdavido.

Then Dantez.

Then her brother.

"Being a detective is harder than I thought."

Yobra nodded.

"Knowing what happened is one thing. Understanding why people did it is another."

Phylmyk smiled.

"That was almost intelligent."

"Almost?"

"Don't become proud."

He stood.

"Goodnight, Detective."

"Goodnight, Mninja."

After he left, Phylmyk opened her notebook again.

At the bottom of the page, she wrote:

*The best clue is not always the one you find.*

*Sometimes it's the one somebody is afraid to tell you.*

She closed the notebook.

Then her phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

One message.

**WELL DONE, PHYLMYK.**

She stared.

A second message arrived.

**YOU FOUND THE FIRST RECORD.**

Then a third.

**BUT THE SIXTH RIDER WAS NEVER PART OF THE FIVE.**

Phylmyk sat upright.

Another message appeared.

**IF YOU WANT THE TRUTH, ASK DANTEZ ABOUT THE BLACK HELMET.**

She reached for her notebook.

Before she could write anything, one final message arrived.

**AND TELL YOBRA THAT SOMEONE FROM THE OLD DAYS IS COMING HOME.**

Phylmyk looked toward the door.

Yobra.

The old road.

The missing lamps.

The sixth rider.

Whoever was sending these messages knew far too much.

She saved them to show Yobra and the safety officer in the morning.

Then she wrote one last line.

**CASE 003 — WHO IS THE SIXTH RIDER?**

Outside, somewhere beyond Gema, a motorcycle engine roared into the night.

## Epilogue: The Shadow Riderz

Dantez stood alone at Kwa Kariz.

The garage had closed for the night.

He was sitting beside an old motorbike, turning a small black helmet over in his hands.

Labonator approached.

"I thought you went home."

Dantez didn't answer.

Labonator sat beside him.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Labonator pointed at the helmet.

"Where did you get that?"

Dantez looked at it.

"Someone left it for me."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Labonator sighed.

"You have got to stop collecting mysterious things."

Dantez smiled slightly.

"You're sounding like Phylmyk."

"Good. Maybe she'll teach me some sense."

Dantez turned the helmet over.

On the back was the familiar symbol.

A motorcycle rider inside a circle.

Underneath were the words:

**THE SHADOW RIDERZ**

But inside the helmet was a different message.

A small white label.

Dantez read it aloud.

"Five riders. Three roads. One secret."

Labonator frowned.

"That's the old Three-Five project."

"Not exactly."

"What do you mean?"

Dantez held the helmet toward him.

There was another line beneath the first.

**THE SIXTH RIDER NEVER LEFT.**

Labonator stared.

"Never left where?"

Dantez looked toward the dark hills beyond Gema.

"I think we've been asking the wrong question."

"Which question?"

"Everybody wants to know who the Shadow Riderz are."

"Yes."

"But maybe we should be asking why they came back."

Labonator felt a chill.

"Dantez."

"What?"

"Don't get involved."

Dantez smiled.

A familiar smile.

The one that usually meant trouble.

"Too late."

He placed the helmet on the workbench.

His phone vibrated.

A new message appeared.

**YOUR FIRST ASSIGNMENT IS READY.**

Below it was a photograph.

Not of Phylmyk.

Not of Yobra.

Not of Kwa Kariz.

It showed a building in Sandona.

A large glass building with an expensive-looking entrance.

On the gate was a sign.

**TOMORROW HOLDINGS**

Dantez stared at the photograph.

Then another message arrived.

**THEY STOLE MORE THAN EIGHTEEN BOXES.**

A third followed.

**THE ANSWERS ARE INSIDE.**

Labonator shook his head.

"No. Absolutely not."

Dantez looked at him.

"What?"

"We're not doing another stupid plan."

Dantez raised an eyebrow.

"We?"

Labonator picked up his football.

"Someone has to stop you from making a fool of yourself."

Dantez grinned.

"Then perhaps you should come along."

"Come where?"

"To find out who's sending these messages. Properly this time."

Labonator considered.

Then shook his head.

"If we're going to investigate, we tell Kariz and the safety officer what we know. No breaking into buildings, no stolen computers, no disappearing schoolbooks."

Dantez laughed.

"You've changed."

"Yes."

"You're becoming boring."

"I'm becoming intelligent."

Dantez glanced toward the garage.

"That's debatable."

Labonator threw his football at him.

Dantez caught it.

For a moment, they both laughed.

Then the lights in the garage flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Dantez's phone lit up again.

**WELCOME BACK, RIDER 06.**

He stopped laughing.

"Rider Six?"

Labonator looked at him.

"Who's Rider Six?"

Dantez stared at the helmet.

He had never joined the original riders.

He hadn't even been old enough.

But somebody had just welcomed him back.

And that could mean only one thing.

Whoever controlled the Shadow Riderz wasn't simply watching Gema.

They had been planning something for years.

Outside the garage, the wind lifted dust along the empty road.

Somewhere in the distance, a motorcycle passed beneath a broken streetlight.

Then another.

And another.

Three engines.

Moving together.

Toward Sandona.

Dantez watched their headlights disappear.

A slow smile spread across his face.

"This," he whispered, "is getting interesting."
