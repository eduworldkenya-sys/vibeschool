## Chapter Four: The Rich Kids of Sandona

Mdavido had a theory.

It wasn't a particularly good theory.

But he was extremely proud of it.

"The missing blue case was stolen by a millionaire."

Phylmyk glanced at him.

"Evidence?"

"Sandona."

"That's a place."

"A place full of millionaires."

"Being rich doesn't make someone a thief."

"Fine. A millionaire with suspicious behaviour."

"Which millionaire?"

Mdavido paused.

"I haven't reached that part of my theory."

Lucanji laughed so hard he nearly dropped his biscuits.

They were walking toward Sandona to meet Amani, who had offered to help them find old photographs from the Three-Five project.

Amani was waiting outside a café, wearing spotless white trainers.

She looked at Mdavido's dusty shoes.

"Did you walk through a quarry?"

"Did you pay school fees for those shoes?"

"Very funny."

"Thank you."

Phylmyk interrupted before the argument could develop.

"Did you find the photographs?"

Amani nodded.

"My mother helped organise donations during the floods. She kept copies of the old festival programmes and community newsletters."

"Can we see them?"

"Yes. But first, one thing."

"What?"

Amani looked at Mdavido.

"Last time we met, you said people in Sandona think they're better than everyone else."

"Some do."

"Some people in Bonde think we're all spoiled."

"Some are."

"Do you ever stop talking?"

"Only when I'm reading."

Amani rolled her eyes.

"Good. Let's find you a book."

She led them into the Sandona Community Library.

The building was beautiful.

Large windows.

Soft chairs.

Shelves of books.

Colourful posters.

Mdavido stopped at the entrance.

For a moment, he forgot to be funny.

"Wow."

Amani smiled.

"You like it?"

He nodded.

"How much does it cost to borrow a book?"

"Nothing. It's a community library."

Mdavido looked around again.

"Nothing?"

"Nothing."

He turned to Phylmyk.

"Detective, we may have found the greatest place in Gema."

"Focus, Mdavido."

"After we solve the case, I'm moving into this library."

They gathered around a table.

Amani opened an old folder.

Inside were photographs from the flood-relief operation.

Five motorcycle riders.

Boxes of reading lamps.

Volunteers from Langa, Shabe and Tamtam.

Wadani standing beside a table of supplies.

Kariz holding a clipboard.

And a younger Yobra, grinning beside a red lantern.

Phylmyk stared.

"That's you."

Yobra said nothing.

"You were really there."

"Yes."

Amani turned the page.

There was a picture of a white delivery van.

On its door was a small silver triangle.

Vanny leaned forward.

"That emblem appears to represent a distinct commercial transport identifier."

Mdavido sighed.

"A company logo."

"Precisely."

Amani pointed at the text beneath the photograph.

**School Relief Delivery — Transport Partner: Silverline Logistics**

Phylmyk frowned.

"Silverline?"

"Yes," Amani said. "They helped transport supplies during the floods."

"What happened to the company?"

"I'm not sure."

She searched through the folder.

Then found a newspaper cutting.

The headline read:

**RELIEF SUPPLIES GO MISSING — TRANSPORT DISPUTE IN GEMA**

Below it was a photograph of the old road.

Phylmyk recognised it immediately.

It was the road marked with the red X.

She picked up the cutting.

Something had been written on the back.

In blue ink.

Three short lines:

*The five remember.*

*The third route knows.*

*Find the place where the bell rings twice.*

Phylmyk looked at Yobra.

His expression changed.

"What is it?" she asked.

He took the paper.

Read it.

And whispered:

"Kwa Kariz."

"What about Kwa Kariz?"

Yobra looked up.

"The old warning bell."

He swallowed.

"Somebody knows where we hid the records."

## Chapter Five: The Bell That Rang Twice

At Kwa Kariz, the mechanics were beginning to get nervous.

The inspection officer was expected at four.

And it was already one o'clock.

Kariz was standing near the garage entrance, arguing with a man who wanted his vehicle repaired.

"How can you tell me the garage is closed?" the customer demanded.

"It isn't closed!"

"Then why aren't you working?"

"Because someone has stolen my records!"

"Are you saying somebody stole my engine too?"

Kariz stared at him.

"Your engine is inside your car."

"Then why isn't my car moving?"

"Because you haven't repaired it!"

The customer looked offended.

Phylmyk decided this was not a good moment to interrupt.

She waited until Kariz had finished arguing.

"Mzee Kariz, we found something."

He examined the newspaper cutting.

When he read the words *Find the place where the bell rings twice*, his expression changed.

He walked to the back of the garage.

"Follow me."

They passed piles of tyres, an old bus engine and a mechanic sleeping beneath the shade of a lorry.

At the rear stood a little corrugated-iron shelter.

Inside hung an old metal bell.

It was rusty.

Its rope was frayed.

And beneath it was a wooden board bearing faded writing.

**THREE ROUTES. FIVE RIDERS.**

Kariz touched the bell.

"This was our warning bell during the floods."

"Why did it ring twice?" Phylmyk asked.

"One ring meant a delivery had arrived. Two rings meant the road was unsafe and the riders needed to return."

Vanny Boy nodded.

"A rudimentary yet functionally effective acoustic communication protocol."

Mdavido looked at Lucanji.

"Did you understand that?"

Lucanji shook his head.

"I only understood acoustic."

"What's acoustic?"

"I don't know. But it sounds important."

Kariz continued.

"We had another bell at the old checkpoint on the third route."

Yobra suddenly looked up.

"The one near the hill?"

"Yes."

"The bell beside the abandoned rest shelter?"

Kariz nodded.

"That one."

Phylmyk stared at her brother.

"You remember exactly where it is."

Yobra said nothing.

Kariz pulled the rope.

*CLANG!*

The sound echoed across the garage.

A sleeping mechanic jumped so violently that his cap fell into an empty bucket.

"WHO HAS DIED?" he shouted.

Everyone burst out laughing.

Everyone except Vanny.

He examined the wooden board.

"Interesting."

Phylmyk leaned closer.

"What?"

"The painted surface exhibits multiple layers, suggesting an earlier inscription beneath the currently visible lettering."

Mdavido sighed.

"He says somebody painted over something."

Kariz fetched a cloth and wiped dust from the board.

Near the bottom, faded letters became visible.

**ROUTE 3 — TAMTAM RIDGE**

And beneath them:

**CHECKPOINT SEVEN**

Yobra went completely still.

Kariz looked at him.

"You remember Checkpoint Seven?"

Yobra swallowed.

"Yes."

Phylmyk stepped closer.

"What's at Checkpoint Seven?"

Yobra looked toward the garage entrance.

"It's where everything went wrong."

A voice interrupted them.

"You're getting close."

Everyone turned.

Dantez stood beside a stack of tyres.

He had been listening.

Phylmyk narrowed her eyes.

"How long have you been there?"

"Long enough."

"Then perhaps you'd like to tell us what you know."

Dantez smiled.

"Perhaps."

"Perhaps what?"

"Perhaps you should stop investigating things that happened before you knew how to spell detective."

Mdavido laughed.

"Even I have to admit that was a good one."

Phylmyk glared at him.

Dantez continued.

"You're looking for an old ledger."

"How do you know?"

"Because everybody is."

"Who is everybody?"

He shrugged.

Phylmyk moved closer.

"Did you steal the blue case?"

"No."

"Do you know who did?"

Dantez hesitated.

Then he said, "Someone who doesn't want the truth coming out."

"About the missing lamps?"

"About much more than lamps."

He turned away.

Phylmyk called after him.

"Dantez!"

He stopped.

"Why are you helping us?"

Without turning, he answered:

"I'm not."

Then he walked away.

Mdavido watched him go.

"That boy has serious problems."

Vanny Boy adjusted his glasses.

"His behavioural presentation suggests an unresolved conflict between self-preservation and a desire for external validation."

Mdavido nodded solemnly.

"Exactly."

Phylmyk stared.

"You understood that?"

"No. But I didn't want him to repeat it."

## Chapter Six: Yobra's Greatest Mistake

They found a quiet place behind Wakibe.

Wadani brought out tea for Yobra and cold water for the others.

She looked at their faces.

"What happened?"

"Nothing," Yobra said.

Wadani put her hands on her hips.

"Yobra Mninja, I have known you since you were shorter than that crate. When you say nothing, it means something."

Yobra looked down.

"Please, Wadani."

She softened.

"Then tell them. Secrets have a way of becoming heavier the longer you carry them."

She returned to the shop.

Phylmyk sat beside her brother.

For once, she did not open her notebook.

Yobra stared at the ground.

"Four years ago," he began, "I thought I was clever."

Mdavido opened his mouth.

"Don't," Phylmyk warned.

Mdavido closed it.

Yobra continued.

"During the floods, I helped Kariz and the volunteers organise supplies. I counted boxes, wrote delivery numbers and recorded which rider was using which route."

"Like a clerk?" Lucanji asked.

"Yes."

"Did they pay you?"

"No."

Lucanji frowned.

"Terrible business."

Yobra laughed quietly.

"One evening, eighteen boxes of school equipment arrived at Kwa Kariz. They were supposed to go to Tamtam."

"Those are the missing boxes," Phylmyk said.

"Yes."

Yobra took a breath.

"The bridge was damaged. The riders couldn't use their normal route, so the supplies were taken to Checkpoint Seven, where a transport company was supposed to collect them."

"Silverline Logistics?"

Yobra nodded.

"Their driver came to the checkpoint. I was there with one of the volunteers."

"What happened?"

"The driver said he needed the delivery schedule. He claimed the company had lost its copy."

"So you gave him one?"

"Yes."

Phylmyk watched her brother carefully.

"The next day, the eighteen boxes were missing."

"Where had they gone?"

"Nobody knew. The company said the boxes had never been handed over. The volunteers said they had."

"And you?"

"I had written down the collection details."

"Then why didn't you tell everyone?"

Yobra looked away.

"Because the driver came to see me."

The others became quiet.

"He knew where I lived. He said I had made a mistake with the records and that if I told people about the collection, everyone would blame me."

Phylmyk felt angry.

Not at Yobra.

At whoever had frightened him.

"I was twelve," Yobra whispered. "I believed him."

"What did you do?"

"I hid my notebook."

"Where?"

"At Checkpoint Seven."

Phylmyk stared at him.

"You hid the evidence?"

"I was scared."

"Why didn't you tell Kariz?"

"I wanted to. Every day I told myself I would."

He rubbed his eyes.

"Then people began arguing. The company blamed the volunteers. Some riders accused each other. The whole project collapsed."

"What about the notebook?"

"I never went back."

"Why?"

Yobra looked at her.

"Because every time I thought about that place, I remembered being frightened. Eventually I convinced myself the notebook wouldn't matter anymore."

He lowered his voice.

"But it does matter."

Phylmyk sat quietly.

She had spent an entire evening imagining that her brother might be involved with villains.

Now she saw something different.

A boy who had been frightened.

A boy who had made a mistake.

And a boy who had carried that mistake for four years.

"Yobra."

He looked at her.

"You should have told someone."

"I know."

"But that man shouldn't have threatened you."

Yobra nodded.

Phylmyk took his hand.

"We can tell the safety officer now."

He looked surprised.

"You're not angry?"

"I'm angry you kept lying to me."

"I didn't lie about everything."

"You said you didn't know anything about the road!"

"That was technically—"

"Don't start speaking like Vanny."

Mdavido laughed.

Even Yobra smiled.

Then Vanny Boy spoke.

"The delayed disclosure of historically significant information is regrettable, but its present evidentiary utility remains potentially substantial."

Everyone looked at him.

"What?" Yobra asked.

Vanny hesitated.

Then, unusually, he tried again.

"Your notebook might still help."

For a moment, nobody said anything.

Mdavido slowly stood.

"Ladies and gentlemen."

He pointed at Vanny.

"HE HAS SPOKEN NORMAL ENGLISH!"

Lucanji began clapping.

Vanny looked deeply uncomfortable.

"Your collective response is disproportionate to the linguistic simplification."

Mdavido sat back down.

"He's back."

Phylmyk laughed.

But her laughter faded when Wadani returned.

She was holding her phone.

"The safety officer wants to see you," she told Yobra.

"Why?"

Wadani's face was serious.

"Someone has reported seeing a silver-helmeted rider near the old road."
