## Chapter Seven: Labonator Must Choose

Labonator was having the worst day of his life.

His blue car had nearly rolled into the road.

His garage was under investigation.

His best friend was behaving like a mysterious criminal.

And he had missed football practice.

That last one hurt most.

He sat on an old tyre, bouncing his football against the garage wall.

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

"Will you stop that?" Kariz shouted.

Labonator caught the ball.

"Sorry."

Five seconds later—

*Thump.*

Kariz emerged from the office.

"LABONATOR!"

"Sorry!"

He tucked the ball beneath his arm.

Then Dantez appeared.

"Come with me."

Labonator looked up.

"Where?"

"Somewhere."

"Can you people stop saying somewhere? Gema has actual names for places."

Dantez ignored the joke.

"I need your help."

Labonator stood.

"What kind of help?"

"The old road."

Labonator lowered his voice.

"No."

Dantez stared.

"What?"

"I said no."

"Since when do you tell me no?"

"Since the last time you made me help move six hundred books."

Dantez looked offended.

"That was a demonstration."

"That was stupidity."

Dantez's eyes narrowed.

"Careful."

Labonator stepped closer.

"No, you be careful. We nearly ruined the Reading Festival. People were angry. Children were crying. And for what?"

"To prove I was right!"

"Sometimes you can be right about your invention and wrong about everything else."

Dantez looked away.

Labonator continued.

"You want people to respect you? Build something useful. Don't create problems just so you can solve them."

For a long moment, Dantez said nothing.

Then he reached into his jacket.

He removed a photograph.

It showed the old Checkpoint Seven.

"Someone sent me this."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Labonator studied the picture.

"What's so special about it?"

Dantez pointed to a small building beside the road.

"The original route checkpoint. The notebook may be hidden there."

"Then give the information to Kariz."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because whoever sent this photograph knows where I live."

Labonator's smile disappeared.

Dantez lowered his voice.

"They've been sending me messages since the competition."

"Threats?"

"Instructions."

"That's worse!"

Dantez looked at him.

"They know about my computers. My projects. Everything."

Labonator felt a chill.

"Have you told anyone?"

"No."

"Then tell someone."

Dantez shook his head.

"I can handle it."

"That's what you said about the books."

Dantez looked toward the garage entrance.

Phylmyk and the others were approaching with the safety officer.

Labonator made a decision.

"Give me the photograph."

"Why?"

"Because somebody needs to do the sensible thing."

Dantez grabbed his arm.

"Labonator—"

"Enough."

The two friends stared at each other.

Then Dantez slowly released him.

Labonator took the photograph.

He walked directly toward Kariz and the safety officer.

Phylmyk noticed.

"What's happening?"

Labonator held out the picture.

"I think this belongs to your investigation."

Kariz examined it.

His expression changed.

"Checkpoint Seven."

Yobra moved closer.

"That's the building."

The safety officer studied the photograph.

"Where did you get it?"

Labonator looked back at Dantez.

For a moment, he hesitated.

Then he spoke.

"Dantez received it from somebody who has been sending him threatening messages."

Dantez's face hardened.

"Labonator!"

"I'm sorry," Labonator replied. "But this has gone too far."

The safety officer turned toward Dantez.

"Is that true?"

Dantez stared at the ground.

Then, reluctantly, he nodded.

The officer's voice softened.

"You should have told someone earlier."

Dantez said nothing.

"Whoever is sending these messages may be trying to frighten you into helping them. You do not have to face that alone."

Dantez looked up.

For the first time, Phylmyk saw something in his expression she hadn't expected.

Relief.

Not much.

Just a little.

Then his face became serious again.

"There's something else," he said.

Everyone turned.

"What?" Phylmyk asked.

Dantez hesitated.

"The person with the silver helmet didn't just steal the blue case."

He looked toward Kariz.

"They copied something from inside it first."

"What?" Kariz demanded.

Dantez swallowed.

"A map."

Phylmyk's heart began to race.

"A map of what?"

Dantez looked at Yobra.

"The old road."

## Chapter Eight: The Boy Who Read the Wrong Page

The safety officer arranged for Kariz, Yobra and two other adults to accompany him to Checkpoint Seven.

The children were told they could help examine the photographs and records at Wakibe while the adults checked whether the old road was safe.

Phylmyk was furious.

"Why can't we go?"

"Because the checkpoint has been abandoned for years," Yobra explained. "And someone may already be there."

"But it's my investigation!"

"It's everybody's investigation now."

Phylmyk folded her arms.

"I hate it when people use sensible arguments against me."

Yobra smiled.

"Then stop making it so easy."

At Wakibe, they spread the photographs across a table.

Mdavido was reading an old Gema community magazine that Amani had found in the Sandona library.

Lucanji ate biscuits.

Vanny Boy examined the delivery records.

And Phylmyk drew arrows between the locations in her notebook.

"Three routes," she said.

She pointed to the map.

"Route One went through Langa."

"Route Two through Shabe," Amani added.

"Route Three went toward Tamtam."

Mdavido looked up.

"Hold on."

"What?"

"Something doesn't make sense."

Phylmyk leaned toward him.

"What?"

"This article."

He showed them a page from the magazine.

The headline read:

**GEMA'S FIVE RIDERS SAVE FLOOD-STRANDED FAMILIES**

Beneath the photograph was a description of the three delivery routes.

Mdavido pointed at a paragraph.

"It says the riders never transported school supplies at night."

"So?" Phylmyk asked.

"So Yobra said the missing boxes were collected in the evening."

Vanny looked up.

"An apparent contradiction between the documented operating protocol and the reported temporal circumstances."

Mdavido stared.

"Yes. That."

Phylmyk read the paragraph again.

It was true.

The volunteers had agreed to stop deliveries before dark because the flood-damaged roads were unsafe.

"Maybe they broke their own rule," Amani suggested.

"Maybe," Phylmyk replied.

Lucanji looked at the newspaper photograph.

"That van has funny writing."

Everyone turned.

"What writing?" Phylmyk asked.

"The white van."

They examined the picture.

The vehicle was parked beside the checkpoint.

Its side carried the Silverline Logistics symbol.

Below it, in small letters, were the words:

**OFFICIAL COLLECTION VEHICLE — UNIT 17**

Mdavido frowned.

"What's wrong with Unit Seventeen?"

"Nothing," Phylmyk said.

Lucanji tapped another photograph.

"Then why is this one Unit Twelve?"

They leaned closer.

A second picture showed what appeared to be the same white van.

Same dent near the rear door.

Same slightly bent side mirror.

Same silver triangle.

But a different unit number.

**UNIT 12**

Phylmyk sat upright.

"The same vehicle has two different numbers."

"Or someone changed the markings," Amani said.

Vanny examined the photographs.

"The matching structural imperfections strongly suggest that the depicted vehicles are identical, notwithstanding the discrepant fleet identifiers."

Mdavido nodded.

"Same van. Different number."

"Precisely."

Phylmyk looked at Lucanji.

"How did you notice that?"

Lucanji shrugged.

"I like looking at cars."

Mdavido grinned.

"Don't tell Labonator. He'll recruit you into his car club."

Lucanji looked excited.

"Do they have biscuits?"

"Probably not."

"Then I'm not joining."

Phylmyk took out her phone and called Yobra.

He answered immediately.

"Phylmyk?"

"We found something."

"What?"

"The van that collected the missing boxes had different numbers in two photographs. Unit Twelve and Unit Seventeen. Same dent, same mirror."

Silence.

Then Yobra spoke.

"Unit Twelve."

His voice sounded strange.

"What about it?"

"That's the number I wrote in my notebook."

Phylmyk felt a cold sensation in her stomach.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"Then somebody changed the van's number afterward."

"Or before," Yobra replied.

She looked at the photographs.

The pieces were beginning to fit together.

Somebody had changed the delivery route.

Somebody had changed the vehicle identification.

Somebody had used a false receipt.

And now somebody was trying to destroy the original records.

"Yobra," she said slowly, "I don't think the eighteen boxes simply went missing."

"What do you think happened?"

"I think somebody took them deliberately."

Before Yobra could answer, there was a sudden noise on his end of the call.

A metallic crash.

Then shouting.

"Yobra?"

No answer.

"Yobra!"

The call disconnected.

Phylmyk stared at her phone.

The others gathered around her.

"What happened?" Mdavido asked.

She looked up.

"Something's wrong at the old road."

## Chapter Nine: Checkpoint Seven

At the old road, Yobra was standing beside a fallen metal sign.

The sign had come loose from the checkpoint shelter when Kariz pushed open the damaged gate.

It had landed with a tremendous crash.

Fortunately, nobody was hurt.

The safety officer was examining the building.

The checkpoint had once been a simple rest shelter where volunteers collected supplies before taking them farther into the hills.

Now the roof was rusting.

Weeds grew through the paving.

A broken bench sat beneath a small window.

And beside the entrance hung an old metal bell.

Kariz looked at it.

"Four years."

Yobra nodded.

"Feels longer."

The officer checked the surrounding area.

"Nobody goes inside until we're sure it's safe."

Kariz agreed.

They examined the outside first.

Near the door, somebody had disturbed the dust and old leaves.

Fresh tyre marks crossed the dirt.

"Someone has been here recently," the officer said.

Yobra pointed toward the hillside.

"Look."

A motorcycle was parked beneath a tree.

Black.

Unmarked.

Empty.

Kariz frowned.

"Whose is that?"

Nobody answered.

The officer called for assistance.

They waited beside their vehicle rather than approaching the motorcycle.

Meanwhile, Yobra studied the old shelter.

He remembered everything.

The rain.

The boxes.

The driver asking for the schedule.

And the fear he had felt afterward.

He remembered hiding his notebook.

But something about the checkpoint looked different.

The old bench had been moved.

He pointed it out to Kariz.

"The bench used to stand underneath the window."

Kariz studied the ground.

"You're right."

Yobra looked around.

Then he saw it.

A small square patch of faded red paint on the wall.

He remembered painting it himself during the relief project.

He had wanted to mark the place where the riders kept their spare records.

"The notebook," he whispered.

"What?"

"I didn't hide it under the bench."

"Where did you hide it?"

Yobra approached the outside wall with Kariz and the officer.

"Behind the old route board."

The board was hanging loose beside the door.

The officer carefully removed it.

Behind it was a shallow, sheltered recess.

Inside sat a small metal biscuit tin.

Yobra almost laughed.

The tin was still there.

Four years later.

Kariz looked at him.

"That?"

Yobra nodded.

"I was twelve."

"Of course you hid your secret documents in a biscuit tin."

The officer examined it.

The lid was stiff, but the tin was dry.

Inside lay a small notebook wrapped in plastic.

The cover had faded.

One corner was bent.

Across the front, in childish handwriting, were the words:

**GEMA DELIVERY RECORDS — YOBRA M.**

Yobra stared at it.

His chest felt tight.

Kariz placed a hand on his shoulder.

"You kept it."

"I was afraid to bring it home."

The officer opened it carefully.

The first pages listed food deliveries.

Then schoolbooks.

Then medicine.

Then the missing shipment.

**18 BOXES — SCHOOL LEARNING LAMPS**

**COLLECTED AT CHECKPOINT SEVEN**

**VEHICLE: SILVERLINE UNIT 12**

**TIME: 6:30 PM**

Below it was a handwritten note:

*Driver says supplies going to Tamtam school.*

And beneath that:

*Driver asked for full route schedule. Gave copy.*

Yobra swallowed.

Kariz read the page.

His expression hardened.

"That's the proof we needed."

The officer turned another page.

There was a vehicle description.

White van.

Dent near rear door.

Bent mirror.

Silver triangle.

And a partial registration number.

Then the officer stopped.

Someone had written a name in the margin.

The name of the person who had signed the collection receipt.

Yobra leaned closer.

He remembered the writing.

He remembered the man.

But he had never known his full name.

The officer studied the page.

"This could help us trace the original shipment."

Kariz let out a long breath.

Then a voice came from behind them.

"Put that back."

Everyone turned.

A man stood near the old gate.

He wore a grey jacket.

Black gloves.

And a silver motorcycle helmet.

Yobra went cold.

He knew that helmet.

He had seen it four years ago.
