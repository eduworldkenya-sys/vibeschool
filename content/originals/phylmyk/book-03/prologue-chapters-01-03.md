# THE SIXTH RIDER
**VibeSchool Originals · The Gema Mysteries · Text-only edition**

*Somebody has been watching Gema for four years. Somebody knows what happened to the missing school supplies. And somebody is using a name that belongs to a person who was never supposed to exist.*

## Prologue: The Person Behind the Glass

At exactly 11:46 p.m., three motorcycles entered Sandona.

They rode past the expensive restaurants, the brightly lit shopping centre and the security guards who usually questioned anyone they didn't recognise.

Nobody stopped them.

The first rider wore a black helmet.

The second wore a silver one.

The third wore no visible markings at all.

They turned into a narrow road behind the largest glass building in Sandona.

**TOMORROW HOLDINGS.**

A gate opened.

The motorcycles disappeared inside.

On the sixth floor, a man was standing before an enormous window, watching the lights of Gema.

He wore an expensive suit.

His desk was spotless.

Not a single paper was out of place.

A woman entered carrying a tablet.

"They've arrived, sir."

"All three?"

"Yes."

"And the boy?"

"Still asking questions."

The man turned.

"Which boy?"

"Dantez."

For the first time, he smiled.

"Ah. Our little computer genius."

"He's becoming difficult to control."

"Intelligent people usually are."

"He has been speaking to Phylmyk."

The smile vanished.

"That detective girl?"

"Yes."

The man walked toward his desk.

"Interesting."

He opened a drawer and removed an old photograph.

Five motorcycle riders stood outside Kwa Kariz.

Behind the open door of a white van was a sixth figure wearing a helmet.

The man studied the photograph.

Then he turned it over.

On the back was a handwritten sentence.

*Never let them find out who counted the boxes.*

He placed the photograph inside a folder.

"Sir," the woman asked, "what should we do about the sixth rider?"

The man looked at her.

Then laughed.

"There was never a sixth rider."

"But the photograph—"

"Photographs can tell people what they want to believe."

He approached the window again.

Below, the three motorcycles were being pushed into an underground parking area.

"Let the children look for a mysterious rider."

He switched off the office lights.

"While they're searching for a person, they'll never think to search for the missing eighteen boxes."

Outside, lightning flashed over Gema.

And somewhere far away, a bell rang twice.

## Chapter One: Six Minutes Past Six

Phylmyk was having the best dream of her life.

She was standing before a huge crowd.

The President was awarding her a golden detective badge.

Cameras flashed.

Reporters shouted her name.

A marching band played.

Then the President leaned toward her and said:

"Detective Phylmyk, there is one final mystery."

She straightened proudly.

"Anything, Your Excellency."

"Who ate the last mandazi?"

Phylmyk opened her mouth.

Before she could answer—

**BANG! BANG! BANG!**

"PHYLMYK!"

She sat upright.

Her detective cap fell from the bed.

"WHO'S THERE?"

"Your brother!"

"What do you want?"

"Breakfast!"

"THAT IS NOT AN EMERGENCY!"

Yobra opened the door.

"There's no sugar."

Phylmyk stared at him.

She looked at the clock.

6:06 a.m.

"Yobra."

"Yes?"

"Do you understand that you have interrupted an important presidential ceremony?"

He looked at her.

"You were dreaming."

"That's beside the point."

"I need you to go to Wakibe."

"Go yourself."

"I'm preparing breakfast."

"You can't prepare breakfast without sugar?"

"I can. It just won't be very sweet."

Phylmyk pulled the blanket over her head.

"Neither is my life."

Yobra laughed.

Five minutes later, she was walking toward Wakibe.

Her coat was buttoned.

Her cap was straight.

Her notebook was in her pocket.

And she was still annoyed.

Gema was unusually quiet.

The early-morning air smelled of wet earth and charcoal smoke. A woman was arranging oranges beside the road. A matatu conductor shouted for passengers going toward Langa.

At Wakibe, Wadani was already opening the shop.

"Good morning, Detective!"

Phylmyk approached the counter.

"Morning."

"Why that face?"

"My brother has committed a serious crime."

Wadani stopped arranging bread.

"What crime?"

"Interrupting a presidential ceremony."

Wadani stared.

"Eh?"

"It was a dream."

Wadani burst out laughing.

"Phylmyk! You will finish me one day."

"One packet of sugar, please."

Wadani reached for the shelf.

Then stopped.

"Before you go, there's something I want you to see."

Phylmyk immediately became serious.

"What?"

Wadani held up an envelope.

It was black.

No stamp.

No sender.

Just one name written across the front.

**PHYLMYK.**

"Who brought it?"

"I don't know."

"When?"

"It was underneath the door when I opened."

Phylmyk took the envelope.

It felt unusually heavy.

Inside was a small card.

On the front was the familiar motorcycle symbol.

On the back were four words.

**SIX RIDERS. FIVE HELMETS.**

Phylmyk frowned.

"That doesn't make sense."

Wadani looked uncomfortable.

"What does it say?"

Phylmyk showed her.

Wadani's hand froze.

For one second, something changed in her expression.

Then she turned away.

"Some children have too much time to waste."

Phylmyk watched her.

"Wadani?"

"Yes?"

"Do you know what this means?"

"No."

The answer came too quickly.

Phylmyk was about to question her when another voice interrupted.

"GOOD MORNING, PEOPLE OF GEMA!"

Mdavido rolled toward the shop on his skateboard.

He was carrying three books beneath his arm.

One nearly fell.

He caught it with his elbow, lost his balance and landed on a sack of onions.

"AY!"

Wadani rushed forward.

"MDAVIDO!"

He sat up.

"I'm okay."

"You have crushed my onions!"

"Only the emotional ones."

"GET OFF MY ONIONS!"

Phylmyk tried not to laugh.

She failed.

Mdavido stood, brushed his trousers and looked at the black card.

"What is that?"

"A message."

"From who?"

"Someone who thinks six riders can share five helmets."

Mdavido considered this.

"Maybe one rider has a very small head."

Phylmyk stared.

"That's your theory?"

"Maybe they were sharing."

She sighed.

"Mdavido, please."

He took the card.

His expression changed.

"Wait."

"What?"

"Six riders. Five helmets."

"Yes."

"What if one of them wasn't wearing a helmet?"

"Then why call them a rider?"

"Maybe they weren't riding."

Phylmyk opened her notebook.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Mdavido smiled.

"You see?"

"What?"

"Sometimes the smartest person in Gema is me."

"Don't become too excited."

"Why?"

"It might be your last intelligent thought of the day."

He laughed.

Then Wadani returned carrying the sugar.

"Here."

Phylmyk paid.

But as she turned to leave, she noticed something.

Wadani had placed the black envelope beneath the counter.

And her hands were trembling.

## Chapter Two: Dantez Makes a Terrible Offer

Dantez was waiting outside Kwa Kariz.

He looked as though he hadn't slept.

His black jacket was dusty.

His eyes were red.

And for once, there was no clever smile on his face.

Labonator stood beside him, eating a chapati.

Phylmyk approached with Mdavido.

"What happened to you?" she asked.

Dantez looked at her.

"I need your help."

Mdavido stopped walking.

He looked left.

Then right.

Then up at the sky.

"Something is very wrong with the universe."

Dantez ignored him.

"Phylmyk, I'm serious."

"So am I," Mdavido said. "Did you hear yourself? You just asked the person you hate for help."

"I don't hate her."

Phylmyk raised an eyebrow.

"You called me an interfering little detective yesterday."

"That was yesterday."

"What changed?"

Dantez took a small black helmet from his bag.

He placed it on the bench.

Labonator stopped chewing.

"Not that thing again."

Phylmyk recognised the symbol immediately.

**THE SHADOW RIDERZ.**

"Where did you get this?"

"Someone left it for me."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

Mdavido folded his arms.

"For a computer genius, you know remarkably little."

Dantez glared at him.

"Would you like to be useful?"

"I'm providing emotional support."

"To whom?"

"Myself."

Phylmyk examined the helmet.

Inside was a white label.

**RIDER 06.**

She looked at Dantez.

"Who is Rider Six?"

"That's what I want to know."

"Why?"

Dantez hesitated.

Then removed his phone.

He showed her several messages.

**YOUR FIRST ASSIGNMENT IS READY.**

**THE ANSWERS ARE INSIDE TOMORROW HOLDINGS.**

**WELCOME BACK, RIDER 06.**

Phylmyk read them twice.

"Welcome back?"

Dantez nodded.

"How can someone welcome you back to a group you never belonged to?"

"Exactly."

Labonator finished his chapati.

"I told him to report the messages."

"Did you?" Phylmyk asked.

"Yes."

"To whom?"

"Kariz and the safety officer. He showed them last night."

Phylmyk looked surprised.

Dantez shrugged.

"Labonator wouldn't leave me alone."

"Because I have a functioning brain," Labonator replied.

"That remains debatable."

Phylmyk smiled.

Then she became serious.

"If we're going to work together, there are rules."

Dantez sighed.

"I knew this was coming."

"Rule One. No lying."

"Fine."

"Rule Two. No stealing."

"Fine."

"Rule Three. No breaking into buildings or other people's computers."

"You're removing all the interesting parts."

"Rule Four. You tell us everything."

Dantez stared at her.

"Everything?"

"Everything."

He looked toward Labonator.

His friend nodded.

Dantez exhaled.

"Fine."

Phylmyk extended her hand.

"Temporary partners?"

Dantez looked at it as though she were offering him a dead cockroach.

"Temporary."

He shook her hand.

Mdavido gasped.

"THIS IS HISTORY!"

Phylmyk released Dantez's hand.

"Mdavido."

"Yes?"

"Please stop."

"I need to document this moment. The detective and the villain have signed a peace treaty!"

Dantez turned away.

"I already regret this."

A quiet voice sounded behind them.

"Your interpersonal reconciliation is an encouraging development, although the sustainability of the arrangement remains questionable."

Vanny Boy.

He stood beneath the garage doorway, holding a notebook.

Mdavido closed his eyes.

"Not now, Vanny."

Vanny adjusted his glasses.

"On the contrary, the temporal circumstances are particularly appropriate."

Phylmyk smiled.

"Good morning, Vanny."

"Good morning."

"Can you help us with something?"

He examined the helmet.

Then the card she had received from Wakibe.

His expression changed.

"Where did you obtain this?"

"Somebody left it at Wadani's shop."

Vanny studied the words.

**SIX RIDERS. FIVE HELMETS.**

Then he studied the label.

**RIDER 06.**

He looked up.

"There is a fundamental inconsistency between the purported numerical identity of the participant and the historical composition of the group."

Mdavido groaned.

"PLEASE!"

Vanny hesitated.

He took a breath.

Then said:

"Someone is lying about the sixth rider."

Phylmyk stared.

Mdavido slowly opened one eye.

"You just said something normal."

"Yes."

"Are you feeling okay?"

Vanny looked offended.

"My neurophysiological condition is entirely satisfactory."

Mdavido smiled.

"There he is."

But Vanny wasn't smiling.

He was examining the inside of the helmet.

"There's something else."

"What?"

He pointed to a tiny mark beside the label.

A number.

**18.**

Phylmyk felt a chill.

Eighteen.

The same number as the missing boxes of school equipment.

Dantez leaned closer.

"Coincidence?"

Vanny shook his head.

"Possibly. But the relationship warrants further investigation."

Phylmyk looked at the helmet again.

For the first time, she wondered whether they had misunderstood everything.

Maybe the sixth rider wasn't connected to the five volunteers at all.

Maybe it was connected to the eighteen missing boxes.

## Chapter Three: The Boy Who Almost Lost a Football Match

The Gema Inter-Neighbourhood Football Championship was the biggest sporting event of the school holidays.

Langa versus Shabe.

Tamtam versus Sandona.

The winning teams would meet in the final.

And this year, a sponsor had offered something extraordinary.

A new football kit for the winning team.

Five hundred exercise books for the participating schools.

And solar reading lamps for the community libraries.

The sponsor was—

**TOMORROW HOLDINGS.**

Labonator stood before the tournament poster.

His mouth fell open.

"LOOK AT THE PRIZES!"

Dantez glanced at it.

"Yes."

"NEW FOOTBALL KITS!"

"Yes."

"BRANDED BOOTS!"

"Yes."

"AND A GOLDEN TROPHY!"

"You're shouting."

"BECAUSE I'M EXCITED!"

Phylmyk approached.

"What time is the final?"

"Saturday afternoon," Labonator said.

"And where?"

"Sandona Sports Ground."

She examined the poster.

The company logo showed a silver triangle.

The same symbol that had appeared on the Silverline delivery vans.

Phylmyk pointed.

"Look."

Dantez leaned closer.

"Tomorrow Holdings."

"Silverline Logistics."

He nodded.

"Same triangle."

"Why would they sponsor football equipment and reading lamps after taking over the company linked to the missing equipment?"

Labonator frowned.

"Maybe they're trying to improve their reputation."

"Or maybe they're trying to hide something."

He looked horrified.

"Can we solve the mystery after football?"

Phylmyk stared.

"Labonator."

"What?"

"There's something suspicious about the sponsor."

"There's something important about the trophy."

Dantez laughed.

"You're impossible."

"Thank you."

The first match began at two o'clock.

Shabe was playing Langa.

Labonator was Shabe's captain.

And unfortunately, he was having a terrible game.

He missed his first pass.

He lost the ball twice.

And when he finally got a clear chance to score, he kicked the ball so high that it flew over the goal, over the fence and into a vegetable stall.

From the stands, Mdavido shouted:

"LABONATOR! ARE YOU PLAYING FOOTBALL OR DELIVERING TOMATOES?"

The crowd burst out laughing.

Labonator glared at him.

Mdavido waved.

"GOOD EFFORT!"

Phylmyk sat beside Vanny and Lucanji.

Dantez stood near the fence, watching.

A few rows ahead, Amani was cheering for Langa.

The teams were tied.

One goal each.

Five minutes remained.

Then a Shabe player fell awkwardly.

The referee stopped the game.

The boy was limping.

Labonator hurried over.

"Are you okay?"

"My ankle hurts."

"Can you continue?"

"I don't think so."

Labonator glanced toward the scoreboard.

Shabe had no experienced substitute.

He looked at his teammate.

Then at the goal.

Then at the crowd.

His teammate lowered his head.

"I'm sorry, Captain."

Labonator placed a hand on his shoulder.

"Don't apologise. We'll manage."

He helped the boy toward the sideline, where an adult attended to him.

The match resumed.

Shabe played with a replacement who had barely practised with the team.

Within minutes, Langa attacked.

A striker broke through.

The ball flew toward the goal.

The Shabe goalkeeper dived.

Missed.

The crowd gasped.

Labonator sprinted back and cleared the ball just before it crossed the line.

"YES!" Mdavido screamed.

Labonator didn't celebrate.

He turned to the younger substitute.

"Stay close to me. We defend together."

Two minutes later, Shabe won a corner.

The ball came toward Labonator.

He could have tried to score.

The goal was open.

Everyone shouted.

"SHOOT!"

Instead, he saw the substitute standing unmarked.

Labonator passed.

The boy kicked.

The ball struck the post.

Bounced.

And rolled into the net.

For one second, the entire pitch fell silent.

Then—

**GOOOOOOAL!**

The crowd erupted.

Shabe had won.

Labonator ran toward the substitute and lifted him into the air.

Mdavido nearly fell from the stands.

"HE PASSED! HE ACTUALLY PASSED!"

Phylmyk laughed.

Even Dantez smiled.

Then something unexpected happened.

A man in an expensive grey suit approached the field.

He was tall, neatly dressed and wearing dark glasses.

Two security staff walked behind him.

The tournament organiser introduced him.

"Ladies and gentlemen, please welcome the director of Tomorrow Holdings, Mr. Adrian Kirenga!"

The crowd applauded.

Kirenga waved.

He approached Labonator.

"Excellent match, young man."

"Thank you, sir."

"You showed leadership."

Labonator grinned.

Then Kirenga did something strange.

He looked directly toward Phylmyk.

His smile changed.

He leaned toward Labonator.

"Your friend in the detective coat."

Labonator frowned.

"Phylmyk?"

"Yes."

"She's over there."

"I know."

Kirenga smiled again.

"Tell her I enjoyed her investigation at Kwa Kariz."

Labonator stared.

"You know about that?"

"Everyone in Gema knows about it."

Kirenga handed him a small envelope.

"Give her this."

Then he walked away.

Labonator hurried toward Phylmyk.

"Detective!"

"What?"

"That sponsor man gave me something for you."

Phylmyk opened the envelope.

Inside was a photograph.

It showed five black-helmeted riders standing outside Kwa Kariz.

The photograph was familiar.

Except for one detail.

Someone had circled the sixth helmet in red ink.

Underneath, in neat handwriting, were the words:

**ASK THE WOMAN WHO SELLS BREAD.**

Phylmyk stopped breathing for a moment.

Wadani.

The woman who ran Wakibe.

The woman who had received the first mysterious delivery.

The woman whose hands had trembled that morning.

Phylmyk looked toward the road.

Kirenga's black car was already disappearing around the corner.

For the first time, she wondered whether someone was trying to reveal a secret—

Or lead her into another trap.
