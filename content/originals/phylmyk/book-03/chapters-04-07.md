## Chapter Four: The Woman Who Sold Bread

Wadani was counting coins when Phylmyk returned to Wakibe.

"Three hundred and forty. Three hundred and fifty. Three hundred and—"

"Wadani."

"Yes, my girl?"

"We need to talk."

Wadani looked up.

Behind Phylmyk stood Yobra, Mdavido, Vanny Boy, Lucanji, Labonator and Dantez.

Wadani stared.

"Have you brought the entire population of Gema to my shop?"

Mdavido raised his hand.

"Not the entire population. Amani is coming."

"And I suppose you all want something?"

Lucanji nodded.

"Biscuits."

"Of course you do."

Phylmyk placed Kirenga's photograph on the counter.

Wadani stopped smiling.

"Where did you get that?"

"Mr. Kirenga."

"Kirenga?"

"The director of Tomorrow Holdings."

Wadani's expression hardened.

"That man."

Phylmyk watched her carefully.

"Do you know him?"

"I know of him."

"Do you recognise the photograph?"

Wadani looked away.

"Wadani."

"What?"

"You told me you didn't know about the sixth rider."

"I said I didn't know what that message meant."

"That's not the same thing."

Wadani slowly placed the coins inside the till.

"Phylmyk, some matters are for adults."

The girl felt her temper rising.

"Everybody keeps saying that!"

"Because sometimes it's true."

"But somebody has been sending us threatening messages!"

That made Wadani turn.

"Threatening you?"

Phylmyk showed her the phone.

Wadani read the messages.

Her eyes widened.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because you wouldn't answer my questions!"

Wadani looked at Yobra.

"You knew about these?"

Yobra nodded.

"We reported them to the safety officer."

Wadani sat down.

For the first time, she looked tired.

Not the ordinary tiredness of somebody who had been standing behind a counter all day.

Something older.

Something heavier.

"Close the door," she told Yobra.

He did.

"Sit down, all of you."

Mdavido pulled up a chair.

Lucanji climbed onto a crate.

Dantez leaned against a shelf.

Vanny remained standing because he claimed sitting after a long walk could interrupt his "post-locomotive cognitive processing."

Nobody asked him to explain.

Wadani took a deep breath.

"Four years ago, when the floods came, Wakibe was one of the collection points for supplies."

"We know," Phylmyk replied.

"What you don't know is that I was responsible for recording every box that passed through this shop."

"Like Yobra?"

"Yes. But I kept the main list."

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"Did you know the five riders?"

"Every one of them."

"Was there a sixth?"

Wadani paused.

Then looked directly at her.

"There were five motorcycle volunteers."

"Then why does the photograph show six helmets?"

Wadani didn't answer immediately.

Instead, she reached into a cupboard.

She removed an old blue exercise book.

Its cover was torn.

Its pages were yellow.

She opened it carefully.

Inside were columns of numbers, dates and names.

The first page read:

**GEMA RELIEF DONATIONS**

**WAKIBE COLLECTION RECORD**

Phylmyk stared.

"You kept another ledger?"

"Of course."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because an old ledger doesn't prove a person is innocent or guilty. And I didn't know whether it was the same one people were looking for."

Dantez stepped forward.

"Does it contain the missing eighteen boxes?"

Wadani nodded.

"Yes."

She turned several pages.

There was the entry.

**18 BOXES — SCHOOL SOLAR LEARNING EQUIPMENT**

**DESTINATION: TAMTAM**

**DISPATCHED TO KWA KARIZ**

But beneath it was something surprising.

In red ink, Wadani had written:

**DO NOT RELEASE WITHOUT SECOND SIGNATURE.**

Phylmyk frowned.

"Why?"

Wadani looked at Yobra.

"Because somebody had been asking strange questions about the shipment even before it arrived."

"Who?"

"A man from the transport company."

"Jaro?" Yobra asked.

Wadani nodded.

"He wanted to know the exact date and time the supplies would move. I told him the schedule wasn't final."

Dantez leaned forward.

"And?"

"He came back the next day. With another man."

"Who?"

"I didn't recognise him."

"Can you describe him?"

Wadani closed the book.

"Tall. Well dressed. Expensive watch. He kept telling me that the supplies belonged to people who had paid for them."

Phylmyk thought of the man at the football pitch.

"Kirenga?"

"I don't know. It was raining heavily. He stayed inside the vehicle."

"Did you see his face?"

"Only briefly."

Phylmyk opened her notebook.

"What happened next?"

Wadani looked toward the window.

"That evening, I went to Kwa Kariz. I wanted to make sure the boxes had been counted correctly."

"Did you?"

"Yes."

"Did you meet the riders?"

"Yes."

"And?"

Wadani became quiet again.

"Something happened that night."

"What?"

She looked at the old photograph.

For a moment, Phylmyk thought she was finally going to explain the sixth helmet.

Instead, Wadani whispered:

"Somebody took a photograph they were never supposed to take."

Everyone stared.

"Which photograph?" Phylmyk asked.

Wadani tapped the one Kirenga had sent.

"This one."

Lucanji leaned forward.

"Why?"

"Because it shows something that was missing from the official records."

"What?"

Wadani opened her mouth.

Then the shop telephone rang.

She looked at it.

The number on the screen was hidden.

She hesitated.

Then answered.

"Hello?"

A man's voice sounded through the receiver.

"Good afternoon, Wadani."

Her face drained of colour.

"Who is this?"

"You've been talking to the children."

Wadani looked at Phylmyk.

"Who are you?"

The man laughed softly.

"Tell the detective girl that if she wants the truth, she should come to Sandona tomorrow."

The line went silent.

Wadani slowly placed the telephone down.

Phylmyk stood.

"Who was it?"

Wadani stared at her.

"I don't know."

Then she looked at the old ledger.

"But whoever it was knows I kept this."

## Chapter Five: The Sandona Invitation

The following morning, Gema woke to an announcement.

Posters had appeared across town.

At the matatu stage.

Outside Wakibe.

Beside the entrance to Kwa Kariz.

Even on the wall near Mama Sia's bookstall in Bonde.

They all carried the same words.

**TOMORROW FESTIVAL**

**A CELEBRATION OF TECHNOLOGY, TALENT AND THE FUTURE**

**SANDONA COMMUNITY GROUNDS**

**FREE ENTRY FOR ALL LEARNERS**

Beneath the announcement were photographs of solar lamps, computers, motorcycles, trophies and smiling schoolchildren.

Mdavido studied one of the posters.

"Free entry?"

"Yes," Amani replied.

"Free food?"

"It doesn't say."

"Then what kind of festival is this?"

Lucanji shook his head.

"A suspicious one."

Phylmyk smiled.

They were gathered outside the Sandona library.

Amani had brought something important.

A festival programme.

"My mother received this yesterday," she said.

Phylmyk opened it.

Inside was a schedule of activities.

10:00 a.m. — Opening Ceremony.

11:00 a.m. — Innovation Challenge.

12:00 p.m. — Young Readers Showcase.

2:00 p.m. — Technology Exhibition.

4:00 p.m. — Community Awards.

On the final page was a photograph of Adrian Kirenga.

Beneath it:

**TOMORROW HOLDINGS: LIGHTING GEMA'S FUTURE.**

Phylmyk pointed to the photograph.

"He's using school supplies to build his reputation."

Amani frowned.

"We don't know that."

Phylmyk turned toward her.

"What?"

"We know his company took over Silverline. We know some equipment went missing four years ago. We don't know that he stole it."

Mdavido raised his hand.

"She's right."

Phylmyk stared at him.

"You agree with Amani?"

"Miracles happen."

Amani smiled.

Then became serious.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"My father works at Tomorrow Holdings."

The others fell silent.

"He works in accounts," she continued. "He doesn't own the company, but he's been there for three years."

Phylmyk looked uncomfortable.

"I didn't know."

"That's why I want us to be careful."

"Do you think your father knows about the missing supplies?"

"I don't know."

"Can you ask him?"

Amani looked down.

"I already did."

Phylmyk leaned closer.

"What did he say?"

"He told me that sometimes a company inherits problems from a business it buys."

"And?"

"He said the important thing is whether the company investigates and corrects those problems."

Phylmyk thought about that.

It was a reasonable answer.

Maybe.

But she still didn't trust Kirenga.

"What else did your father say?"

Amani hesitated.

"He told me to stay away from Mr. Jaro."

Yobra looked up sharply.

"Why?"

"He said Jaro is not working for Tomorrow Holdings anymore."

"Since when?"

"Since yesterday."

Phylmyk felt her pulse quicken.

Yesterday.

The day Jaro had appeared at Checkpoint Seven.

She took out her notebook.

"What happened?"

Amani looked around.

"He was dismissed."

"For what?"

"My father wouldn't say."

Vanny Boy adjusted his glasses.

"The temporal proximity between the discovery of the historical records and the termination of the transport supervisor's employment is potentially significant."

Mdavido sighed.

"Translation: That's suspicious."

"Indeed."

Phylmyk looked at the festival programme again.

"Tomorrow Holdings is putting on an exhibition of solar lamps."

"Yes," Amani said.

"Could we see them?"

"Of course. It's a public exhibition."

"Good."

Dantez, who had been unusually quiet, stepped forward.

"Why don't we simply ask Kirenga about the eighteen boxes?"

Phylmyk stared.

"Ask him?"

"Yes."

"Directly?"

"Yes."

Mdavido started laughing.

Dantez frowned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Then why are you laughing?"

"Because you're suggesting we solve a mystery by having a normal conversation. This is a historic day."

Dantez rolled his eyes.

Phylmyk thought carefully.

"We can ask questions. But we shouldn't assume he'll tell us the truth."

Amani nodded.

"And if we find something important, we tell the safety officer."

"Agreed."

Lucanji raised his hand.

"Can we eat before we begin?"

Mdavido looked at him.

"You just ate."

"That was before the investigation."

"Lucanji!"

"What?"

"How many investigations do you have in one stomach?"

Lucanji thought for a moment.

"Six."

Vanny looked interested.

"That would require an unusually high gastrointestinal storage capacity."

Mdavido shook his head.

"Do not encourage him."

## Chapter Six: The Lamp with Two Names

The technology exhibition opened beneath a large white tent.

Hundreds of children had come from across Gema.

There were small robots.

Solar-powered fans.

Computer demonstrations.

Model buildings.

Electric circuits.

Even a bicycle fitted with a generator.

Labonator was fascinated.

He moved from table to table like a child in a sweet shop.

"LOOK AT THIS!"

A student from Shabe had built a small model vehicle that followed a painted path.

Labonator crouched beside it.

"How does it work?"

The student explained.

Labonator listened carefully.

For once, he did not interrupt.

Nearby, Dantez was studying a display about renewable energy.

He looked unimpressed.

"I could make something better."

Phylmyk turned.

"Then why don't you?"

He shrugged.

"Maybe I will."

"Without stealing anything?"

"Detective, are you going to mention that forever?"

"Only until you stop giving me reasons."

They smiled.

A little.

At the centre of the exhibition stood Tomorrow Holdings' biggest display.

A row of elegant solar learning lamps.

Beside them was a large poster.

**TOMORROW BEACON**

**THE FUTURE OF EVENING STUDY**

Children gathered around the lamps.

A representative explained that the units could be charged during the day and used for reading after sunset.

Phylmyk listened.

Then noticed Lucanji staring at the bottom of one lamp.

"What is it?"

He pointed.

"That lamp has two names."

Phylmyk bent closer.

A new sticker on the lamp read:

**TOMORROW BEACON — MODEL B**

But beneath the edge of the sticker, old lettering was visible.

Lucanji had spotted it because the new sticker was peeling slightly.

Phylmyk leaned closer without touching the display.

She could make out three letters.

**TAM.**

Her heart began beating faster.

"Tamtam," she whispered.

Vanny moved beside her.

"Potentially a residual asset marking beneath the current product identification label."

Mdavido crouched.

"Old writing under a new sticker."

"Precisely."

Phylmyk called Amani.

"Look at this."

Amani examined the lamp.

"It could be an old model they've refurbished."

"Exactly."

"That's not necessarily wrong."

"Unless the old model was part of the missing school shipment."

Amani considered.

"Do you have proof?"

Phylmyk opened her notebook.

"Not yet."

The exhibition representative approached.

"Can I help you?"

Phylmyk stood.

"Yes. Where do these lamps come from?"

The woman smiled.

"They're part of our community lighting initiative."

"Are they new?"

"Some are new. Others have been refurbished."

"Refurbished from where?"

The woman hesitated.

"I don't have the inventory details here."

"Who does?"

"Our warehouse team."

"Could we see the records?"

The woman's smile became more cautious.

"Those records aren't public."

Phylmyk nodded.

"Thank you."

She walked away.

Mdavido hurried after her.

"That's it?"

"What do you mean?"

"You're not going to shout at her?"

"Why would I?"

"Because you always shout when somebody doesn't answer."

Phylmyk stopped.

"I'm improving."

Mdavido grinned.

"Slowly."

They returned to the main tent.

But as they walked past the exhibition, Vanny Boy noticed something else.

Each lamp had a small number printed on its base.

He wrote down the numbers visible on the public display.

Then compared them with photographs from the old donation programme.

He frowned.

"Curious."

Phylmyk turned.

"What?"

"The numerical identifiers are consistent with an older equipment batch."

"Meaning?"

"These lamps may belong to the same manufacturing series as the missing shipment."

Amani looked at the photographs.

"But that doesn't prove they're the actual missing lamps."

"Correct," Vanny said.

He looked disappointed.

For the first time, his complicated reasoning hadn't given him a definite answer.

Lucanji stepped closer.

"Vanny?"

"Yes?"

"Can a lamp have the same number as another lamp?"

"Manufacturing serial identifiers are normally intended to distinguish individual units."

Lucanji nodded.

"So if we find the exact same number in the old records, we'll know it's the same lamp?"

Vanny stopped.

He looked at Lucanji.

Then at the photographs.

Then at his notebook.

A slow smile appeared.

"That is an excellent observation."

Mdavido nearly dropped his book.

"HE COMPLIMENTED SOMEBODY!"

Lucanji smiled proudly.

Then asked:

"Does that mean I can have biscuits?"

Nobody answered.

## Chapter Seven: The Boy Who Didn't Want to Be Smart

Vanny Boy had always been the cleverest child in most rooms.

At school, he finished difficult Mathematics questions before other learners had written their names.

He could explain how a radio worked.

He understood computer systems.

He remembered numbers nobody else noticed.

But there was one thing he struggled with.

Talking to people.

Not because he disliked them.

He simply found ordinary conversations confusing.

People interrupted.

People joked.

People asked questions without giving enough information.

And when he tried to explain himself, they laughed at his words.

Sometimes, he preferred being alone.

That afternoon, he sat quietly behind the Sandona library, examining the old donation photographs.

Phylmyk found him there.

"Vanny?"

He looked up.

"Yes?"

"Why aren't you with the others?"

"I am conducting an independent comparative examination."

"You're sitting alone."

"That is also factually accurate."

She sat beside him.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Phylmyk asked:

"Does it bother you when Mdavido jokes about the way you talk?"

Vanny looked surprised.

He considered the question.

"His observations are generally intended to produce humour rather than hostility."

"That wasn't what I asked."

Vanny looked down.

Sometimes, complicated words were easier than simple feelings.

"Occasionally," he admitted.

Phylmyk nodded.

"I'll talk to him."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I do not wish to be treated as an individual incapable of managing ordinary social interaction."

Phylmyk smiled slightly.

"Vanny."

"Yes?"

"You can just say you want to handle it yourself."

He considered this.

Then nodded.

"I want to handle it myself."

"Good."

They returned to the photographs.

Vanny pointed at an old delivery record.

"The eighteen boxes were recorded under a single shipment reference. However, the individual equipment serial numbers were documented separately."

"Where?"

"In the donation register."

"Do we have it?"

"No."

"Who has it?"

"Possibly the original donor."

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"Who donated the lamps?"

Vanny looked at the old programme.

"The Gema Readers Foundation."

"Do they still exist?"

"I don't know."

Phylmyk smiled.

"That's the most normal answer you've given me all week."

Vanny almost laughed.

They went inside.

Mdavido was sitting at a table with a mountain of books.

Amani stood beside him.

"What are you doing?" Phylmyk asked.

"Research," Mdavido replied proudly.

"You're reading six books?"

"Seven."

"Why?"

"Because I am an intellectual."

Amani rolled her eyes.

"He's trying to find the old foundation."

Phylmyk sat down.

"Any luck?"

Mdavido opened a local history magazine.

"Actually, yes."

He pointed at a small article.

**GEMA READERS FOUNDATION DONATES SOLAR STUDY EQUIPMENT**

Below it was a photograph of the original donation ceremony.

A group of schoolchildren stood beside eighteen large boxes.

On one box was a printed label.

**GRF-TAM-001**

Mdavido pointed to it.

"That's the beginning of the serial range."

Vanny leaned closer.

"Yes."

Amani turned the page.

Another photograph showed a second box.

**GRF-TAM-018**

Phylmyk felt her excitement rising.

"One to eighteen."

"Exactly," Mdavido said.

"Could the lamps at the exhibition come from those boxes?"

"Possibly."

Vanny took out his notebook.

He compared the visible identifiers.

Then he stopped.

His hand trembled slightly.

"Phylmyk."

"What?"

He showed her his notes.

One of the display lamps carried an old marking partially visible beneath the new label.

**GRF-TAM-0...**

The rest was hidden.

Phylmyk stared.

They were getting close.

Very close.

Then Mdavido noticed Vanny's expression.

"What's wrong?"

Vanny looked at him.

For a moment, he hesitated.

Then said:

"Sometimes I don't know how to explain things so people understand."

Mdavido stopped smiling.

He put down his book.

"Oh."

Vanny continued.

"I know I use complicated words. I don't always realise how they sound."

Mdavido looked uncomfortable.

"I was just joking."

"I know."

Silence.

Then Mdavido scratched his head.

"Sometimes I joke too much."

Vanny nodded.

"That is a reasonable assessment."

Phylmyk tried not to laugh.

Mdavido smiled weakly.

"See? You're doing it again."

Vanny looked at him.

Then something wonderful happened.

He smiled too.

"Yes."

Mdavido extended his hand.

"Friends?"

Vanny considered the offer.

Then shook it.

"Friends."

Lucanji, who had been listening quietly, raised his biscuit.

"Can I be friends too?"

Mdavido laughed.

"You're already our friend."

"Good."

"Why?"

"Because friends share food."

He held out the packet.

Everyone took a biscuit.

For a moment, the mystery could wait.

Then Phylmyk's phone vibrated.

Amani checked the message.

It was from her father.

**Amani, please come home. Something has happened at Tomorrow Holdings.**

A second message followed.

**The warehouse records have disappeared.**
