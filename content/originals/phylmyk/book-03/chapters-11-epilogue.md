## Chapter Eleven: Eighteen Boxes

By late afternoon, the investigation had taken a major turn.

The company agreed to an official review of its remaining warehouse stock.

The warehouse records had not all been destroyed.

Some historical stock information was still held in the accounts archive.

With the old Gema donation register, Yobra's notebook, Wadani's ledger and the company summaries, the officers were able to trace the missing shipment more clearly.

The results were astonishing.

Sixteen boxes remained in the Sandona warehouse.

Two had been opened and used for the demonstration stock.

All eighteen consignments could now be accounted for in the company's records.

The equipment had been mixed with acquired commercial inventory instead of being delivered to the schools for which it had been donated.

For four years, those schools had never received it.

Kariz sat quietly when he heard the news.

Wadani covered her mouth.

Yobra stared at the ground.

Phylmyk felt both happiness and anger.

The equipment had been found.

But finding it did not erase what had happened.

Mr. Barasa explained that the ownership and responsibility questions would still need to be resolved formally.

The Gema Readers Foundation would be involved.

The transport records would be reviewed.

And the authorities would determine whether anyone had deliberately misrepresented the shipment.

Phylmyk listened.

Then asked:

"Will the children finally get their lamps?"

Kariz looked at her.

"That's what we're working toward."

Beside them, Mdavido was unusually quiet.

He had spent years borrowing books because his school library did not have enough.

He knew what it meant to want something for learning and be unable to get it.

"Eighteen boxes," he whispered.

Phylmyk looked at him.

"What?"

"Eighteen boxes sitting in a warehouse while children were trying to study without proper lighting."

He shook his head.

"That's terrible."

Amani sat beside him.

"I know."

For a moment, their usual teasing disappeared.

Labonator approached with his football.

He looked at the group.

"What's wrong?"

Mdavido glanced up.

"Nothing."

Labonator sat down.

"That's the second time today somebody has said nothing when something is clearly wrong."

Dantez gave a small smile.

"You're learning."

Labonator looked at him.

"Don't ruin my moment."

They sat together.

Then Lucanji appeared carrying two packets of biscuits.

He offered one to Mdavido.

Mdavido took it.

"Thank you."

Lucanji smiled.

"You're welcome."

For once, nobody made a joke.

Then Vanny Boy spoke.

"The ethical implications of diverting educational resources from their intended beneficiaries are considerably more significant than the mere financial value of the assets."

Mdavido looked at him.

Then smiled sadly.

"You mean the children needed the lamps more than the company needed the money."

Vanny nodded.

"Yes."

Mdavido handed him a biscuit.

"Then say that next time."

Vanny took it.

"I shall endeavour to do so."

Lucanji frowned.

"What does endeavour mean?"

Mdavido sighed.

"Here we go again."

## Chapter Twelve: The Night Gema Shone

Three weeks later, the people of Gema gathered at Tamtam Community School.

The recovered equipment had been examined.

Usable lamps and charging units had been repaired or checked for safety. Unserviceable items were replaced through an agreed arrangement involving Tomorrow Holdings, the original donors and the local education committee.

The schools were finally receiving the resources intended for them.

The event was simple.

No enormous stage.

No expensive banners.

No speeches about controlling the future.

Just families, teachers, learners and tables filled with equipment.

Wadani had brought tea.

Kariz had helped organise transport.

Yobra had assisted with the final inventory.

Amani and her father had worked with the donors to reconcile the records.

Mdavido was helping arrange reading books.

Lucanji was supposed to be helping too.

Instead, he was eating mandazi.

Phylmyk found him sitting beneath a tree.

"Lucanji!"

He looked up.

"Yes?"

"You're supposed to be carrying books."

"I carried three."

"Only three?"

"Yes."

"Then what happened?"

"I became tired."

"From carrying three books?"

"Detective, everybody has limits."

She laughed.

Nearby, Vanny Boy was explaining the solar lamps to a group of younger children.

"The photovoltaic modules facilitate conversion of incident solar radiation into electrical energy—"

The children stared.

Vanny stopped.

He looked toward Mdavido.

Mdavido raised an eyebrow.

Vanny tried again.

"Sunlight charges the lamp during the day."

The younger children smiled.

"Then you can use it for reading after sunset."

One girl raised her hand.

"Does it work when the sun isn't shining?"

Vanny nodded.

"Yes, if it has already stored enough energy."

The girl smiled.

"Thank you!"

Vanny looked pleased.

Very pleased.

Mdavido gave him a thumbs-up.

From across the compound, Labonator shouted:

"PHYLMYK!"

She turned.

"WHAT?"

"COME HERE!"

She hurried over.

Labonator stood beside his blue car.

Dantez was leaning against the bonnet.

Yobra stood nearby.

"What happened?" Phylmyk asked.

Labonator grinned.

"Guess what."

"What?"

"MY CAR HAS BEEN FIXED!"

Phylmyk stared.

"Again?"

"Properly this time!"

"Does it still roll away by itself?"

"NO!"

"Are you sure?"

"YES!"

She folded her arms.

"Then explain why you're holding the handbrake."

Labonator looked down.

Dantez burst out laughing.

Yobra shook his head.

"Some things in Gema will never change."

The ceremony began.

The headteacher thanked everyone who had helped recover the missing equipment.

Wadani received a certificate for preserving the original donation records.

Kariz received recognition for the garage's volunteer work during the floods.

Yobra was thanked for coming forward with his notebook.

When his name was called, he looked surprised.

He walked to the front.

The headteacher shook his hand.

"Sometimes courage means telling the truth about a mistake we made long ago."

Yobra nodded.

Then looked toward Phylmyk.

She smiled.

He smiled back.

Later, several learners gathered beneath a classroom veranda.

A teacher switched on one of the solar reading lamps.

A warm light spread across the open books.

The children leaned closer.

Some began reading quietly.

Others pointed at pictures.

One small boy held his lamp carefully and said:

"Now I can finish my story at home."

Phylmyk stood nearby.

For once, she didn't open her notebook.

She simply watched.

Then Mdavido joined her.

"Detective?"

"Yes?"

"How does it feel?"

"How does what feel?"

"Solving a case."

She thought for a moment.

"Good."

"Only good?"

"Very good."

He smiled.

"Better than winning an award?"

She nodded.

"Much better."

They watched the children reading.

Then Mdavido said:

"You know, I think Gema needs a detective club."

Phylmyk raised an eyebrow.

"Really?"

"Yes."

"What would we call it?"

"Mdavido and the—"

"No."

"I haven't finished!"

"No."

He laughed.

"Fine. What about the Gema Detectives?"

Phylmyk considered.

"That's not bad."

"Not bad?"

"Actually, it's good."

Mdavido grinned.

"Write that down."

"What?"

"Mdavido had a good idea."

Phylmyk opened her notebook.

She wrote:

**CASE 003 — THE SIXTH RIDER**

**STATUS: SOLVED**

Underneath, she added:

*There were five motorcycle riders.*

*The sixth person was Wadani, the record keeper.*

*The mysterious Rider 06 messages were an attempt to manipulate the investigation.*

*The eighteen missing equipment consignments have been traced.*

Then she paused.

And wrote one final line.

*Never confuse a mystery with the truth somebody wants you to believe.*

She closed her notebook.

The case was over.

Or so she thought.

Because at Kwa Kariz, somebody had just left a black envelope on Dantez's workbench.

And this time, the message wasn't addressed to him.

It was addressed to Vanny Boy.

## Epilogue: The Boy They Chose

Kwa Kariz was quiet.

The mechanics had gone home.

The garage doors were locked.

Only a small light remained on inside the office.

Dantez was sitting beside his laptop, staring at a black envelope.

Labonator stood behind him.

"Open it."

Dantez said nothing.

"Dantez."

"What?"

"Open the envelope."

"Why don't you open it?"

"Because it's on your workbench."

"That doesn't mean it belongs to me."

Labonator folded his arms.

"Are you afraid?"

Dantez looked offended.

"Of an envelope?"

"Then open it."

Dantez reached for the paper.

Inside was a small white card.

No address.

No sender.

Just one sentence.

**THE WRONG BOY WAS CHOSEN.**

Dantez frowned.

"What does that mean?"

Labonator looked over his shoulder.

"Maybe they're talking about you."

"Very funny."

A second card fell from the envelope.

Dantez picked it up.

This one contained a photograph.

Vanny Boy.

Sitting beneath the jacaranda tree behind Wakibe.

Reading his enormous book.

The photograph had been taken recently.

Very recently.

Dantez's stomach tightened.

On the back were three words.

**WE WANT HIM.**

Labonator stopped smiling.

"No."

Dantez stared at the photograph.

"No what?"

"We're taking this to Kariz and the safety officer."

Dantez nodded.

"Yes."

Labonator blinked.

"Yes?"

"Yes."

"You're not going to try solving this alone?"

Dantez looked toward the dark garage window.

For a moment, he remembered everything.

The six hundred missing books.

The false delivery receipts.

The demonstration he had ruined.

The threatening messages.

And the strange people who seemed to know his every move.

"No," he said quietly. "Not this time."

Labonator smiled.

"Good."

Then Dantez's laptop made a sound.

*Beep.*

Both boys turned.

A message had appeared on the screen.

**GUARDIAN GRID 35 — NEW INVITATION**

Dantez froze.

The display showed the black motorcycle symbol.

Below it was a name.

**VANNY BOY**

Then a sentence appeared.

**INTELLIGENCE SHOULD NEVER BE WASTED.**

Another followed.

**WE CAN GIVE HIM WHAT GEMA CANNOT.**

Labonator stared.

"What does that mean?"

Dantez didn't answer.

He knew exactly what it meant.

Someone was offering Vanny Boy a place among the Shadow Riderz.

A place where his intelligence would be celebrated.

A place where nobody would laugh at the complicated words he used.

A place where he might finally feel understood.

And that worried Dantez more than any threat he had received himself.

He closed the laptop.

"We need to warn him."

### Meanwhile, behind Wakibe...

Vanny Boy was sitting alone beneath the jacaranda tree.

He was studying a book about computer systems.

The town had grown quiet.

A gentle wind moved through the leaves.

He turned a page.

Then heard footsteps.

A woman in a dark jacket stood a short distance away.

She wore ordinary clothes.

But in one hand she carried a black motorcycle helmet.

"Vanny Boy?"

He looked up.

"Yes?"

"You're difficult to find."

"That statement is factually inconsistent with my predictable daily routine."

The woman smiled.

"Exactly the sort of answer I expected."

Vanny adjusted his glasses.

"Do I know you?"

"No."

"Then may I inquire about the purpose of this unsolicited interpersonal interaction?"

She laughed.

Not cruelly.

Almost warmly.

"You're very clever, aren't you?"

Vanny looked uncomfortable.

"My cognitive abilities vary according to the complexity and domain of the problem under consideration."

"Of course."

She sat on the opposite end of the bench.

"I've heard you're good with computers."

"I possess an above-average familiarity with several computational disciplines."

"And Mathematics?"

"Particularly."

"And engineering?"

"To a reasonable degree."

She smiled.

"Wonderful."

Vanny closed his book.

"May I ask who you are?"

The woman placed a small card on the bench between them.

**THE SHADOW RIDERZ**

Vanny looked at it.

He knew that symbol.

Everyone in Phylmyk's group knew it.

He stood immediately.

"I believe this interaction requires the attention of an appropriate responsible adult."

The woman did not move.

"Relax, Vanny. I'm not here to frighten you."

"What do you want?"

"To offer you an opportunity."

"What kind?"

"Computers. Inventions. Engineering. People who speak your language."

Vanny hesitated.

"Clarify the final assertion."

She smiled.

"People who understand the things you say."

For a moment, he said nothing.

That was different.

Most people wanted him to simplify.

Most people wanted him to speak less.

Most people laughed before he finished a sentence.

This stranger seemed interested.

Genuinely interested.

"What organisation do you represent?" he asked.

She held up the helmet.

"The Shadow Riderz."

Vanny looked at it.

Then at the woman.

"And what would be expected of me?"

She smiled.

"One small task."

Vanny frowned.

"Specify the task."

She leaned closer.

"Find out what Dantez knows about Guardian Grid 35."

Vanny became very still.

The woman continued.

"That's all."

He looked toward Wakibe.

Through the shop window, Wadani was arranging bread for the morning.

Beyond the road stood the buildings of Gema.

The town he knew.

The friends who sometimes laughed at him but had stood beside him when it mattered.

Phylmyk.

Mdavido.

Lucanji.

Yobra.

And even Dantez, who had finally started making better choices.

Vanny looked at the card.

Then at the woman.

For once, he didn't need complicated terminology.

"No."

The woman blinked.

"Excuse me?"

"I said no."

"Don't you want to work with people who understand you?"

Vanny thought carefully.

Then answered.

"I already have friends."

He picked up his book.

"And I don't share their information with strangers."

He walked toward Wakibe.

Behind him, the woman slowly rose from the bench.

She watched him leave.

Then took out her phone.

She dialled a number.

A man answered.

"Well?"

"He refused."

Silence.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

The man sighed.

"Unfortunate."

"What should I do?"

There was another pause.

Then the man said:

"Leave him alone."

The woman frowned.

"Sir?"

"We don't need Vanny Boy to join us."

"Then why did you send me?"

The man laughed softly.

"To find out whether he would."

The woman looked toward Wakibe.

"And now?"

"Now we know whose side he's on."

The call ended.

She placed the helmet beneath her arm.

And walked away.

### At the other end of Gema...

Phylmyk was brushing her teeth when her phone rang.

Dantez.

She answered.

"What do you want?"

"Phylmyk."

"What?"

"There's something you need to know."

She rolled her eyes.

"Please don't tell me you've stolen another six hundred books."

"No."

"Or changed another computer clock?"

"No."

"Or created another brilliant invention that ruins everybody's day?"

"PHYLMYK!"

She stopped.

His voice sounded different.

Serious.

Worried.

"What happened?"

"The Shadow Riderz."

She sat down on the edge of her bed.

"What about them?"

"They've chosen someone else."

"Who?"

Dantez took a breath.

"Vanny Boy."

Phylmyk went completely still.

Then she reached for her notebook.

On the cover were the words:

**DETECTIVE PHYLMYK — PRIVATE CASE FILES**

She opened it.

Turned to a fresh page.

And wrote:

**CASE 004**

**THE BLACK HELMET**

She looked toward the window.

Gema was quiet.

Too quiet.

Then she said:

"Dantez."

"Yes?"

"This time, we're not waiting for trouble to find us."

A pause.

Then Dantez replied:

"Good."

And for the first time—

The detective and the villain were on the same side.
