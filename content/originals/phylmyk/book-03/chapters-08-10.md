## Chapter Eight: The Missing Records

Amani's father was waiting outside the Sandona Community Library.

His name was Mr. Barasa.

He wore a white shirt, brown trousers and glasses that kept slipping down his nose.

He looked worried.

Very worried.

"Amani," he said when he saw her. "I told you to come home."

"I'm sorry, Dad. I was with my friends."

He looked at the group.

Then at Phylmyk's detective coat.

"Ah. You're the famous detective."

Phylmyk straightened.

"Yes, sir."

Mr. Barasa almost smiled.

"My daughter has told me about you."

"Good things, I hope."

"Some good. Some concerning."

Mdavido leaned toward Labonator.

"Definitely concerning."

Phylmyk kicked his shoe.

Mr. Barasa continued.

"I understand you've been investigating the missing school equipment."

Phylmyk nodded.

"Yes."

"Then I need to tell you something."

He opened a folder.

"I work in the accounts department at Tomorrow Holdings. Three years ago, our company acquired warehouses and other assets from Silverline Logistics."

"We know," Amani said.

"What I didn't know was that some of those assets may have included equipment belonging to a school donation programme."

Vanny Boy leaned forward.

"An unresolved provenance discrepancy."

Mr. Barasa nodded.

"Exactly."

Mdavido stared.

"Finally. An adult who speaks Vanny."

Mr. Barasa continued.

"Yesterday, after the matter at Kwa Kariz, I asked our warehouse manager for the original acquisition records."

"What did he say?" Phylmyk asked.

"He said they were missing."

"All of them?"

"The inventory files covering the old Silverline shipment."

Dantez frowned.

"Someone deleted the records?"

"No. The paper file is missing, and the electronic folder cannot currently be found."

Phylmyk looked at him.

"Could someone have removed them deliberately?"

"Possibly. But we mustn't make accusations before the company investigates."

He took out another document.

"This is a summary from an older, authorised accounts archive."

Phylmyk leaned closer.

The document listed assets transferred to Tomorrow Holdings.

Old vehicles.

Workshop equipment.

Storage containers.

Office furniture.

And a line that made her stop.

**SOLAR EDUCATION UNITS — 18 CONSIGNMENT BOXES.**

Her heart began racing.

"Eighteen!"

Mr. Barasa nodded.

"The number matches."

"And where are they now?"

"That's the problem. The records don't say whether those boxes contained the Gema donation equipment or other stock."

Amani frowned.

"Can't we check the warehouse?"

"That is what I have requested."

"Has Mr. Kirenga agreed?"

Mr. Barasa hesitated.

"He says there is nothing to investigate."

Dantez laughed bitterly.

"Convenient."

Mr. Barasa looked at him.

"You must be Dantez."

"Yes."

"My daughter says you are very talented with computers."

Dantez looked surprised.

"She said that?"

Amani rolled her eyes.

"I also said you behave terribly sometimes."

"That sounds more like you."

Mr. Barasa continued.

"If you're as talented as people say, you should use your skills responsibly. People trust information systems to tell the truth."

Dantez looked away.

For once, he had no clever reply.

Mr. Barasa handed the document to Amani.

"I've already shared the information through the appropriate channels. The safety officer can compare it with the recovered delivery ledger."

Phylmyk took a breath.

They had something important.

But not enough.

The new company records mentioned eighteen boxes.

The old Gema records mentioned eighteen boxes.

And the display lamps had markings that looked familiar.

They needed one detail to connect them.

An individual identifier.

Something that could not easily be explained away.

Then Lucanji spoke.

"Why don't we ask the person who packed the boxes?"

Everyone turned.

Mr. Barasa smiled.

"That's a sensible question."

Phylmyk frowned.

"Who packed them?"

Mr. Barasa looked at the old document.

"The collection was organised through Wakibe."

Wadani.

Again.

Phylmyk felt the pieces shifting.

They weren't searching for a sixth rider anymore.

They were searching for the person who had counted every box.

## Chapter Nine: The Sixth Helmet

Wadani was waiting for them.

She had closed Wakibe early.

The metal shutters were halfway down, and a handwritten sign hung on the door.

**CLOSED FOR STOCKTAKING.**

Mdavido read it aloud.

"Stocktaking?"

Lucanji frowned.

"Is that when people steal stock?"

"No," Vanny replied. "It is the systematic enumeration and reconciliation of inventory assets."

Lucanji considered.

"So counting things?"

"Precisely."

Mdavido sighed.

"One day, Vanny, you'll be able to say two words without using twenty."

They entered through the side door with Wadani's permission.

Inside, the shop was quiet.

Wadani had placed the old blue ledger on the counter.

Beside it lay the photograph of the six helmets.

She looked at Phylmyk.

"I suppose you're going to ask me again."

Phylmyk nodded.

"Yes."

"Then this time, I'll answer."

Wadani sat down.

"Four years ago, on the evening the school supplies disappeared, I went to Kwa Kariz to check the final delivery count."

She opened the ledger.

"I was responsible for eighteen boxes. I had signed for them at Wakibe. I needed to make sure they reached the transport team."

Phylmyk listened carefully.

"When I arrived, the five volunteer riders were preparing to leave. It was raining heavily."

"What happened?" Yobra asked.

"One of the riders handed me a spare helmet because I was getting soaked while we checked the boxes beside the van."

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"A spare helmet?"

Wadani nodded.

"I put it on."

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Mdavido pointed at the photograph.

"WAIT!"

Everyone turned.

"The sixth helmet!"

Wadani smiled sadly.

"Yes."

Phylmyk looked at the photograph again.

The figure behind the van door.

The helmet.

The familiar position beside the delivery boxes.

"You?"

Wadani nodded.

"It was me."

Lucanji stared.

"Wadani was a Shadow Rider?"

"No, my boy."

"But you wore the helmet!"

Wadani laughed.

"Wearing a helmet doesn't make you a motorcycle rider."

Mdavido considered this.

"True. I once wore a chef's hat. Nobody let me cook."

Phylmyk almost smiled.

But she had another question.

"Why didn't you tell us earlier?"

Wadani looked at Yobra.

"Because after the boxes disappeared, people began blaming one another. Some said the riders had stolen them. Others said Kariz was involved. Some even suspected me."

"Why?"

"Because I was the last person to check the boxes before the van left."

Phylmyk looked at the ledger.

"Did you sign the delivery?"

"I signed the handover count. But the transport supervisor was supposed to provide a second signature."

She turned a page.

There was a blank space.

**TRANSPORT SUPERVISOR SIGNATURE: __________**

Phylmyk leaned closer.

"Nobody signed?"

Wadani shook her head.

"I refused to release the final copy until the supervisor signed. He said he would return."

"Jaro?"

"Yes."

"And he never did?"

"No."

Wadani looked down.

"I was frightened. People were angry. I didn't know who to trust. And when the original transport records disappeared, I feared mine would be taken too."

"So you hid the ledger?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

Wadani smiled.

"Here."

Phylmyk looked around.

"Here?"

"In Wakibe."

"But where?"

Wadani pointed at the old biscuit display.

Mdavido stared.

"Don't tell me."

"Behind it."

He looked horrified.

"All this time, an important document has been hiding behind biscuits?"

Wadani laughed.

"Nobody ever asks to see what's behind the biscuit shelf."

Lucanji raised his hand.

"I do."

"That's because you keep looking for more biscuits!"

Everyone laughed.

But Phylmyk was thinking.

Wadani had worn the sixth helmet.

She had never been one of the five riders.

And she had never left Gema.

The mysterious phrase suddenly made sense.

**THE SIXTH RIDER NEVER LEFT.**

Except it wasn't quite true.

There had never been a sixth rider.

There had been a sixth person helping with the supplies.

The record keeper.

Wadani.

Phylmyk looked at her.

"Mr. Kirenga knew you were in that photograph."

Wadani nodded.

"Apparently."

"He sent us the picture."

"Yes."

"And somebody sent you threatening messages."

Wadani looked worried.

"Yes."

Phylmyk slowly closed her notebook.

"Then I think we've been looking at this the wrong way."

"What do you mean?" Yobra asked.

"Someone wanted us to think there was a dangerous secret rider."

Dantez leaned forward.

"But instead there was a witness."

"Exactly."

Phylmyk looked at Wadani.

"And that witness kept the original records."

A silence settled over the shop.

Then Vanny Boy spoke quietly.

"The deception was not designed to conceal the sixth individual. It was designed to discredit the individual by assigning a fictitious identity."

Mdavido blinked.

"Wait."

He looked at Phylmyk.

"Translation?"

Phylmyk smiled.

"They wanted us to think Wadani was part of the Shadow Riderz, so nobody would believe her."

Vanny nodded.

"An accurate interpretation."

Dantez stared at the photograph.

Then he said something that made everyone go quiet.

"Mr. Kirenga isn't trying to hide Wadani."

Phylmyk turned.

"What?"

"He's trying to frighten her into handing over the ledger."

Wadani looked at him.

"How do you know?"

Dantez held up his black helmet.

"Because someone tried the same thing with me."

## Chapter Ten: The Man Who Owned Tomorrow

Adrian Kirenga was preparing for the biggest speech of his career.

His grey suit had been pressed.

His shoes were polished.

His hair was perfectly arranged.

On the stage behind him was a banner.

**TOMORROW HOLDINGS — A BRIGHTER FUTURE FOR GEMA**

A crowd filled Sandona Community Grounds.

Schoolchildren sat in rows.

Teachers talked.

Parents held phones.

The new solar lamps were arranged on a table beside the stage.

Mr. Kirenga adjusted his tie.

"How do I look?" he asked his assistant.

"Excellent, sir."

"Confident?"

"Very."

"Trustworthy?"

"Of course."

He smiled.

"Good."

Then his assistant leaned toward him.

"There's a problem."

Kirenga sighed.

"What now?"

"The safety officer is here."

"And?"

"He has asked to examine the equipment."

Kirenga's smile faded.

"Why?"

"The old donation records."

For a moment, Kirenga said nothing.

Then he straightened his tie again.

"Tell him we'll cooperate."

"Yes, sir."

"And keep the children away from the storage area."

His assistant nodded.

Kirenga walked toward the stage.

The audience applauded.

"Good afternoon, Gema!"

The crowd cheered.

"Today, Tomorrow Holdings is proud to introduce an exciting programme to support our children."

More applause.

"We believe every learner deserves a bright future!"

The crowd cheered again.

At the back of the grounds, Phylmyk stood beside Yobra, Amani, Mdavido, Lucanji, Vanny Boy, Dantez and Labonator.

Wadani was speaking quietly with the safety officer.

She had brought her ledger.

Kariz had brought copies of the old dispatch records.

Mr. Barasa had provided the authorised company accounts summary.

And the Gema Readers Foundation had confirmed that an original list of equipment identifiers was available.

Phylmyk looked toward the stage.

Kirenga was smiling at the crowd.

He seemed confident.

Too confident.

"Do you think he knows we're here?" Mdavido whispered.

Phylmyk nodded.

"Yes."

"Then why is he smiling?"

"Because he thinks we'll accuse him without proof."

Dantez folded his arms.

"He's probably right to think that."

Phylmyk turned.

"What?"

"Before today, that's exactly what I would have done. Made a dramatic announcement. Embarrassed him in front of everybody."

Labonator nodded.

"And created a bigger mess."

Dantez glanced at his friend.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Phylmyk looked at the safety officer.

"That's why we're letting the adults examine the records."

Kirenga was still speaking.

"Tomorrow Holdings has invested heavily in modern technology for our community!"

The crowd applauded.

Then he approached the solar-lamp display.

"These are the future!"

He lifted one of the lamps.

At that exact moment, the exhibition coordinator approached the stage.

She whispered something to him.

His smile faltered.

He lowered the lamp.

Then looked toward the safety officer.

For a moment, Phylmyk saw the polished businessman disappear.

In his place stood a worried man.

The coordinator whispered again.

Kirenga forced a smile.

"Ladies and gentlemen, we'll take a short break before the demonstration."

He walked offstage.

The crowd began murmuring.

Mdavido leaned forward.

"What's happening?"

Amani's father approached.

"The inspector has confirmed something important."

Phylmyk felt her heart race.

"What?"

"Two of the demonstration lamps have individual identifiers matching the original donated equipment records."

Vanny Boy straightened.

"Exact identifier correspondence?"

Mr. Barasa nodded.

"Yes."

Vanny whispered:

"Remarkable."

Amani looked worried.

"What does that mean for Dad?"

Mr. Barasa placed a hand on her shoulder.

"It means the company needs to explain how the equipment came into its possession. That's all we know so far."

Phylmyk watched Kirenga walk toward the administrative tent.

For a second, she thought he might disappear.

But the safety officer approached him.

They spoke quietly.

Kirenga gestured toward the company representative.

Then toward the lamps.

The conversation continued.

Nobody shouted.

Nobody ran.

No dramatic arrest happened.

And somehow, that made the moment more serious.

Because for the first time, Kirenga wasn't giving a speech.

He was answering questions.
