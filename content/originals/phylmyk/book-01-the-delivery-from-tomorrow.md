# The Delivery from Tomorrow
**Series:** Phylmyk and the Dantez Files  
**Book:** 1  
**Publisher:** VibeSchool Originals  
**Setting:** Gema, including Langa, Shabe, Tamtam, Kwa Kariz, Wakibe, Bonde and Sandona  
**Audience:** Ages 8–13  
**Edition:** 1.0 · 13 November 2026

*Some towns have secrets. Gema has secrets that refuse to stay secret.*

---

## Chapter 1: The Girl Who Didn't Trust Mondays

Phylmyk did not like three things.

People who lied.

People who touched her detective notebook.

And Mondays.

Unfortunately, today was Monday.

She stood before her mirror, straightening her navy-blue detective jacket. Over it she wore a light brown coat, the kind detectives wore in the American films she loved. Her boots were polished, her cap was perfectly straight, and a small silver magnifying-glass emblem glittered on her chest.

Phylmyk was twelve. She had Black American roots, but Gema was home.

"Detective Phylmyk," she informed her reflection. "Reporting for duty."

"Reporting for breakfast, you mean."

Her older brother, Yobra Mninja, leaned against the doorway, eating a mandazi.

"Why are you dressed like you're investigating the president?"

"Professional appearance is important."

"Professional appearance for what? You haven't washed your cup from yesterday."

"I'm concentrating."

"On what?"

"Crime prevention."

Yobra studied the socks beneath her bed. "Start with preventing crimes against cleanliness."

She threw a pillow. He dodged without moving his feet.

Nobody knew exactly how Yobra had earned the nickname *Mninja*. Some claimed he could climb walls. Others claimed he once escaped through a classroom window without his teacher noticing. Yobra denied everything, which made him even more suspicious.

"Come on," he said. "We need sugar, bread and cooking oil from Wakibe."

"Those aren't detective matters."

"Then don't eat breakfast."

Phylmyk grabbed her notebook. "Fine. But I'm bringing my equipment."

"You mean the broken magnifying glass?"

"It isn't broken. It has character."

"Like your socks?"

"YOBRA!"

He was already outside laughing.

Gema was waking up. Matatus hooted at the junction. Shopkeepers lifted shutters. A woman called out the price of tomatoes. A radio played an old Kenyan song somewhere between a barber's kiosk and a stall piled high with green bananas.

Beyond the busy centre lay the localities of Langa, Shabe and Tamtam. Everyone in Gema knew the famous places: Wakibe, the main shop; Kwa Kariz, the noisy garage; Bonde, the street everyone warned you about; and Sandona, where rich children wore expensive trainers and behaved as though ordinary dust was beneath them.

At Wakibe, Wadani was already arguing with a customer.

"Yesterday you said the bread was too small," the customer complained. "Today it is smaller!"

"Then tomorrow buy two!" Wadani answered.

Phylmyk smiled. Normal morning. Normal arguments. Normal Gema.

Then a motorcycle stopped outside.

Its rider wore a black helmet. He carried a wooden box.

And everything stopped being normal.

## Chapter 2: A Package from the Future

"Delivery for Wakibe," the rider said.

"From whom?" Wadani asked.

"Just sign here."

"I didn't order a wooden box."

"The address is correct."

Phylmyk stepped closer. "Excuse me. Who sent it?"

The rider turned. His dark visor hid his face.

"Who are you?"

"Detective Phylmyk."

"Self-appointed," Yobra added.

"Identification, please," Phylmyk said.

The rider stared. "You're a child."

"An observant child."

Without another word, he placed the box on the counter and walked away. Phylmyk watched his motorcycle disappear around the corner. The bike had no company markings. He had kept his gloves on. And he had not waited for anybody to sign.

"Suspicious," she murmured.

"Everything is suspicious to you," Yobra said.

"Because suspicious things keep happening."

Wadani lifted the box. It was surprisingly light.

From inside came a quiet sound.

*Tik. Tik. Tik.*

Yobra froze. "Is that supposed to be ticking?"

"Could be a clock," Wadani said.

"Let's not open it ourselves," Phylmyk replied.

Wadani moved everyone away from the box and called the local safety officer to check it. While they waited, Phylmyk noticed a copy of the delivery receipt had been left on the counter.

"Yobra. What time is it?"

"Seven forty."

"Are you certain?"

"Unless my watch has resigned."

She pointed at the paper. **DELIVERY COMPLETED: 08:15.**

"That's thirty-five minutes from now," Yobra said.

Phylmyk opened her notebook. **Clue One: A receipt from the future.**

A small voice sounded behind a sack of maize flour.

"Maybe the man is a time traveller."

Lucanji was crouching beside the sacks, holding a packet of biscuits. Eight years old and of Mexican-African heritage, Lucanji had a bright smile and an astonishing talent for appearing where nobody expected him.

"Lucanji! Why are you hiding?"

"I'm not hiding. I'm investigating."

"Investigating what?"

"Whether biscuits taste better before breakfast."

Yobra laughed.

Lucanji leaned toward Phylmyk. "Did you notice his shoes?"

"Whose?"

"The rider's. They were clean. He said he came through Bonde. But it rained there last night and the road's muddy."

"How do you know he said that?"

"I was listening."

"That's called eavesdropping."

"Is it still eavesdropping if you're eating biscuits?"

Before Phylmyk could answer, Wadani's phone rang. She listened. Her face changed.

"What do you mean they're missing? All of them?"

She ended the call, staring at the wooden box.

"The books," she whispered.

"What books?" Yobra asked.

"The six hundred books for the Gema Reading Festival. They've disappeared."

## Chapter 3: The Boy Who Spoke Like a Computer

Every year, schools from Langa, Shabe and Tamtam joined the Gema Reading Festival. Children read stories, acted in plays, competed in spelling contests and received books donated by local businesses.

This year, six hundred new books were meant for school reading corners. Three vehicles had departed from collection points that morning. None had reached Wakibe. Somebody had changed their delivery instructions.

"Three drivers can't all take a wrong turn," Wadani said.

"Unless somebody gave them the same wrong instructions," Yobra replied.

Phylmyk tapped the strange receipt. "We need someone who understands computers."

"Oh, no," Yobra groaned.

"Oh, yes."

They found Vanny Boy beneath the jacaranda tree behind Wakibe. Thirteen-year-old Vanny was reading a book so thick it could have injured somebody. Three pencils lay parallel beside a small notebook.

"Vanny!" Phylmyk called.

He did not look up.

"VANNY BOY!"

He raised his head slowly. "The excessive amplitude of your vocal emissions is producing an entirely avoidable disturbance of my cognitive equilibrium."

Yobra sighed. "Here we go."

"We have a problem," Phylmyk said.

"Statistically, the majority of human social interactions commence with an unresolved problem."

"Six hundred books are missing."

"An appreciable inventory discrepancy."

"What?"

"Many books are missing," Yobra translated.

"We know that!" Phylmyk cried.

She showed Vanny the receipt. He examined it, adjusted his glasses and said, "Ah. Deliberate temporal metadata displacement."

Lucanji frowned. "Is that a disease?"

"No. The timestamp has been artificially offset relative to the actual chronological reference."

A skateboard rolled over the paving stones.

"Can somebody switch this boy to English?" Mdavido demanded.

He was twelve, noisy, mischievous and forever teasing classmates, although he secretly loved school, stories and the library. His skateboard was tucked under one arm; a borrowed adventure novel sat under the other.

"Somebody changed the clock on the receipt machine," Yobra translated.

"An oversimplified but substantially accurate interpretation," Vanny said.

"Don't pretend you understood that," Mdavido told Phylmyk.

"I understood the important part."

"Which part?"

"The part involving the machine."

"That was almost every part!"

Then Vanny pointed to a tiny mark in the receipt's corner: **K.K.**

"The printing apparatus may have originated from a local establishment bearing these initials."

Mdavido leaned forward. "I know a place that uses a printer like this."

"Where?" Phylmyk asked.

"Kwa Kariz."

The garage.

## Chapter 4: Trouble at Kwa Kariz

Kwa Kariz was the loudest place in Gema. Engines growled. Hammers clanged. Mechanics argued with stubborn vehicles. One man pleaded with an engine, "Come on, baby. Today we must cooperate!"

The engine answered with a horrible cough.

Mdavido laughed. "Even cars don't want to work on Mondays."

"We ask questions," Yobra reminded the others. "Nobody searches anyone's property."

Phylmyk nodded. "Agreed."

They spotted Labonator leaning over a bright blue car. He held a spanner in one hand and a football in the other.

He loved cars, football and anything that moved fast. People said he could identify vehicles from their engines. Others said he simply enjoyed making engine noises with his mouth.

"Labonator!" Phylmyk called.

"Eh, Detective! Come to arrest my car? It has already suffered enough."

"Do you print delivery receipts here?"

"Sometimes."

"Does your printer leave K.K. on the paper?"

"Yes."

"Who used it this morning?"

His grin slipped.

"Nobody."

"Nobody?"

"Nobody important."

Phylmyk held out the receipt. "Six hundred school books have disappeared."

Labonator looked toward the back of the garage. Genuine fear crossed his face.

"Go home," he whispered.

"What?"

"Forget the books. You're getting involved in something bigger than you understand."

Mdavido tried a weak joke. "What's bigger? A lorry?"

Nobody laughed.

From the shadows came a soft electronic beep. A young man in a black hood stood beside a sleek motorcycle. A laptop rested on the bench. When he noticed Phylmyk, he closed it.

Then he smiled.

"Who's that?" she asked.

"Nobody," Labonator said.

Suddenly a mechanic shouted, "Your blue car is rolling!"

Labonator ran. Yobra hurried to warn people out of its path. A mechanic climbed in and stopped it safely.

When Phylmyk turned back, the young man was gone. So was the laptop.

On the floor, she found a black sticker with a motorcycle rider inside a circle.

Beneath the symbol were three words: **WE OWN TOMORROW.**

## Chapter 5: Mdavido Gets Accused

By midday everyone in Gema was discussing the missing books. Some blamed the drivers. Others blamed thieves from Bonde. A few claimed the six hundred books had never existed.

Phylmyk knelt outside Wakibe, studying marks in the dust.

"Four little wheels," she muttered. "Skateboard wheels."

Mdavido looked up from his borrowed book. "Don't even think about it."

"Where were you at seven forty?"

"Going to school."

"Did you pass Wakibe?"

"Yes."

"Did you enter the delivery area?"

Mdavido slammed the book shut. "Are you accusing me?"

"I'm asking questions."

"Those are the questions you ask when you're accusing somebody!"

Lucanji tugged Phylmyk's sleeve, but she waved him away.

Mdavido stood, holding his book tightly. "You think I stole books? Me?"

"Explain the wheel marks."

"I came to borrow this book from Wadani. The library doesn't have it, and I couldn't buy my own."

Phylmyk hesitated.

"You see me skating and joking and you decide I must be a thief."

"Mdavido—"

"Maybe your notebook tells you everything about people."

He pushed off on his skateboard and disappeared around the corner.

Lucanji looked at Phylmyk. "That was mean."

"I was investigating."

"You can investigate without being mean."

Yobra crouched by the marks. "Measure them."

Phylmyk took out her ruler. She measured the spacing, imagined Mdavido's skateboard and checked again.

Oh.

These were wider than his board's wheels.

They belonged to a delivery trolley.

She closed her notebook. For the first time that day, she felt less like a detective and more like a girl who had made a serious mistake.

Yobra stood. "Come on."

"Where?"

"To apologise."

## Chapter 6: The Worst Street in Gema

Bonde was called the worst street in Gema. Its drains were poor, some buildings stood empty and arguments could turn loud. When anything went missing anywhere in town, people mentioned Bonde.

But it also had vegetable sellers, repair workers, bookstalls and families who wanted the same safety and happiness as everyone else.

Phylmyk was beginning to understand that reputations were not evidence.

They found Mdavido outside a little bookstall, reading with his skateboard beside him.

"I was wrong," Phylmyk said.

He turned a page. "Congratulations."

"The tracks weren't yours. I'm sorry."

He closed the book. "Do you know why I make jokes?"

She shook her head.

"If everyone is laughing, they don't ask why my shoes are old or why I borrow books instead of buying them."

She sat beside him.

"Sometimes I tease people because I don't want them teasing me first," he admitted.

"That doesn't make teasing okay," Yobra said.

"I know."

Phylmyk held out her hand. "Partners?"

"On conditions. No accusing me without evidence."

"Agreed."

"And I get to name our detective team."

"What name?"

"Mdavido and the Other People."

"Absolutely not."

They shook hands anyway.

From the shop doorway, Mama Sia, who had sold second-hand books in Bonde for twenty years, called to them.

"If you're finished negotiating, perhaps you'd like to know about the strange van."

"A van?" Phylmyk sprang up.

"White. Came just after eight. Stopped by the old storage building. Three men unloaded boxes marked 'Gema Reading Festival'."

"Were they carrying the books?"

"Certainly looked like it."

"Recognise anyone?"

"No. But one kept bouncing a football."

Labonator.

Yobra took out his phone. "We should inform the adults. That building isn't safe."

As they left, Lucanji pointed to the bookstall wall.

Another black motorcycle sticker.

Underneath it, somebody had written **35**.

Vanny leaned close. "A recurring numerical identifier strongly suggests intentional symbolic consistency."

"Translation?" Mdavido sighed.

"The number matters," Yobra said.

"Precisely."

"Vanny, just once, say something normal."

Vanny considered this. "The numerical value possesses considerable investigative significance."

Mdavido threw up his hands. "WE HAVE LOST HIM!"

## Chapter 7: The Secret of Sandona

Wadani called the Gema Reading Festival organisers and the local safety officer. An adult team went to check the old building at Bonde. The children agreed to stay away.

But Phylmyk still needed to understand the number thirty-five.

"The receipt was thirty-five minutes wrong," she said. "The sticker says thirty-five. Why?"

"It might tell us when something happens," Yobra suggested.

"Or where," Phylmyk said.

"Or who!" Lucanji announced.

He pointed at a festival poster outside Wakibe:

**GEMA READING FESTIVAL — SANDONA COMMUNITY HALL — 2:00 PM**

In small print beneath the sponsor logos were the words:

**SANDONA INNOVATION CHALLENGE — PROJECT 35**

Vanny dropped his notebook. "A potentially significant correlative convergence!"

Mdavido picked it up. "Translation: We've found something."

Sandona was not like the rest of Gema. The roads were smooth, the shops had giant windows, and cafés sold drinks with names longer than the cups.

At a fashionable outdoor café, a girl called Amani spotted Mdavido.

"Hey, Skateboard Boy! Still reading those old books?"

"Still paying too much for cold juice?" Mdavido replied.

Her friends laughed.

Phylmyk approached. "We need to ask about Project Thirty-Five."

Amani's smile vanished. She led them away from the noisy tables.

"It was an invention contest," she explained. "Students built projects to solve problems in Gema. Transport, electricity, water, school supplies."

"What happened to Project Thirty-Five?"

"A boy designed a system to track deliveries. The judges rejected it."

"Why?"

"Officially, it wasn't ready. But some people thought he didn't belong in Sandona."

"Because he wasn't rich?" Mdavido asked.

Amani nodded. "His demonstration was better than most of ours."

She opened an old photo on her phone.

A young man in a black jacket stood by a laptop and a motorcycle. The project board behind him read:

**GUARDIAN GRID 35**

Below it was a name.

**DANTEZ.**

Vanny whispered, "The convergence of circumstantial variables is becoming statistically difficult to disregard."

Mdavido stared at the picture. "Sometimes I wish you came with subtitles."

## Chapter 8: Enter Dantez

Dantez loved computers, motorcycles and winning.

He hated being told no. He especially hated being told something was impossible.

His workshop behind Kwa Kariz was full of wires, broken keyboards, circuit boards, motorcycles and plans for inventions. Guardian Grid 35 had been his proudest design: a program for tracking deliveries around Gema.

The Sandona judges had rejected it.

Dantez had smiled politely that day, gone home, and smashed an old keyboard. Not because it was broken, but because he was furious.

Now he stared at his laptop. Three white dots on a map showed the missing book deliveries.

Labonator entered holding a football.

"We have a problem."

"We have several," Dantez replied. "Be specific."

"The books are still in Bonde. The festival starts soon. People are angry."

"Good."

Labonator stared. "Good?"

"They laughed at me. At two o'clock I'll prove my invention can find every missing delivery. Then the books will go back and they'll have to respect me."

"You created the problem so you can pretend to solve it."

Dantez closed the laptop halfway. "You're beginning to sound like a teacher."

"I'm beginning to sound like somebody who doesn't want trouble."

Dantez reminded him about the blue car he'd helped repair and the football trials he'd supported.

"Friends support each other," Dantez insisted.

"Friends also tell each other when they're wrong."

For a moment, Dantez looked uncertain.

Then the screen beeped.

**UNEXPECTED ACCESS REQUEST.**

**GUARDIAN GRID DEMONSTRATION CANCELLED.**

"Who cancelled it?" Dantez demanded.

A black motorcycle symbol flashed.

**THE SHADOW RIDERZ ARE WATCHING.**

For the first time that day, he looked afraid.

"Who are the Shadow Riderz?" Labonator asked.

"Nobody."

But Dantez's hands were shaking.

## Chapter 9: The Clock That Lied

By early afternoon the safety officer had confirmed the books were still in their boxes at Bonde. Volunteers arranged to bring them back.

Everyone celebrated.

Except Phylmyk.

"Why hide the books and then leave clues leading us to them?" she asked.

"Maybe Dantez isn't very clever," Mdavido suggested.

"No. He's clever. He wanted people to find them—after his demonstration."

Yobra checked his watch. "One-thirty."

Mdavido snapped his fingers. "They're using a giant screen at the festival! What if he plans to take it over?"

They hurried to Sandona Community Hall.

Inside, students filled the seats. Teachers stood by the stage. Parents chatted as volunteers carried the first recovered boxes into the building.

Amani hurried toward them. "Somebody sent the organisers a strange message."

She showed Phylmyk her phone:

**AT TWO O'CLOCK, GEMA WILL LEARN WHO CONTROLS TOMORROW.**

The motorcycle symbol appeared beneath it.

Upstairs, a technician was battling with the presentation computer. The welcome slides kept disappearing.

Vanny studied the screen. "An unauthorised content substitution process."

"Someone's changing your slides," Mdavido translated.

"I understood," the technician said.

"Oh."

Phylmyk looked at the system clock. **2:20**.

Her watch said **1:45**.

Thirty-five minutes.

"The same trick," she whispered.

"Somebody changed the computer clock?" Yobra asked.

"Yes. But we don't have to use that computer. Get another presentation device and check it independently."

The technician called the event organiser. With approval, they disconnected the unreliable system and switched to a checked replacement.

For a moment the giant screen went black.

The hall fell silent.

Then the festival logo appeared. Children cheered.

Phylmyk breathed out.

Her phone buzzed.

An unknown number had sent a message:

**YOU THINK YOU'VE WON?**

Another followed:

**COME TO KWA KARIZ. ALONE.**

Underneath was the black motorcycle symbol.

## Chapter 10: A Detective Never Goes Alone

"Absolutely not," Yobra said.

"He wants to meet me."

"Exactly why you shouldn't go."

"He's challenging me."

"You're twelve, Phylmyk."

She wanted answers. But her brother was right. Courage was not the same as recklessness.

They showed the messages to the safety officer, who arranged to check the garage with its owner. The children waited at Wakibe.

Twenty minutes later, Labonator appeared.

His jersey was dirty and his smile had vanished.

"Where's Dantez?" Phylmyk asked.

"I don't know."

"Don't lie."

"I'm not lying."

He sat on a crate and told them about the plan. Dantez had wanted the Sandona judges to admit his invention was good. Labonator had helped move the boxes and now regretted it.

"You must tell the adults everything," Yobra said.

"I will."

Phylmyk watched him carefully. "Who are the Shadow Riderz?"

Labonator went pale.

"I thought Dantez invented that symbol," he admitted. "But somebody else sent him a message using it."

"Who?"

"Dantez doesn't know."

A motorcycle engine sounded outside—low, smooth, unfamiliar.

Labonator stood. "That engine. I've heard it before."

A black motorcycle passed Wakibe slowly. Two people sat on it, both wearing helmets. The passenger turned.

Dantez.

He lifted one gloved hand, not quite waving, more like issuing a challenge.

Then the motorcycle disappeared.

"That wasn't Dantez's usual bike," Labonator whispered.

"Whose was it?" Phylmyk asked.

Labonator shook his head.

The case wasn't finished.

Not even close.

## Chapter 11: The Book Nobody Wanted

The Gema Reading Festival went ahead.

All six hundred books were returned, sorted and sent to school reading corners. Wadani thanked every volunteer, even the ones who looked embarrassed by her enormous hugs.

Mdavido entered the reading competition.

To everyone's surprise, he won.

Not because he was loud. Not because he was popular. Because he had read more books than nearly anyone his age and could explain the stories with real feeling.

On the stage, he grinned at Phylmyk.

"Where's your notebook? Write down that Mdavido is officially a genius!"

Vanny Boy raised one hand. "The available evidence does not justify a definitive classification of exceptional general intelligence on the basis of a single literary competition."

"Vanny, can I have ONE moment?"

Even Vanny smiled.

Amani found Mdavido afterward.

"I shouldn't have mocked your old books," she said. "I'm sorry."

"Apology accepted."

"Could I borrow the Captain Mako story?"

"Only if you don't spill your expensive juice on it."

They laughed.

Phylmyk stood near a window, looking at the crowded hall. The books were safe. Her friends were smiling. Labonator was telling the adults what he knew.

But the black motorcycle symbol still troubled her.

Why had somebody interfered with Dantez's plan? Who had cancelled his demonstration? And why were strangers interested in Gema?

Lucanji appeared with an old blue book.

"Look what I found behind the stage."

The cover had no title. Just a black motorcycle rider inside a circle.

Inside the front cover someone had written:

**FOR THE GIRL WHO ASKS TOO MANY QUESTIONS.**

Phylmyk turned the page.

A map of Gema showed Langa, Shabe, Tamtam, Kwa Kariz, Wakibe, Bonde and Sandona. Each place had a black circle.

Beyond the town, near an old road leading to the hills, somebody had drawn a red X.

Underneath were the words:

**DANTEZ WAS ONLY THE FIRST TEST.**

The next page showed five riders standing beside five motorcycles. All wore black helmets. Four faced away. The fifth stared directly toward the camera.

At the bottom:

**THE SHADOW RIDERZ.**

And below it:

*The first rider has already entered Gema.*

Phylmyk turned one more page.

**YOUR BROTHER KNOWS THE ROAD.**

She looked up.

Yobra had stopped laughing. He was staring at the book in her hands.

"Where did you get that?" he asked.

"Do you recognise these riders?"

No answer.

"Yobra."

He glanced toward the entrance.

"Phylmyk, there are things about Gema you don't know."

"Then tell me."

"Not here. Somebody might be listening."

"Have you been to the old road?"

He looked away.

And that was answer enough.

## Epilogue: The Rider in the Rain

Rain fell across an abandoned petrol station beyond Gema.

A black motorcycle stopped beneath the rusting roof. Dantez climbed off the passenger seat.

"Who are you?" he demanded.

The rider remained seated.

"That isn't important."

"You ruined my demonstration."

"You ruined it yourself."

"My system worked!"

"Your system was predictable."

"Nobody knew what I was planning."

"Phylmyk knew."

Dantez said nothing.

"She found your books. Understood your clocks. Stopped your little show."

"So what?"

"She is clever."

"So am I."

"Then prove it."

The rider held out a small black envelope. Dantez opened it.

Inside was a photograph of Phylmyk standing outside Wakibe.

Beneath it were two words:

**FIND YOBRA.**

"Why?"

The rider started the engine.

"Because your detective has something we need."

"What?"

"She doesn't know yet."

The motorcycle rolled into the rain. Its red taillight disappeared around the bend.

For the first time, Dantez wondered whether he was playing the game—or being played.

He folded the photograph and slipped it into his jacket.

"Fine," he whispered.

"Let's play."

**End of Book One.**
