## Chapter Seven: The Blue Box

The blue box was smaller than a school lunchbox.

It was also much heavier.

Zuri carried it out of the Zero One cargo compartment and placed it on Reva's table at Kwa Kariz.

The lid was scratched. The metal had darkened around the corners. A small latch held it shut.

"May I open it?" Reva asked.

Zuri nodded.

"You're the owner of the old project records."

Reva smiled.

"Some of the records. Remember, the community helped fund the workshop."

Labonator leaned closer.

"Maybe there's treasure."

Mdavido grinned.

"Golden spanners."

Lucanji shook his head.

"Biscuits."

Phylmyk sighed.

"Do you boys think about anything else?"

"Yes," Labonator said. "Football."

Reva opened the latch.

Inside were two oilcloth envelopes, a brass tag and a tiny black-and-white photograph.

No gold.

No biscuits.

No mystery jewels.

Labonator looked personally betrayed.

Reva unfolded the first envelope.

It contained a page bearing a purple stamp:

**GEMA COMMUNITY WORKSHOP COMMITTEE**

**LAND-USE AND EQUIPMENT AGREEMENT**

There were signatures beneath it.

Biko.

Reva.

A community chairperson named Mzee Mutinda.

And a fourth signature that was too faded to read.

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"Is that the document Mavua was talking about?"

"It's a copy of the original agreement," Reva replied. "Not the final land register."

"Does it say the community owns Garage Zero?"

"It says the property was allocated for a community-use workshop under conditions that must be checked in the public records. It doesn't settle every question by itself."

Dantez nodded.

"So we need the council archive."

"Exactly."

Biko examined the brass tag.

It had a little stamped number.

**GZ-001.**

He smiled.

"Our first inventory tag. We put one on Zero One and another in this box."

Zuri looked at the vehicle.

"There's still a tag beneath its seat."

Biko laughed softly.

"Then you restored more than you realised."

Reva opened the second envelope.

Inside were old service cards for community bicycles, wheelbarrows and delivery equipment.

One card carried a name.

**MAMA SIA — BONDE BOOK DELIVERY.**

Mdavido nearly shouted.

"MAMA SIA HAD A DELIVERY SERVICE?"

"She did," Biko said. "When the floods blocked the road, she used to take books to children who couldn't reach the school library."

Phylmyk smiled.

That sounded exactly like Mama Sia.

Then she lifted the photograph.

It showed Zero One carrying a stack of books beneath a small canvas cover.

Beside the tricycle stood a younger Reva.

And a girl with plaited hair.

Sefu leaned forward.

"That's my mother."

Reva nodded.

"Your mother helped paint the original name."

Zuri touched the edge of the photo.

She had never seen it before.

All those years, she had thought Zero One was just an old machine waiting to be fixed.

Now it felt like part of her family.

Dantez looked at the agreement again.

"Why would anyone threaten Zuri over this?"

Reva did not answer immediately.

She looked toward the workshop gate.

"Because some people see a place and think only of what it is worth to sell."

Phylmyk wrote that down.

Then her phone rang.

It was the safety officer.

"We have spoken to the local land office," he told her, with Reva beside the phone. "They have located the archived file reference. There's a community-use agreement recorded there, but it will need formal verification."

"So the property can't just be sold?" Phylmyk asked.

"The situation isn't that simple. But the file gives the responsible people a proper starting point."

Phylmyk nodded.

She had learned that the truth often arrived more slowly than an exciting guess.

Then Zuri pointed to a strange mark on the inside of the box lid.

A handwritten instruction.

**WHEN THE ROAD IS LOST, FOLLOW THE BELL.**

Biko stared.

"That's my handwriting."

"What does it mean?" Dantez asked.

Biko looked toward Shabe.

"We used it when the flood changed the safe delivery routes. It was a reminder to check the community warning point."

"The bell in Garage Zero?" Phylmyk asked.

Biko shook his head.

"Not that bell."

He tapped the table.

"The original signal station was somewhere else."

For a moment, silence settled over the workshop.

Labonator raised his hand.

"Please don't tell me there is another secret garage."

Biko smiled.

"No."

Labonator exhaled.

"Thank goodness."

"A little signal shed," Biko added.

Labonator stared.

"THAT'S WORSE!"

## Chapter Eight: The Wrong Thief

The rumours became ugly on Saturday.

Someone had posted photographs online showing Zuri standing beside a collection of tools.

Under the photographs were the words:

**SHABE GARAGE GIRL CAUGHT WITH STOLEN PARTS.**

By lunchtime, people were repeating the accusation as though it were a fact.

Zuri didn't come to the repair shed.

Sefu sat alone at Wakibe, staring at his phone.

Phylmyk found him there.

"How is she?"

"Angry."

"Does she know who posted it?"

"No."

Sefu looked at his bicycle.

"She says she's never going back."

Labonator walked in carrying a football.

"What happened?"

Sefu showed him the photograph.

Labonator's face changed.

"Those are her tools."

"Exactly."

"Then why are people saying they're stolen?"

"Because somebody wrote it underneath the picture."

Dantez took one look and shook his head.

"There's no evidence here. It's a photograph of tools."

Vanny Boy leaned forward.

"The image lacks sufficient provenance, acquisition history and comparative inventory evidence to substantiate the allegation."

Mdavido nodded.

"It doesn't prove anything."

"Precisely."

Reva had already reported the accusation and given the tool receipts to the safety officer. She had asked the local office to check the alleged theft report before repeating it publicly.

Kariz also checked his own stock records.

His missing tools were a set of specialised sockets, not the ordinary spanners in Zuri's photograph.

The rumours had confused two different things.

But there was still a question: who had made the accusation look official?

Amani came into Wakibe with her father.

Mr. Barasa carried a copy of a document submitted to the local property office.

"I was asked to review a community-use proposal," he explained. "An agent has attached this incident report."

He showed them a page headed:

**GARAGE ZERO — UNSAFE OCCUPATION AND SUSPECTED THEFT**

At the bottom was a reference code.

**MAV-008.**

Phylmyk frowned.

"Mavua."

Mr. Barasa nodded.

"The code appears to belong to his agency. But that alone doesn't tell us who wrote the online post."

"Can the officer ask him?" Yobra said.

"That's the right next step."

The safety officer compared the report with the actual complaints and spoke to Reva, Kariz and the nearby workshops.

There was no verified theft linked to Zuri.

The property office agreed to remove the unsubstantiated allegation from the current review while the source was investigated.

That afternoon, the children returned to the shed with Reva.

Zuri was sitting on a wooden crate.

Her tools were packed away.

Dantez sat beside her.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then he said, "A few months ago, people called me a genius. I believed it so much I thought I could do anything."

Zuri looked at him.

"Then what happened?"

"I moved six hundred schoolbooks just to prove I had built a clever tracking system."

She stared.

"You did what?"

"It was a terrible idea."

"Terrible?"

"Catastrophic."

Zuri shook her head.

Dantez continued.

"People didn't stop remembering it just because I apologised. I had to change what I did."

Zuri stared at the old cargo tricycle.

"So what do I do?"

"You keep your records. Ask the adults to help with the false accusation. And if you still want to build Zero One, build it properly."

She looked at him.

"Why are you helping me?"

He smiled.

"Because your machine is better than mine."

Zuri laughed.

"No, it isn't."

"It could be."

Labonator appeared from behind the door.

"And because he secretly loves old vehicles!"

Dantez threw a rag at him.

Labonator ducked.

Zuri smiled for the first time all day.

Then Sefu hurried into the shed, waving his phone.

"Zuri! Someone has posted another picture."

She froze.

"Of what?"

Sefu turned the screen.

It showed a man carrying a box out of Garage Zero.

The man wore a pale jacket.

His face was partly hidden by the door.

On the side of the box was a faded painted zero.

Phylmyk leaned closer.

"Who took that?"

"A neighbour saw the old garage open and took it from across the road."

Dantez frowned.

"Was the person allowed inside?"

Sefu shook his head.

"We don't know."

Phylmyk looked at the photograph again.

This time, the mystery was not about what Zuri had taken.

It was about what somebody else had removed.

## Chapter Nine: Zero One Refuses to Cooperate

The school holiday repair fair was two days away.

Zuri wanted Zero One to make its first proper demonstration there.

Not on the public road.

Not at night.

Inside a fenced community yard, with Reva, Kariz and a qualified mechanic checking every moving part.

Dantez liked the plan.

Labonator liked it even more.

"Imagine," he said, "if Zero One succeeds and we build Zero Two."

"Why?" Zuri asked.

"Because it will be twice as good."

"That's not how numbering works," Vanny Boy said.

"Don't ruin the dream."

At the inspection yard, the mechanics examined the brakes, steering, tyres and cargo platform.

The first safety check found a damaged component.

The test was postponed.

Zuri stared at the ground.

"Again?"

Kariz shook his head.

"Better to discover a problem before moving than after."

She nodded.

But her eyes filled with tears.

Dantez stood beside her.

"We can repair it."

"Maybe there's always going to be another problem."

"Then we fix one at a time."

Zuri turned toward him.

"Are you saying that because you believe it or because you think that's what a nice person says?"

Dantez looked surprised.

He thought carefully.

"Both."

She laughed despite herself.

The mechanic found a suitable replacement part, and Reva supervised the work. The next inspection was scheduled for the following morning.

Meanwhile, Labonator had prepared a banner.

It was enormous.

**ZERO ONE: THE LEGEND RETURNS!**

Zuri stared at it.

"That's a bit much."

"A bit much? This is history!"

"It's a cargo tricycle."

"A LEGENDARY cargo tricycle."

Mdavido approached carrying a roll of tape.

"Shouldn't you wait until it works?"

Labonator looked offended.

"Optimism is free."

Dantez smiled.

"Repair parts aren't."

The next morning, the vehicle passed its checks.

Zuri stood beside the controls while Reva explained the test procedure.

Kariz watched.

Biko watched.

Mzee Kito watched.

Dantez and Labonator watched.

And Lucanji watched from a safe seat beside Wadani, eating a mandazi.

Zuri began the demonstration.

The tricycle moved forward.

Slowly.

One metre.

Two.

Three.

Then it stopped.

Labonator gasped.

"IT'S DEAD!"

Zuri checked the controls under Reva's supervision.

The mechanic inspected the system.

A minor connection had come loose during the test.

It was repaired and checked again.

Zuri tried once more.

Zero One rolled forward across the fenced yard.

Then turned.

Then stopped exactly where it was supposed to.

For a moment, everyone stood silent.

Then Labonator screamed.

"IT LIVES!"

Mdavido jumped from his chair.

"WELCOME BACK, ZERO ONE!"

Wadani clapped.

Reva put her hands over her face.

Biko smiled so broadly that his eyes almost disappeared.

Zuri stepped down.

She looked at her aunt.

"Did it work?"

Reva hugged her.

"It worked."

Dantez stood nearby, clapping.

Then Labonator put his arm around him.

"Admit it. This is better than your delivery trick."

"Much better."

"Because it didn't involve stealing books!"

"Are you going to mention that forever?"

"Yes."

They laughed.

Zuri turned toward the old building beyond the fence.

Garage Zero remained damaged.

Its ownership still needed resolution.

The mysterious man in the photograph was still unidentified.

And an important box had apparently been removed.

But Zero One had moved under its own power.

For the first time, Zuri believed she might be able to build something that helped her town.

Then Reva's phone rang.

She listened.

Her smile disappeared.

"Yes."

A pause.

"Tomorrow?"

She put down the phone.

Biko turned.

"What happened?"

Reva looked at the children.

"The property-use hearing has been moved forward."

"To when?"

"Tomorrow morning."

Labonator stared at the banner.

"Before we even get to show the tricycle?"

Reva shook her head.

"The tricycle can still be demonstrated. But the committee needs to decide what happens to Garage Zero."

Zuri looked at Dantez.

Dantez looked at Phylmyk.

And Phylmyk did what she always did when things became complicated.

She opened her notebook.

**CASE: THE PHANTOM GARAGE**

**NEXT QUESTION: WHO WANTS GARAGE ZERO EMPTY?**
