## Chapter Four: Zuri and the Broken Thunder

Zuri did not look like a ghost.

She looked like somebody who had spent the morning losing an argument with an engine.

Her hair was tied in a scarf. There was a smear of grease across her cheek. A small cut on one knuckle had been carefully covered with a plaster.

She stood outside a low brick shed beside Garage Zero, with Reva supervising the visit.

Behind her was a strange three-wheeled vehicle.

Its front resembled an oversized bicycle. The back was a small cargo platform with a metal cover. On the side, faded white paint read:

**ZERO ONE.**

Labonator gasped.

"Oh, I love it."

Dantez circled the vehicle slowly.

"Who built this?"

"Biko and Aunt Reva," Zuri said. "I repaired the frame, replaced the old cables and rebuilt the wheel bearings."

"All by yourself?"

"With help. My aunt checked the important parts."

Dantez studied the wheels.

"That's clever."

Zuri looked at him carefully.

"Are you being serious?"

"Yes."

"People usually say it looks ridiculous."

"People usually don't know what they're looking at."

For the first time, Zuri smiled.

Labonator was already imagining Zero One painted blue.

"Imagine delivering books with this! You could put a speaker on it and shout 'READING HAS ARRIVED!'"

Mdavido grinned.

"Please don't let him near the speaker."

Reva took the children around the safe shed. She had locked the damaged main building and put a warning sign on its gate.

"We're not entering the old structure until the inspector clears it," she said.

Phylmyk nodded. She opened her notebook.

"Zuri, why do people think there's a phantom mechanic?"

Zuri looked embarrassed.

"Because I used to practise here after school. Sometimes I stayed until dark. The lamp made shadows against the old garage wall."

"And the engine noises?"

"Zero One. It doesn't run reliably."

"Why work so late?"

"Because I didn't want everyone watching me fail."

Dantez looked at her.

He understood that feeling more than he wanted to admit.

"Can I show you something?" Zuri asked.

She lifted the seat cover of Zero One, exposing a small compartment containing tools and a folded list.

"These are all the parts I've used. My aunt asked me to write down where each one came from."

Vanny Boy inspected the list.

"Excellent traceability."

Mdavido leaned toward Phylmyk.

"Translation?"

"Good records," Phylmyk said.

Vanny nodded.

"Precisely."

Zuri looked at the group.

"So if anyone says I stole parts, ask them which parts. I'll show you the receipts."

Labonator put a hand over his heart.

"Respect."

Then Sefu emerged from the shed carrying a coil of cable.

"Zuri, the bell rang again."

Zuri froze.

"What bell?" Dantez asked.

"The one inside the old garage."

Reva frowned.

"That bell hasn't worked in years."

Sefu shook his head.

"I heard it last night."

"At what time?" Phylmyk asked.

Sefu looked at Dantez.

"Nine seventeen."

Silence.

Zuri bit her lip.

"We thought it was the wind."

Biko, who had accompanied Reva, stared at the damaged building.

"That building used to have a signal bell. But it was not supposed to ring from the engine room."

"Why not?" Nia asked.

"Because the bell was mounted in the office."

Dantez slowly turned toward Garage Zero.

A bell ringing from the wrong room.

A radio nobody remembered owning.

A blue light that kept appearing.

He looked at Phylmyk.

"Okay. Now it's interesting."

Zuri shook her head.

"It is not interesting when people say I'm stealing and the council might close my workshop."

Dantez looked at her.

He put his notebook away.

"You're right. I'm sorry."

Zuri's expression softened.

Then the door to the damaged building moved slightly.

Everyone turned.

A small brown cat emerged through a gap beneath the door.

It carried something shiny in its mouth.

Lucanji gasped.

"THE PHANTOM!"

The cat dropped a metal washer and raced beneath a fence.

Mdavido laughed so hard he had to sit down.

"THE GHOST IS A CAT!"

Vanny looked at the washer.

"That does not explain the repeated acoustic events."

Mdavido wiped his eyes.

"Vanny, let us enjoy one victory."

## Chapter Five: The Man Who Sold Empty Spaces

The man from Sandona arrived in a polished white car.

He parked outside the shed, stepped carefully around a puddle and looked at his shoes with deep disappointment.

"My name is Mr. Mavua," he announced.

Nobody had asked.

He wore a cream jacket, carried an expensive folder and had the nervous smile of somebody who needed other people to agree with him.

Reva stepped forward.

"What do you want?"

"To discuss an exciting opportunity."

"Then discuss it."

Mavua glanced at the children.

"Perhaps privately."

"These are my guests."

He cleared his throat.

"Very well. The property has been unused for years. I represent an investor interested in purchasing the site for a modern vehicle-storage facility."

"Purchasing it from whom?" Biko asked.

"The legal owner, of course."

"Who is the legal owner?"

Mavua opened his folder.

"This is subject to confirmation."

Reva stared.

"You've been offering to buy land without confirming who owns it?"

"Madam, the paperwork is still being processed."

Phylmyk wrote something in her notebook.

**Interesting expression: 'still being processed.'**

Mavua pointed at Garage Zero.

"This building is a hazard. It will cost money to repair. I can arrange a proper clearance and offer generous compensation for the obsolete equipment."

"Obsolete?" Zuri repeated.

He glanced at Zero One.

"That old contraption, for example."

Labonator gasped as though someone had insulted his grandmother.

"CONTRAPTION?"

Dantez caught his arm.

"Let him finish."

Mavua smiled.

"My clients have no use for old bicycles."

"It's a cargo tricycle," Zuri said.

"A cargo tricycle. Of course."

"Then please say it correctly."

Mavua's smile slipped.

Reva stepped forward.

"Until the ownership and land-use records are confirmed, I will not sign anything."

"Then you may lose the offer."

"That is a risk I can live with."

Mavua closed his folder.

"Very well. But the workshop cannot stay like this forever."

He walked toward his car.

Then paused.

"Oh. One more thing."

"What?" Biko asked.

"I would advise you to stop discussing ghost stories with the children. It makes the property look ridiculous."

Phylmyk watched him drive away.

"How did he know about the ghost stories?"

"Half of Gema knows," Mdavido replied.

"True."

Amani, who had come with her father, examined a copy of the proposed purchase letter that Mavua had given Reva.

"There's no parcel number," she said.

Mr. Barasa nodded.

"And no confirmed seller. It's an expression of interest, not evidence of ownership."

Dantez looked embarrassed.

Last year, he might have assumed the expensive car and neat suit meant Mavua knew everything.

Now he knew better.

Reva folded the letter.

"We need the original records."

Biko rubbed his forehead.

"We searched for them after the flood. All we found was the old equipment register."

"Where are the records supposed to be?" Phylmyk asked.

"In the community committee archive. Or with the lawyer who prepared the agreement."

"Who's the lawyer?"

Reva thought.

"A woman named Mrs. Ndeda. She retired several years ago."

Wadani, who had joined them, raised her hand.

"I remember her."

Everybody turned.

"She came to Wakibe once and bought thirty packets of tea leaves for a meeting."

Mdavido blinked.

"How does that help?"

Wadani gave him a look.

"It doesn't. I'm remembering."

Lucanji nodded.

"Memory is important."

"Thank you, Lucanji."

Then Wadani snapped her fingers.

"Wait! Mrs. Ndeda gave the committee a copy of the agreement. I saw a blue folder in Kariz's office during the floods."

Kariz groaned.

"Please don't tell me I have misplaced another important document."

Labonator folded his arms.

"You said it, not me."

Phylmyk closed her notebook.

"Then we're going back to Kwa Kariz."

Dantez looked at Zuri.

"Meanwhile, keep your parts records safe."

She nodded.

"Thank you."

He smiled.

"Don't thank me yet. I haven't fixed anything."

Zuri grinned.

"At least you're admitting it."

## Chapter Six: The Night They Stayed Away

At nine o'clock, the group gathered at Wakibe.

Not at Garage Zero.

Wadani had insisted.

"I've had enough of children running toward danger because they saw something shining," she told them.

Phylmyk looked offended.

"We don't always do that."

Yobra raised one eyebrow.

"Do you want a list?"

"No."

Across town, Reva, Biko, Kariz and the safety officer were meeting an authorised building inspector near Garage Zero. An electrician was there to examine the supply equipment from outside the damaged section.

The adults had agreed to report what they found.

The children had been asked to remain somewhere safe.

Lucanji thought it was wonderful.

"Wadani has biscuits."

Mdavido opened his novel.

"Finally. A detective assignment I understand."

Vanny Boy arranged old photographs on the counter.

Amani helped him compare the route maps.

Dantez stared at his phone.

Labonator stared at Dantez.

"Stop looking at it."

"At what?"

"Your phone."

"I'm expecting a message."

"From who?"

"The adults."

"Then looking every three seconds won't make them send it faster."

Dantez sighed.

Phylmyk glanced at the clock.

9:16.

One minute.

She felt a ridiculous urge to run to the door.

Then she remembered Zuri's face.

*It isn't interesting when people say I'm stealing.*

She stayed seated.

At 9:17 the phone rang.

Everybody jumped.

Wadani picked up.

"Hello?"

She listened.

Then laughed.

"Yes, Kariz. They're all here. No, none of them has escaped."

Phylmyk rolled her eyes.

Wadani put the call on speaker with Kariz's permission.

"All right," Kariz said. "We have some answers."

"What happened?" Dantez demanded.

"The blue light came on at 9:17. The electrician found a timer connected to an old battery lamp in the permitted equipment shelter. It appears to have been set to switch on at the same time every night."

"Who set it?" Phylmyk asked.

"We don't know yet."

"And the bell?"

"The inspector heard it. Wind was moving a loose metal plate against an old bell bracket. No one was ringing it."

Mdavido grinned.

"Ghost defeated."

"Not entirely," Kariz said.

Everyone froze.

"What do you mean?" Dantez asked.

"We also heard a voice from inside the damaged building."

Silence.

"What voice?" Phylmyk whispered.

"A recorded voice, coming from an old battery radio on a shelf. It repeated the same words twice."

Biko spoke in the background.

"Route Three, can you hear me?"

Phylmyk's skin prickled.

"That's what the first report said!"

Kariz continued.

"The inspector and safety officer will document the equipment. Nobody is going inside tonight. We'll retrieve anything relevant only when the building is declared safe for entry."

Dantez leaned forward.

"Was the radio connected to the timer?"

"The electrician thinks both were connected to an old training display. He needs to examine it before we know."

The call ended.

Wadani put down the phone.

"Well. No ghosts. Just old machines."

Vanny tilted his head.

"Yet the synchronised activation and recorded communication suggest deliberate preservation of a historical signal procedure."

Mdavido looked at him.

"There's still a clue."

"Correct."

Dantez sat quietly.

He was looking at the old Zero One drawing.

On its side, beneath the name, was a tiny square shape.

He enlarged the photo Zuri had sent him earlier.

A small blue box.

It was fixed behind the cargo seat.

He frowned.

Then he noticed the same box in a photograph taken four years earlier.

He showed Phylmyk.

"Look."

She leaned closer.

"What?"

"The blue box has been there since the first prototype."

"So?"

"Why would someone tell the person in the radio to leave it where it is?"

Phylmyk stared at the picture.

"Maybe it contains something."

Labonator stood.

"We can ask Zuri in the morning."

Dantez nodded.

No sneaking out.

No ghost hunt.

No dangerous midnight heroics.

He smiled faintly.

"Tomorrow."

At that moment, an unknown number sent him a message.

**THE BLUE BOX DOES NOT BELONG TO ZURI.**

Beneath it was a photograph of Zero One.

Taken earlier that afternoon.

Dantez felt the excitement fade.

Someone was still watching.

And whoever it was knew far too much.
