# THE SHADOW RIDERZ
## Book Two: The Phantom Garage

**VibeSchool Originals · Gema Story Universe · Text-only edition**

*Everyone said Garage Zero was empty. So who kept fixing the machines inside?*

---

## Prologue: The Light at 9:17

At 9:17 on a wet Thursday night, a blue light came on in a building that had been locked for four years.

It was not a bright light. It was barely enough to touch the cracked windows, the dusty floor and the rusting sign above the door.

**GARAGE ZERO.**

Then an engine coughed.

Once.

Twice.

A third time.

A passing boda boda rider slowed, decided he had not heard anything, and accelerated so quickly that his loose raincoat flapped behind him like a frightened bat.

Inside, a shadow moved across the wall.

Someone dragged a wooden crate toward a workbench.

A small radio crackled.

"Testing," a voice whispered. "Route Three, can you hear me?"

Static answered.

The shadow tried again.

"Route Three?"

A low voice came through the speaker.

"I can hear you. Leave the blue box where it is."

"I found the papers."

"Did you read them?"

"Only the first page."

"Then you know why the door must stay closed."

A gust of wind rattled the roof.

The figure looked toward the entrance.

Outside, somebody was standing beneath an umbrella.

Watching.

The radio hissed.

"Don't let them take it."

"Who?"

The reply was almost swallowed by thunder.

"The person who wants everyone to believe this place is haunted."

Then the radio went dead.

At 9:18, the blue light disappeared.

By morning, all of Gema would be talking about ghosts.

And at Kwa Kariz, two boys would be arguing about a football.

## Chapter One: My Car Has Developed Feelings

Labonator loved his blue car.

He cleaned it every Saturday, even when it did not need cleaning.

He checked its tyres as carefully as a doctor checking a patient.

He had given it a name.

*Princess Thunder.*

Dantez had told him that was the worst name ever given to a machine.

Labonator had responded by refusing to speak to him for eleven minutes.

Now the two boys stood in Kwa Kariz, staring at a note somebody had pushed beneath the garage office door.

**GARAGE ZERO IS OPEN.**

Underneath were three smaller words.

**NINE. SEVENTEEN. TONIGHT.**

Dantez studied the paper.

"Nine seventeen. That's oddly specific."

Labonator looked at his watch.

"It's nine seventeen somewhere."

"That isn't how time works."

"Vanny would understand me."

"Vanny would probably file a complaint against that sentence."

Kariz walked past carrying a tray of bolts.

"What are you two whispering about?"

Dantez showed him the note.

Kariz stopped so abruptly that two bolts fell to the ground.

"Where did you get that?"

"Someone left it here," Dantez said. "Is Garage Zero a real place?"

Kariz looked from the note to the workshop gate.

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Near the old road to Shabe."

"Why is it called Zero?"

Kariz picked up the bolts one by one.

"Because it was the first workshop Biko and Reva used before they helped build this place."

"Then why is it closed?"

Kariz straightened.

"Because the roof was damaged. Because the owners disagreed about the lease. Because sometimes a place closes and should stay closed."

Labonator nodded solemnly.

"Like my grandmother's biscuit tin."

Kariz stared at him.

"What?"

"She closes it and says it must stay closed."

"Labonator," Dantez said, "please."

Kariz took the note and read it again.

"Whoever sent this is trying to draw somebody there. You're not going."

"We weren't planning to," Dantez said.

Labonator gave him a look.

Kariz noticed.

"I mean it. The building was condemned for public use after the flood. I'll speak to Biko and the local office."

He walked away.

Labonator leaned closer to Dantez.

"You were planning to."

"No."

"You had that face."

"What face?"

"Your 'I have an extremely bad idea' face."

Dantez folded his arms.

"I was thinking."

"Exactly."

Before Dantez could reply, Mdavido rolled into the garage on his skateboard.

"Attention, citizens! Bonde is haunted."

Labonator laughed.

"Bonde has been haunted by unpaid electricity bills for years."

"I'm serious. Mama Sia said someone heard an engine running in Garage Zero after dark."

Dantez held up the note.

Mdavido examined it.

"Oh."

"Oh what?"

"Oh, I'm going to tell Phylmyk."

"Don't."

Mdavido was already typing.

"Too late."

Five minutes later, Phylmyk arrived with Yobra, Lucanji and Vanny Boy.

Phylmyk's cap was crooked.

She was eating a mandazi.

Dantez stared.

"You're eating during an investigation?"

"Detectives need breakfast."

"It's almost lunchtime."

"Then I'm early for lunch."

Lucanji held out a biscuit.

"I support this policy."

Yobra examined the note and looked at the old road beyond the garage.

"Garage Zero wasn't haunted when we were children," he said.

"Did you ever go inside?" Phylmyk asked.

"Once. It smelled like petrol, wet wood and old shoes."

Mdavido shuddered.

"Definitely haunted."

Vanny Boy peered at the paper.

"The timing indicates a deliberately specified rendezvous or recurring event, but no causal relationship to supernatural activity can be established."

Labonator nodded.

"Translation: ghost may be on a schedule."

"No," Vanny replied. "Precisely the opposite."

Everyone laughed except Kariz, who returned carrying a phone.

He had just spoken to Biko.

"Biko says somebody else has reported lights at the workshop."

"So what do we do?" Phylmyk asked.

"We ask the people responsible for the property and let the safety officer know. And you children stay away from an unsafe building."

Dantez looked toward the road again.

For once, he agreed.

But somebody was watching Kwa Kariz from the opposite pavement.

A boy on a bicycle, wearing a green raincoat.

When Dantez met his eyes, the boy rode away.

Across his backpack was a white painted number.

**0.**

## Chapter Two: The Boy Who Delivered Nothing

The rider in the green raincoat appeared at Wakibe the following morning.

Phylmyk spotted him first.

He was standing beside the biscuit shelf, arguing with Wadani.

"I didn't order a parcel," Wadani said.

"I'm not delivering a parcel."

"Then why are you holding a delivery bag?"

"Because it's my schoolbag."

Wadani stared.

The boy looked about fourteen. His hair was shaved close to his head, and his bicycle was covered in small reflectors.

Phylmyk approached.

"What's your name?"

The boy looked suspicious.

"Why?"

"Because I'm asking."

"Do you ask everybody?"

"Almost."

"I'm Sefu."

"From?"

"Shabe."

"Where did you get that zero on your bag?"

Sefu became still.

He glanced at the door.

"Can we talk outside?"

Wadani folded her arms.

"You can talk right here. I don't want anyone whispering behind my shop again."

Sefu put his bag on the counter and removed a folded sheet of paper.

It was a drawing of a three-wheeled bicycle with a large metal box at the back.

On the side were the words:

**ZERO ONE.**

Dantez, who had just arrived, leaned closer.

"What's that?"

"An old delivery tricycle," Sefu said. "My sister is trying to repair it."

"Your sister?"

"Zuri."

"Who is Zuri?"

Sefu hesitated.

"The person you're calling the phantom mechanic."

Mdavido, entering behind them, stopped so suddenly that his skateboard struck the doorway.

"I KNEW IT!"

Wadani turned.

"Knew what?"

"There's a phantom!"

Sefu rolled his eyes.

"There's a mechanic."

Dantez took the drawing.

"Where is this tricycle?"

"Garage Zero."

"That's an unsafe building," Yobra said, arriving with Phylmyk.

"I know. She doesn't work inside the damaged part. She's been using a small shed beside it."

"At night?" Phylmyk asked.

Sefu looked embarrassed.

"Sometimes."

"Why?"

"Because people laugh at her."

That silenced the group.

Sefu continued.

"Zuri repairs bicycles and simple electrical things. She's really good. But every time she tries to help at a workshop, somebody says she's a girl and should go and sell clothes."

Dantez looked annoyed.

"That's stupid."

"Exactly."

Sefu folded his arms.

"So she kept practising in private. She found some old parts our aunt had stored and made a little workspace beside Garage Zero."

"Who gave her permission?" Yobra asked.

"Our aunt. She used to own part of the place."

Dantez suddenly remembered something Kariz had said.

"Biko and Reva built it."

Sefu nodded.

"Reva is our aunt."

Phylmyk studied him.

"Then who's making the engine noises inside?"

"Old Zero One. It starts, coughs, then stops. Zuri is trying to fix it properly."

"And the blue light?"

"A battery lamp."

"And the radio?"

Sefu blinked.

"What radio?"

Phylmyk looked at Dantez.

"Someone heard a radio there."

Sefu shook his head.

"Zuri doesn't have a working radio."

Dantez put down the drawing.

"So not all the stories came from her."

"No."

Sefu reached into his bag again.

This time he took out a torn page from an old notebook.

On it was a message written in neat handwriting.

**IF THE TRICYCLE RUNS, GARAGE ZERO BELONGS TO GEMA.**

Dantez frowned.

"What does that mean?"

Sefu looked toward the road.

"That's what Zuri wants to find out."

Phylmyk put her hand on the paper.

"Has anyone threatened your sister?"

"Not exactly."

"What do you mean?"

Sefu swallowed.

"Someone offered to buy the tricycle."

"Who?"

"An agent from Sandona."

"Did she sell it?"

"No."

"What happened?"

"He said if she didn't sell, he would make sure Garage Zero was cleared and everything inside went for scrap."

Wadani placed a hand on Sefu's shoulder.

"Have your aunt speak to Kariz and the local office. She shouldn't deal with threats by herself."

Sefu nodded.

Then he looked at Dantez.

"Can you help her?"

Dantez studied the drawing again.

A three-wheeled delivery machine.

Old batteries.

A tiny cargo platform.

And a bright blue light.

For the first time in days, he felt something stronger than curiosity.

He wanted to build.

But the real mystery wasn't the machine.

It was the note.

*If the tricycle runs, Garage Zero belongs to Gema.*

## Chapter Three: A Garage That Should Not Exist

Reva arrived at Kwa Kariz shortly after midday.

She was tall, with strong arms, silver earrings and a voice that could stop a noisy mechanic in the middle of an argument.

Kariz recognised her immediately.

"Reva."

"Kariz."

They stared at each other.

Labonator whispered, "Are they going to fight?"

Mdavido whispered back, "I hope not. I forgot to bring popcorn."

Reva laughed unexpectedly.

"I can hear you, boys."

Both straightened.

She sat beside Biko, who had come after Kariz called him.

For a while they talked about the old days.

Then Phylmyk asked the question everyone had been avoiding.

"Who owns Garage Zero?"

Reva looked at Biko.

"We owned the business together."

"And the building?" Dantez asked.

"That's complicated."

Kariz sighed.

"Not again."

Reva continued.

"When we opened Garage Zero, the local community committee helped us with the land. The agreement was that we could use the building for repairs and provide affordable services for community projects."

"So it wasn't just yours?" Yobra asked.

"No. It was meant to benefit the town."

Biko took out a folder.

"We kept the records in the old office. When the flood damaged the building, some papers were lost."

"And somebody wants to buy it now?" Phylmyk asked.

Reva nodded.

"An agent has offered to clear the site. He says the workshop is abandoned and no longer serves anyone."

Sefu stood.

"But Zuri is using it!"

Reva looked at him gently.

"Using a small shed for practice is not the same as making an unsafe old garage suitable for public work."

Sefu looked down.

"She knows."

"Then why hide it?" Dantez asked.

Reva hesitated.

"Because Zuri believes the old delivery tricycle can prove the site is still useful. And because she is worried nobody will listen to her."

Labonator folded his arms.

"Why wouldn't they listen?"

Reva gave him a look.

He thought about it.

Then lowered his eyes.

"Oh."

Biko opened the folder.

"There was also a second agreement. If the workshop stopped operating, the equipment designed for community delivery was supposed to be offered to a local volunteer programme, not sold privately."

"Zero One," Dantez said.

Reva nodded.

"The first machine we built for carrying books and small supplies along narrow roads."

"Did it work?"

"Sometimes," Biko said.

Reva laughed.

"Mostly when Biko wasn't touching it."

"That is unfair."

"Your first test ended in a ditch."

"The ditch was poorly positioned!"

Labonator burst out laughing.

Dantez leaned over the design.

"Can it be repaired?"

"With proper parts and an adult supervisor, yes," Reva said. "But proving a machine can run doesn't settle who legally owns a property."

Phylmyk nodded.

"So the note was misleading."

"Perhaps," Reva said. "Or it was written by somebody who wanted the old project remembered."

Vanny Boy had been silent.

Now he lifted his head.

"There is a material difference between a machine's operational viability and a property's legal tenure."

Mdavido smiled.

"Working engine doesn't equal land ownership."

"Precisely."

Kariz looked at Reva.

"Have you seen the note?"

She examined it.

Then shook her head.

"Not my handwriting."

Biko examined it too.

"Not mine."

"Then whose?" Phylmyk asked.

Nobody answered.

At that moment, Kariz's phone rang.

He listened.

His expression changed.

"Yes. I understand."

He ended the call.

"There's been another complaint about Garage Zero."

Reva looked up.

"What kind?"

"The local office received a report that somebody has been stealing parts from nearby workshops."

Dantez frowned.

"And they're blaming Garage Zero?"

Kariz nodded.

Sefu jumped to his feet.

"Zuri didn't steal anything!"

Reva pulled him gently back into his chair.

"We don't know what happened yet."

Kariz looked at the others.

"Then we find out. Properly."

Outside, rain began to fall.

And at the edge of Shabe, a blue light flickered once behind a cracked garage window.
