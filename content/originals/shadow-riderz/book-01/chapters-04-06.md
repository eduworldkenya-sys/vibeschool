## Chapter Four: Bonde Has Its Own Heroes

When people in Gema heard the word Bonde, some thought of broken drains, shouting and trouble.

Mdavido thought of Mama Sia's bookstall.

Lucanji thought of a woman who sold the best roasted maize in town.

And Nia thought of somebody who knew where the real riders had gone four years ago.

They met Mama Sia outside her stall just after lunch.

The old woman was arranging a stack of adventure books.

"Ah," she said, noticing Phylmyk. "The detective has brought her whole department."

"Today we're not here to investigate books," Phylmyk replied.

"That's a shame. Books are the only things in this town that behave themselves."

Mdavido pointed to a novel.

"How much?"

"Thirty shillings."

"Twenty?"

"Thirty."

"Twenty-five?"

Mama Sia folded her arms.

"Young man, are you buying a book or negotiating a government contract?"

Labonator laughed.

Mdavido paid thirty.

Then Nia held up the old route photograph.

"Mama Sia, do you recognise these volunteers?"

Mama Sia adjusted her glasses.

"Of course. Kito from Tamtam. Baraka from Shabe. Reva from Langa. Old Mwangi. And that tall one—"

She stopped.

"What?" Dantez asked.

"That tall one moved away."

"What's his name?"

"Biko."

"Where did he go?"

"I don't know. He used to repair radios."

Nia nodded. "My father said Biko handled messages."

Phylmyk took notes.

"Not deliveries?"

"Sometimes deliveries. Mostly communications," Mama Sia replied. "When the floods came, telephone networks were unreliable in some places. Biko set up a small community message desk."

"Was he honest?" Dantez asked.

Mama Sia gave him a sharp look.

"Young man, don't ask an old woman to turn a person's life into one word."

Dantez lowered his eyes.

"Sorry."

"People can make mistakes and still do good things. Now, what do you really want?"

Phylmyk showed her the black helmet.

Mama Sia examined the symbol.

"This is not the old riders' badge."

"We know."

"Then whoever made it wants you to confuse the two."

She pointed down Bonde.

"There's a small noticeboard beside the community hall. The electricity people posted something there yesterday."

"An outage notice?" Dantez asked.

"Yes. They said planned repair work would affect several places in Gema."

The children hurried to the noticeboard.

The notice listed Bonde, Shabe, Tamtam and Kwa Kariz.

It also listed times when brief interruptions were expected.

Dantez read it twice.

Then slowly lowered the paper.

"Seven minutes."

Labonator leaned closer.

"What?"

"The message sender didn't predict a blackout. They knew about scheduled maintenance."

Phylmyk smiled. "So the electricity was never a supernatural clue."

"I never said supernatural."

"Your face did."

Mdavido laughed.

Nia frowned.

"But whoever wrote our list also knew the timings."

"Because anyone could read this notice," Yobra replied.

They stood quietly for a moment.

Some of the mystery had disappeared.

And that felt good.

Phylmyk looked at Dantez.

"See? Investigating ordinary explanations matters."

He nodded.

Then something caught his eye.

At the bottom of the notice, someone had taped a second, smaller paper.

**SPECIAL DELIVERY — BONDE COMMUNITY HALL — 7:07 PM.**

The black motorcycle symbol appeared beneath it.

Dantez stared.

"Now that," he said, "is not on the maintenance schedule."

## Chapter Five: The Wrong Package

At seven o'clock, Bonde Community Hall was full of people.

Not because of the Shadow Riderz.

Because Mama Sia had organised an evening reading club.

Children sat on plastic chairs.

Parents stood near the entrance.

Two teachers had brought books.

A small solar lamp illuminated the front table.

Phylmyk and the others had told the local safety officer about the suspicious delivery notice. He was nearby with the hall caretaker.

Nobody wanted to interrupt the reading session over a message that might be nothing.

Dantez sat at the back with Labonator.

A little girl was reading aloud from a story about a river.

She stumbled over a long word.

Vanny Boy, sitting beside Mdavido, leaned forward.

"The term is *extraordinary*."

The girl tried again.

"Ex-tra-or-di-na-ry."

"Very good," Vanny said.

Mdavido whispered, "Your turn. You say something simple."

Vanny looked puzzled.

"I just did."

"You're improving."

At 7:07 a white delivery van stopped outside the hall.

The driver got out and approached the caretaker.

He carried a brown parcel.

Dantez stood.

"So it was real."

Labonator grabbed his arm.

"Don't do anything stupid."

"I wasn't going to."

"Your face says otherwise."

The caretaker checked the label.

**BONDE COMMUNITY READING CLUB**

The sender was listed as **GEMA COMMUNITY SUPPLIES**.

Inside, after the safety officer confirmed it was safe to handle, were twenty reading lamps and a signed donation letter.

Mama Sia read the letter aloud.

The lamps were a gift from a local community group.

The audience clapped.

Lucanji clapped loudest.

"More lights!"

Phylmyk smiled.

But Dantez didn't.

He had noticed something odd.

The delivery driver had asked the caretaker to sign two documents.

One was the delivery receipt.

The other was a blank page.

The caretaker refused the second.

"Why should I sign a blank sheet?" he asked.

The driver looked embarrassed.

"It's probably been included by mistake."

He removed it from the clipboard.

Dantez and Phylmyk exchanged a glance.

A blank form.

A delivery of educational equipment.

The old Gema mystery had started with missing signatures and altered records.

Dantez approached the driver with the officer nearby.

"Excuse me. Who sent the lamps?"

The driver pointed at the label.

"It's from a community group."

"Do you know their contact?"

"The organiser's number should be on the letter."

Phylmyk looked.

There was a phone number.

Wadani, standing near the front, recognised it.

"That's the Gema Readers Foundation office," she said. "A real donation."

The driver had made a paperwork mistake, but the lamps appeared genuine.

Dantez felt foolish.

Again.

The safety officer took the details and reminded the caretaker to keep copies of completed documents.

The reading club continued.

For a while, everything seemed normal.

Then a message arrived on Dantez's phone.

**WRONG PACKAGE.**

He looked up sharply.

Another message followed.

**THE REAL DELIVERY IS FOR YOU.**

He showed Labonator.

Labonator sighed.

"Of course it is."

At the far end of the hall, a little boy approached them carrying a folded piece of paper.

"Are you Dantez?"

"Yes."

"A woman outside said I should give you this."

Phylmyk immediately asked the boy to stay inside with his teacher.

Yobra went to speak to the officer.

The paper contained no threats.

Only an address.

**SANDONA COMMUNITY LIBRARY — 10:00 A.M.**

And one sentence.

**BRING THE BLACK HELMET.**

Dantez stared at it.

A public place.

A daytime meeting.

At last, the person behind the messages wanted to talk.

But why hide behind a child to send the invitation?

Labonator looked at Dantez.

"We're not going alone."

Dantez nodded.

"Agreed."

Phylmyk looked surprised.

Mdavido whispered, "He's growing up."

Dantez heard him.

"Not that much."

## Chapter Six: The Woman with the Silver Notebook

The next morning, the Sandona Community Library was busy.

Students studied at the tables. A librarian was arranging books on a trolley. Amani was helping her father with a community finance workshop.

Dantez arrived with Labonator, Phylmyk, Yobra and Nia.

Kariz and the safety officer knew about the meeting.

At ten o'clock, the woman from the sixth-floor office entered.

She wore a dark jacket and carried a silver notebook.

Nia noticed the notebook first.

"That's her," she whispered.

"How do you know?" Dantez asked.

"My father received a letter from a woman carrying a notebook like that. He said she was asking about the old riders."

The woman approached.

"My name is Mara."

Dantez folded his arms.

"You're the one sending messages."

"Some of them."

"Some?"

She sat opposite him.

"I work for a youth technology programme that was being developed with Tomorrow Holdings."

Phylmyk leaned forward.

"What programme?"

"A plan to recruit young inventors and mechanics to learn practical skills."

"Then why call yourselves the Shadow Riderz?" Nia demanded.

Mara hesitated.

"The name was chosen by the people funding the programme."

"You didn't ask the five volunteers for permission?"

"No."

"Then you shouldn't have used it."

Mara looked uncomfortable.

"I agree."

Dantez placed the black helmet on the table.

"You called me Rider Six."

"Yes."

"You offered Vanny a place."

"Yes."

"And you photographed children without asking them."

Mara looked down.

"I was told it was part of a talent assessment."

Phylmyk's expression hardened.

"You frightened him."

"I know."

"Did you also send the warnings about the helmet?"

Mara shook her head.

"Not all of them."

Dantez felt his stomach tighten.

"What do you mean?"

She opened the silver notebook.

"At first, the messages were part of a badly designed recruitment exercise. We wanted to see whether students would solve puzzles, trust strangers or hand over information."

Labonator stared.

"That's a terrible way to recruit children."

Mara nodded. "Yes."

"What changed?" Phylmyk asked.

"Someone started using the same symbol to send messages I didn't write."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Mara took out a printed copy of the invitation she had sent to Vanny.

Then another document.

This one contained a list of tasks that had never been approved by her team.

**RETURN THE HELMET BEFORE SEVEN.**

**THE RIDER HAS ALREADY LEFT.**

**THE REAL DELIVERY IS FOR YOU.**

Dantez stared.

"Those are the messages."

Mara nodded.

"Somebody was watching our programme and copying our design."

"Then what do they want?" Phylmyk asked.

Mara turned to the last page.

It contained a photograph of the original five riders' badge.

And beneath it was a handwritten warning.

**STOP ASKING FOR THE PAST. START ASKING FOR BIKO.**

Nia leaned forward.

"Biko?"

Mara nodded.

"That's the name the stranger gave me. He said he used to belong to the riders."

Phylmyk remembered Mama Sia's story.

"The radio repairman."

"Yes."

"Where is he?"

Mara shook her head.

"I don't know."

Dantez looked at the black helmet.

"What's missing from inside?"

Mara hesitated.

"A memory card."

"What was on it?"

"An audio file. Something recorded by one of the original volunteers."

Labonator blinked.

"Why put it in a helmet?"

"It wasn't an official company record. Someone wanted me to pass it to a person outside Tomorrow Holdings without attracting attention."

"Who asked you?"

Mara took a deep breath.

"The man who appeared in our office that night."

"Was it Biko?" Nia asked.

"I never saw his face."

Dantez sat back.

The helmet wasn't a trophy.

It wasn't a recruitment prize.

It was a delivery box.

And somebody had removed the real contents before they could reach the intended person.

Phylmyk looked at the woman.

"Who was supposed to receive the recording?"

Mara looked directly at Dantez.

"You."
