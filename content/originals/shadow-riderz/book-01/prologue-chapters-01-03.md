## Prologue: The Helmet Nobody Wanted

At midnight, the lights went out in Sandona.

Not all of Sandona.

Not the big hotels. Not the shopping centre. Not the glass building belonging to Tomorrow Holdings.

Only one room on the sixth floor of that building.

Inside, a woman stood beside an empty desk, looking at a black motorcycle helmet.

A small red light blinked underneath its lining.

She tapped it with one finger.

Nothing.

The woman took out her phone and made a call.

"He refused," she said.

A man's voice answered. "Vanny Boy?"

"Yes. He didn't even consider the offer."

"Good."

She frowned. "Good? You sent me all the way to Wakibe."

"Did you tell him we understood his intelligence?"

"Yes."

"Did you offer him computers, a workshop and friends who speak his language?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"He said he already had friends."

The voice grew quiet.

Then the man laughed.

"Excellent."

The woman stared at the helmet.

"Sir, I don't understand the purpose of this."

"That makes two of us," said another voice behind her.

She turned.

The office door was open.

A man in a raincoat stood in the darkness.

He was holding a photograph taken four years earlier, when five motorcycle volunteers had carried supplies around flooded Gema.

"Who are you?" the woman demanded.

He placed the photograph on the desk.

"The person whose symbol you're using."

She looked at it.

Five motorcycles.

Five riders.

And the words:

**THREE ROUTES. FIVE RIDERS. ONE GEMA.**

The man pointed to the black helmet.

"That mark doesn't belong to Tomorrow Holdings."

"Security!"

Nobody answered.

The man stepped backward.

"I'll ask you once. Stop sending messages to children."

Then he disappeared into the corridor.

The woman grabbed the helmet and hurried to the window.

Outside, a motorcycle roared out of the underground garage.

A second followed.

Then a third.

And for the first time, she understood something.

The Shadow Riderz she thought she was recruiting for might not be the same Shadow Riderz who had been watching her.

## Chapter One: Labonator's Brilliant Disaster

Labonator was certain he had invented the future.

Dantez was certain he had invented a disaster.

They stood at Kwa Kariz, staring at a small machine made from an old dashboard clock, a speaker, several wires and a cracked plastic lunchbox.

"What is it supposed to do?" Dantez asked.

Labonator grinned.

"It reminds drivers to check their vehicles before leaving."

"And why is there a football attached to it?"

"It's not attached. It's supporting the speaker."

"The speaker is balanced on a football."

"Engineering."

Dantez folded his arms. "That's not engineering. That's gravity waiting to embarrass you."

Labonator ignored him and pressed the button.

The machine played a cheerful beep.

Then a voice shouted:

**CHECK YOUR BRAKES! CHECK YOUR TYRES! CHECK YOUR FUEL!**

Every mechanic in Kwa Kariz turned.

Kariz dropped his spanner.

"What on earth is that?"

"A safety invention!" Labonator shouted proudly.

The machine beeped again.

**CHECK YOUR BRAKES! CHECK YOUR TYRES! CHECK YOUR FUEL!**

"Turn it off!" Kariz yelled.

Labonator pressed the button.

Nothing.

He pressed it again.

The voice became louder.

**CHECK YOUR BRAKES!**

A mechanic emerged from beneath a car.

"I AM CHECKING MY BRAKES!"

**CHECK YOUR TYRES!**

"THE TYRES ARE HERE!"

**CHECK YOUR FUEL!**

"DOES THIS THING THINK I'M DEAF?"

Dantez began laughing.

Labonator tried to unplug the speaker. His elbow knocked the football. It rolled away, pulling the lunchbox off the workbench.

The machine hit the ground.

Silence.

Kariz stared at the pieces.

Labonator looked devastated.

"My invention."

Dantez patted his shoulder.

"On the bright side, you've invented silence."

"You're not helping."

"I'm providing emotional support."

"You're providing emotional damage."

From outside came a familiar voice.

"That sounds like a standard weekday at Kwa Kariz."

Phylmyk stood at the entrance wearing her detective cap. Beside her was Yobra Mninja.

Dantez rolled his eyes.

"Do detectives ever take days off?"

"Only when villains stop making trouble."

"I'm not a villain."

"Your public record is complicated."

Labonator bent to collect the broken plastic. Phylmyk helped him.

For a moment, they worked quietly.

Then Dantez's phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

He looked at the screen.

**THE BLACK HELMET IS NOT YOURS.**

He frowned.

Another message followed.

**RETURN IT BEFORE SEVEN.**

Phylmyk noticed his face.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Labonator looked up.

"Don't start."

Dantez sighed and showed them.

At the bottom was an image of the familiar motorcycle rider inside a circle.

Yobra became serious.

"Keep the messages. We'll show Kariz."

"Already done," Dantez said. "And the safety officer knows about the earlier ones."

Phylmyk nodded.

"Good."

Dantez looked toward the workbench, where the black helmet had been sitting since the previous night.

It was gone.

He stared.

"Labonator."

"What?"

"Where's the helmet?"

Labonator looked around.

"It was right there."

Phylmyk checked the bench.

A note lay beneath a spanner.

**THE RIDER HAS ALREADY LEFT.**

Kariz came over and read it.

His expression changed.

"Close the side gate," he told one of the mechanics. "And let's check who came into the yard."

Dantez looked toward the empty bench.

For the first time that day, nobody felt like laughing.

## Chapter Two: Seven Minutes of Darkness

At 6:53 that evening, the lights went out in Bonde.

Then in Shabe.

Then along the road to Wakibe.

At exactly seven, they came back.

Except the lights at Kwa Kariz.

Those stayed off.

Kariz stood outside with a torch.

"Another outage?"

Dantez looked at his watch.

"Seven minutes."

Labonator frowned. "What?"

"The first outage lasted exactly seven minutes."

"Maybe electricity has a timetable."

"Electricity doesn't carry a wristwatch."

Before they could argue, Wadani called from Wakibe.

"Kariz, are your lights working?"

"No."

"Mine just came back."

"Good for you."

"Don't be rude. I'm asking because the freezer nearly stopped."

Kariz sighed.

A few minutes later, a utility worker arrived after the garage reported its outage. She checked the approved supply connection and told Kariz that the problem would need further work.

Nobody was allowed near the damaged electrical cabinet.

Dantez watched from a distance, thinking.

A seven-minute outage in three localities.

A second outage at the garage.

And a message demanding a helmet before seven.

It might be coincidence.

He hated coincidences.

"You're thinking again," Labonator said.

"That happens."

"It's never safe when you do it."

Dantez looked at him. "Tell me something. Who knew where we kept the helmet?"

"Kariz. You. Me. Phylmyk."

"And the mechanics."

"And Vanny."

"And every customer who came into the garage."

Labonator scratched his head.

"So everybody."

"Exactly."

"Excellent. We have narrowed it down to the whole of Gema."

Dantez almost smiled.

Then a voice came from the dark.

"You're looking at the wrong clock."

They turned.

A girl stood beneath the garage's security light, which had just flickered back on.

She looked about fifteen, with a red raincoat tied around her waist and a small notebook in her hand.

"Who are you?" Dantez asked.

"My name is Nia."

"Do we know you?"

"Not yet."

Labonator stepped forward. "Are you here about the broken safety machine? Because there's a warranty problem."

Nia stared at him.

"What?"

"Nothing," Dantez said quickly. "Ignore him."

Nia looked at the repaired lights.

"Seven minutes. Then seven again."

Phylmyk approached from the gate with Yobra.

"How do you know about the outages?"

"Because the same thing happened in Tamtam yesterday."

Dantez frowned.

"Tamtam isn't on the same street."

"No," Nia said. "But it is on the same list."

"What list?"

Nia unfolded a page.

There were four handwritten names.

**TAMTAM — SHABE — BONDE — KWA KARIZ.**

Beside each was a time.

All exactly seven minutes apart.

"Where did you get that?" Phylmyk asked.

"My father made the list."

"Who is your father?"

Nia looked toward the garage.

"One of the original five riders."

For a moment, the sound of traffic outside seemed very far away.

Then Labonator raised his hand.

"Question."

Nia turned.

"Yes?"

"Is your father the person who stole the helmet?"

She looked at him.

"No."

"Do you know who did?"

"No."

"Then why are you here?"

Nia held out the page.

"Because he says somebody is bringing the old Shadow Riderz name back."

Dantez felt his excitement return.

"And?"

"And he wants to stop them."

## Chapter Three: A Girl from Tamtam

Nia's father had been one of the five volunteers who carried medicine, food and school supplies during the great floods.

His name was Mzee Kito.

He lived near Tamtam Ridge, where he repaired bicycles and small engines under a tin-roofed shed.

He had once helped organise the safe delivery routes from Kwa Kariz.

He still kept the original route badge in a wooden box.

Nia showed them a photograph of it.

The badge had three curved roads beneath five dots.

It did not show a black motorcycle inside a circle.

Dantez stared.

"That's not our symbol."

"Exactly," Nia replied.

"Our?"

"Whoever has been sending messages used a different design."

Phylmyk took out her notebook.

"So someone borrowed the Shadow Riderz name and invented another logo."

Nia nodded.

"My father says the original volunteers never wore matching black helmets. Their helmets were whatever they could afford."

Mdavido arrived carrying a library book.

He listened for a moment.

Then pointed at Dantez.

"Have you been following a fake motorcycle club?"

Dantez glared.

"I haven't joined any club."

"Then why does your phone keep saying Rider Six?"

"Because someone thinks they're funny."

"Possibly because they met you."

Labonator laughed.

Dantez did not.

Nia continued.

"The fake messages started after the missing learning lamps were traced."

Phylmyk frowned.

"Then the person sending them might be connected to Tomorrow Holdings."

"Maybe," Nia said. "But my father won't accuse anyone without proof."

Yobra nodded approvingly.

"Good."

Dantez turned toward him.

"Everybody is suddenly obsessed with proof."

"Because it works better than guessing," Phylmyk replied.

Nia unfolded another paper.

It was a copy of a letter.

**TO THE PEOPLE OF GEMA: DO NOT TRUST ANYONE CLAIMING TO COMMAND THE SHADOW RIDERZ.**

Beneath it were five signatures.

Mzee Kito's was first.

Dantez read it twice.

"The original riders signed this?"

"Yes."

"Then why isn't it public?"

"My father wanted to deliver it to Kariz first."

Phylmyk nodded.

"Let's take it there."

But when they returned to Kwa Kariz, something strange was waiting.

The garage had reopened after the electrical repair.

On the counter lay a black motorcycle helmet.

The missing helmet.

Kariz stood beside it, looking furious.

"Where did that come from?" Dantez demanded.

"Someone dropped it outside the gate," Kariz said.

Labonator picked up the note that lay beside it.

It read:

**IF YOU WANT THE TRUTH, GO TO BONDE.**

Dantez let out a slow breath.

"Of course."

Mdavido grinned.

"Maybe the mystery wants you to exercise."

Then Vanny Boy emerged from the office, where he had been helping Kariz organise the messages.

He examined the helmet from a respectful distance.

"Interesting."

Everybody groaned.

Vanny ignored them.

"There appears to be a discrepancy in the object's internal padding configuration relative to the earlier photographs."

Dantez frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"The inside looks different," Phylmyk translated.

"Correct."

Kariz inspected it.

A small pocket had been stitched beneath the lining.

It was empty.

Vanny stared at it.

"Someone removed something."

Dantez looked at the note.

Bonde.

The missing pocket.

The false Shadow Riderz.

The seven-minute blackouts.

He was beginning to understand one thing.

Someone wanted him to follow instructions.

And every instruction led farther from the truth.
